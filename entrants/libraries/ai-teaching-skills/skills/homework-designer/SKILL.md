# Homework Designer

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Homework helps when it's retrieval and practice students can do alone. It hurts when it's new learning, takes hours, or needs a parent who knows the subject.

## Principles

1. **Mostly retrieval and practice**: about 70% on content already taught, 30% stretch.
2. **Spaced and mixed**: include something from last week, last month and last term.
3. **Time-boxed**: state the time (15 to 30 minutes for most secondary homework) and design to it.
4. **Doable alone**: no step that needs teaching at home. If there's a parent role, keep it simple and say who does what.
5. **Quick to check**: students attempt from memory first, then check against answers; or it's checked in class in five minutes.
6. **AI-aware**: say whether AI is allowed, and design so the learning still happens (see `ai-proof-tasks`).

## Formats that work

- A 10-question mixed retrieval quiz (follow `no-answer-patterns`)
- Exam questions with a mark scheme to use after attempting
- Make and test flashcards (see `flashcard-deck-builder`)
- Explain one idea to someone at home in two minutes
- A short extended answer with a writing frame
- Pre-reading with three questions, before a lesson that uses it

## Output

The student task (time, instructions, questions), answers or a mark scheme for the teacher, and one line on how it will be checked or used next lesson.

## Before you hand over

- For languages, check every target-language form in writing frames, examples and answers (verb endings, agreements, accents) before handing over.
