# Revision Timetable Builder

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

A revision timetable fails when it's too ambitious in week one. Build one a real student will follow.

## Inputs

Exam dates per subject and paper, topics per subject, the student's weak topics (from mocks or diagnostics), time available each day (after school, weekends, holidays), fixed commitments (prayer times, family, work, sport, faith classes), and how long they can focus.

## Rules

1. **Work back from the exam dates.**
2. **Weight topics** by marks in the exam and by weakness.
3. **Short blocks**: 25 to 40 minutes, then a break. Two to four blocks on a school day is plenty.
4. **Space and interleave**: each topic comes back after 2 to 3 days, then a week, then two. Mix subjects within a day.
5. **Active methods in every block**: self-testing, past questions, flashcards, teach-back. Never "read notes" alone.
6. **Protect rest**: one lighter day a week, sleep, and fixed commitments first.
7. **Buffer**: leave 10 to 15% of slots empty for catch-up.
8. **Past papers** in the final weeks under timed conditions.

## Output

- A week-by-week calendar (table or downloadable calendar file), each block naming the subject, the topic and the method
- A one-page "how to do a revision block" guide
- A weekly check-in: what got done, what to move, confidence per topic
