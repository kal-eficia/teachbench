# Behaviour Plan

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Most behaviour is shaped by routines, relationships and consistency, long before consequences. Plan those first, and keep everything within the school's own policy.

## Ask

The age group, the setting (in person or online), the specific problem (low-level chat, lateness, refusal, off-task online, conflict), what's been tried, and the school's behaviour policy.

## Build the plan

1. **Three to five expectations**, phrased positively and taught explicitly ("Cameras optional, chat on topic", "Equipment out in the first minute").
2. **Routines** for the moments that go wrong: entry, the start task, transitions, handing out, getting attention, packing up. Script them and practise them.
3. **Positive first**: specific praise, a ratio of at least 3 positives to 1 correction, recognition that matters to this age group.
4. **Least intrusive correction first**: a look, proximity, a quiet reminder of the routine, a private word, then the school's consequences in order.
5. **Scripts** for common moments, calm and short: "I notice you're..., the expectation is..., thank you."
6. **Repair**: after any consequence, a short restorative conversation (see `restorative-conversation`).
7. **For one student who needs more**: patterns (when, where, with whom), what helps, a simple daily target, home contact plan, and when to involve the pastoral team or SENCO.

## Output

The class plan (expectations, routines with scripts, praise and correction ladder), any individual plan, and a two-week review point. Note anything that must be checked against school policy.
