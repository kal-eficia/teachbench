# Question-Level Analysis

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

A total score says how a student did. Question-level analysis says why, and what to teach on Monday.

## Inputs

A spreadsheet or table of marks per question per student, plus the paper (or at least what each question tested and its command word and mark value).

## Steps

1. **Tag every question**: topic or spec point, command word (state, explain, evaluate), marks, and type (recall, application, extended).
2. **Calculate** per question: average percentage, percentage scoring zero, percentage full marks.
3. **Aggregate** by topic, by command word and by question type.
4. **Separate the causes**:
   - Low on one topic across command words: a **knowledge gap**
   - Low on "explain" or "evaluate" across topics: an **exam technique** gap
   - Many zeros on long questions late in the paper: **timing or stamina**
   - Low on a question that's easy on paper: check the question for **reading or wording** issues
5. **Rank** by marks available to win back (marks lost × how many students), not by lowest average alone.
6. **Find student groups** with the same profile for targeted intervention.

## Output

- A summary table: topic or skill, average %, marks lost, cause
- The top five reteach priorities with the lesson each needs
- Intervention groups (by profile; names only if the teacher shared them)
- The command-word practice to add to starters (see `exam-technique-coach`)
- One chart if it helps the department see the picture
