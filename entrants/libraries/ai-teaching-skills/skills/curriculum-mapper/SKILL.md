# Curriculum Mapper

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

The most expensive mistake in exam teaching is a specification point nobody taught. The second is three weeks on something worth two marks.

## Inputs

- The specification (pasted, attached, or the board and code)
- What's been taught: schemes of work, lesson titles, class logs or the teacher's list
- What's planned for the rest of the course
- Optional: past-paper frequency or the board's published weightings

## Steps

1. **Break the specification into atomic points**, keeping the board's numbering.
2. **Tag each point**: taught (when), planned (when), assessed (how), retrieved since (how often).
3. **Weight each point** by how much it's examined, where that's known.
4. **Find the problems**:
   - *Gaps*: not taught and not planned
   - *Thin*: taught once, never retrieved or assessed
   - *Over-taught*: time well beyond its exam weight
   - *Out of order*: taught before something it depends on
5. **Check the time**: lessons left against points left, at a realistic pace.

## Output

- A coverage table: spec ref, point, taught, planned, assessed, retrieved, weight, status
- A traffic-light summary by topic
- The top five actions in order ("teach 1.3.2 before the mocks; add 4.2 to two starters")
- If the time doesn't fit, say so plainly and propose what to compress.
