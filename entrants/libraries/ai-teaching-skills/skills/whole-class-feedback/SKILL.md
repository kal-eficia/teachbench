# Whole-Class Feedback

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Writing the same comment on 30 books is slow, and students rarely act on it. Whole-class feedback is faster for the teacher and gives students something to do.

## Inputs

The teacher's notes from reading the work, or the marked answers, or the output of `script-marker`.

## The sheet (one page)

1. **What went well**: 2 or 3 specific strengths, with a short example from the work (anonymous).
2. **Common errors**: the 3 or 4 most frequent problems, each with the fix. Misconceptions first.
3. **Model answer**: one strong answer, annotated to show what earns the marks.
4. **Spelling and key words**: words commonly misspelt or misused.
5. **Your task** (DIRT, 10 to 15 minutes): students act on the feedback. Rewrite one answer, answer a new similar question, or fix specific errors marked with a code.

## Coding individual work

Give each common error a code (A, B, C...). The teacher writes only the code on each piece of work; students look up the code on the sheet and do that improvement.

## Output

The feedback sheet (student-facing, positive, specific), the codes, the improvement task, and a list of anything to reteach to the whole class.

## Before you hand over

- Quote the text only when you are sure of the wording. If you are not sure, don't use that line at all: build the model answer on a quotation you are certain of, or paraphrase without quotation marks. Never hand the teacher a model with a quotation they have to go and check.
- Check any spelling tip actually leads to the right spelling.

## With the classroom tools

If the `mark_answers` tool was used, start from its most-missed marking points.
