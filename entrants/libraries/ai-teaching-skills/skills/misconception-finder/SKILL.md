# Misconception Finder

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

A wrong answer is information. Ten wrong answers that share a cause are a lesson plan.

## Mode A: from student work

1. **Collect** the answers with the question, the correct answer, and (if available) each student's confidence.
2. **Group wrong answers by cause, not by question.** Look for:
   - *Confusions between pairs*: RAM/ROM, lossy/lossless, CSS/CSV, mass/weight
   - *Over-generalised rules*: "bigger file always means better quality"
   - *Everyday meaning beating technical meaning*: "virus", "memory", "energy"
   - *Procedure without understanding*: right steps, wrong place
   - *Reading the question wrong*: describe vs explain, units, "two" means two
3. **Weigh each group**: how many students, and how many were confident while wrong. Confident and wrong is the priority: it won't fix itself.
4. **Separate slips from misconceptions.** One student, once, with low confidence is probably a slip. Several students, repeatedly, is a misconception.

## Mode B: before teaching a topic

List the 5 to 8 misconceptions students most often bring to it, each with the tell-tale wrong answer that reveals it. These become hinge-question distractors.

## Output

For each misconception, most urgent first:

- **The wrong idea**, in the student's words
- **Evidence**: which questions, how many students (names only if the teacher shared them and wants them)
- **Why it's appealing**: what makes it feel right
- **The fix**: a counter-example or contrast, a 5-minute reteach plan, and one hinge question to check it's gone (with no answer patterns)
- **When to revisit**: the next two starters it should appear in

End with a one-line summary for the teacher: the single misconception to fix first, and why.

## Care

- Student names stay out of anything shared beyond the teacher.
- Don't label students; label ideas.
