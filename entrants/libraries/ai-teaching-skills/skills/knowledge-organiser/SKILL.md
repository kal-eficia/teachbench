# Knowledge Organiser

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

A knowledge organiser is not a summary of the topic. It's the exact knowledge students must hold in long-term memory, chosen ruthlessly and laid out so they can test themselves on it.

## Choose the content

- Start from the specification and the mark schemes: what earns marks?
- **Key vocabulary** (8 to 15 terms) with the definition an examiner would credit, in the board's wording where it matters.
- **Core facts** as short numbered statements, one fact each.
- **One or two diagrams** with labels: a process, a structure, a cycle.
- **Formulae, dates, quotations or code syntax** if the subject needs them.
- **Common mistakes**: 3 to 5 "not this, but this" pairs.
- **Links**: what this topic builds on and what comes next.

Cut anything interesting but not examined at this level.

## Layout (one A4 page; landscape works well)

Boxed, numbered sections so a teacher can say "cover section 3 and write it out". Facts numbered within sections so students can quiz each other: "what's fact 2.4?"

## Self-quiz version

Produce the same page with definitions and key words blanked out, plus 10 retrieval questions. If any are multiple choice, follow `no-answer-patterns`.

## For students (include briefly)

Look, cover, write, check. Ten minutes three times a week beats an hour the night before.

## Output

The organiser as a printable page, the blank version, and the 10 questions with answers for the teacher.

## Before you hand over

- One page means one page: aim for 300 to 400 words plus a timeline or diagram. Cut anything the teacher didn't list. Extras go in a separate 'if you want more' note to the teacher, not on the organiser.
