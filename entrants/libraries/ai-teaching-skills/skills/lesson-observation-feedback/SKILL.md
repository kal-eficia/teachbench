# Lesson Observation Feedback

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Feedback that lists ten points changes nothing. One precise, practised action step changes teaching.

## From notes to feedback

1. **Evidence, not judgement**: quote what was seen and heard ("At 10:14, 6 of 24 students answered the hinge question; the rest waited").
2. **Strengths**: two specific things that worked, and why they worked.
3. **One priority**: the change with the most impact on learning, not the easiest to spot.
4. **An action step**: concrete, small and practisable in a few minutes ("Before naming a student, pause three seconds and say 'everyone think'"), not general advice ("improve questioning").
5. **Practise it**: in the feedback conversation, rehearse the action step.
6. **Follow-up**: when it'll be seen again, and what success looks like.

## Tone

Supportive, specific, private. Ask the teacher's view first ("How do you think it went? What would you change?"). No lesson grades.

## Output

- The written feedback (strengths, priority, action step, follow-up)
- A conversation plan with opening questions and the rehearsal
- A short record for the teacher's development log
