# CPD Session Designer

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Teachers sit through a lot of training that changes nothing. A good session changes one thing they do on Monday, and checks later that it stuck.

## Shape (60 to 90 minutes; scale to fit)

1. **Why this matters (5 min)**: one problem teachers recognise from their own classrooms.
2. **The idea (10 to 15 min)**: the principle and the evidence, briefly. No more than three key points.
3. **See it (10 min)**: a live model, a short video, or a worked classroom example.
4. **Try it (20 to 30 min)**: teachers apply it to their own upcoming lesson or materials, in pairs. This is the most important part; protect the time.
5. **Share (10 min)**: two or three pairs show what they made.
6. **Commit (5 min)**: each teacher writes the one thing they'll do in the next two weeks.

## Follow-up (where most training fails)

- A check-in at two weeks: what happened, what got in the way
- A short resource or template they can reuse
- Optional peer observation focused only on this one thing

## For AI training

Teachers must use the tool on their own real tasks during the session, with the school's data rules stated at the start. Show what it gets wrong as well as what it does well.

## Output

The session plan with timings and facilitator notes, slides or a handout outline, the practice task, the commitment card, and the follow-up plan.
