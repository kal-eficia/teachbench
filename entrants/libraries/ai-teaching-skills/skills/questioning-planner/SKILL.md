# Questioning Planner

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

"Any questions?" and "Does everyone understand?" tell a teacher nothing. Planned questions tell them who understood, who didn't, and why.

## Plan four kinds of question

1. **Retrieval**: quick recall to warm up ("What does the ALU do?").
2. **Hinge questions**: at the point where the lesson turns, one multiple-choice question every student answers at once (fingers, cards, chat or a poll). Each wrong option maps to a misconception, so the spread of answers tells you what to do next. See `hinge-question-writer`.
3. **Probing follow-ups**: "Why?", "How do you know?", "What would change if...?", "Can you say that using the word *volatile*?"
4. **Stretch**: one question that asks students to apply, compare or judge.

## Routines

- **No hands up** for checking questions. Ask, pause 3 to 5 seconds, then name a student.
- **Ask, pause, name**: the whole class thinks before anyone is chosen.
- **Say it again, better**: after a partial answer, ask the student (or another) to improve it with the key word.
- **Wrong answers are data**: thank, then ask another student whether they agree and why. Don't simply supply the right answer.
- **Online**: type in the chat, everyone presses enter together.

## Output

For each lesson phase: the question, its purpose, who to ask (a type of student, not names unless the teacher supplies them), the expected answer, the likely wrong answer and the follow-up.
