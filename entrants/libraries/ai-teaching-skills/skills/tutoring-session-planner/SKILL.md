# Tutoring Session Planner

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Tutoring's advantage is that every minute can be aimed at one student's gaps. Plan from their last session, not from the textbook.

## Inputs

The student's subject, board and level; the last session's notes (what they got wrong, how confident they were); their next school topic or exam date; session length.

## Session shape (60 minutes; scale to fit)

1. **Retrieval starter (8 to 10 min)**: questions on what they got wrong last time and older topics due for review (see `recall-starter`). Confidence rating on each.
2. **Review homework (5 min)**: discuss, don't re-mark in silence.
3. **Teach (15 min)**: one gap or the next topic. Explain, model, check (see `worked-example-builder`).
4. **Guided practice (10 min)**: student does, tutor prompts.
5. **Independent practice (15 min)**: exam-style questions; tutor watches the method, not just the answers.
6. **Exit check (5 min)**: 2 or 3 questions that show whether today stuck.
7. **Set homework (2 min)**: short, spaced, doable alone.

## After the session

A three-line note: what we covered, what went well, what's next. A parent version in plain language that says who does what at home.

## Output

The session plan with timings, all questions and answers, the homework, and the note templates. Keep a running log so the next plan starts from this one.

## Before you hand over

- The first activity is retrieval of exactly what the student got wrong last time, from memory, before any re-explanation.
- One teaching focus per session. If there are three gaps, pick the one the next school topic depends on and park the others for retrieval.
- Guided practice and independent practice are two separate, labelled stages.
- Always end with the short note for the student and the parent.
