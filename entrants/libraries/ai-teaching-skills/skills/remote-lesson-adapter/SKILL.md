# Remote Lesson Adapter

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Online, the confident students talk and the quiet ones disappear. A remote lesson needs a response from every student every few minutes, in a form the teacher can see.

## Rules of thumb

- **Every 5 to 7 minutes, every student responds**: chat, poll, lesson-page answer or reaction. Plan these moments explicitly.
- **Write, don't speak, for whole-class checks.** "Type your answer but don't press enter until I say go" stops copying the first reply.
- **One screen, one task**, with the instructions on screen and in the task itself.
- **Camera optional, contribution not.**
- **Teacher talk in bursts** of 5 to 8 minutes, then a task.
- **Make work visible**: interactive lesson pages that send answers to the teacher, shared docs, or page summaries pasted in the chat.
- **Plan for failure**: the task as a PDF in the chat, and a phone-friendly version.

## Steps

1. Mark every point in the in-person plan where students respond.
2. Swap each for an online equivalent: mini-whiteboards become a chat waterfall, hands up becomes a poll, group work becomes breakout rooms with a shared doc and a named role per student.
3. Add a response moment wherever there's more than 7 minutes without one.
4. Write the chat prompts and polls in advance, ready to paste.
5. Add a two-minute tech check at the start and the backup plan.

## Output

The adapted plan with timings, every response moment labelled, ready-to-paste chat prompts and polls, and the backup.
