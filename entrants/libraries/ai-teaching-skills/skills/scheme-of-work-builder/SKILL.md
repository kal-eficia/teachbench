# Scheme of Work Builder

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

A scheme of work is a promise about what students will be able to do by a date. Good ones are sequenced so each lesson builds on the last, revisit earlier content before it fades, and leave room for the lessons that go wrong.

## Inputs

- Subject, specification (board and code) or national curriculum stage
- Year group, number of classes, lesson length and lessons per week
- Start and end dates, holidays, and any fixed assessment weeks
- What students already know (last term's scheme or a baseline)
- Teaching context: remote or in person, devices available, class sizes

Ask only for what you can't infer. State assumptions at the top of the document.

## Principles

1. **Sequence by dependency.** Teach what later topics need first (binary before data representation, cells before organ systems). Draw the dependency chain before placing lessons.
2. **Spaced retrieval is built in.** Every lesson opens with a starter that revisits content from 1, 3 and 6+ lessons ago. Name the topics each starter draws from.
3. **Balance the week.** Mix exam-question lessons with creative or practical lessons (posters, debates, short presentations, builds). Not every lesson is creative; not every lesson is past-paper practice.
4. **Assessment points are planned, not added.** A short check every 3 to 4 lessons, an end-of-unit assessment, and a six-week review against the baseline.
5. **Slack is honest.** Leave one flex lesson in every six for catch-up, absences and the lesson that needs reteaching.
6. **Tell families about practical needs in advance.** If a lesson needs materials or software at home, flag it a week ahead.

## Output: one table per half-term

| Wk | Lesson | Objective (spec ref) | Starter draws from | Main task type | Check | Resources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Then, after the tables:
- **Assessment calendar** with dates
- **Key vocabulary** per unit
- **Common misconceptions** per unit (feed them to the `misconception-finder` skill)
- **Home heads-up list**: what families need to know about, and when

## Check

- [ ] Every spec point in range is taught at least once and retrieved at least twice
- [ ] No lesson depends on something taught later
- [ ] Creative and exam-focused lessons are both present every fortnight
- [ ] Flex lessons exist
- [ ] Dates fit the real calendar, including holidays

## Before you hand over

- Never put a checkpoint or assessment in a lesson that also introduces new content. Give it its own slot or a consolidation lesson.
- Check the pace lesson by lesson for the age group: one new idea per lesson for beginners.
