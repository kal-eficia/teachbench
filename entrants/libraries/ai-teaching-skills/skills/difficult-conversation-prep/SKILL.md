# Difficult Conversation Prep

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Hard conversations go better when the facts are straight, the goal is clear and the first minute is planned.

## Prepare

1. **Facts**: what happened, with dates and evidence. Separate facts from interpretations.
2. **Their view**: what the other person is likely to feel and want. What are they worried about underneath?
3. **Your goal**: the one outcome that would make this a success (agreement on a plan, a relationship repaired, information shared).
4. **What's not negotiable**, and what is.
5. **Who else should know or attend** (line manager, pastoral lead), and whether school policy says how this conversation should happen.

## The conversation

- **Opening (30 seconds)**: purpose, warmth, the shared aim. "Thank you for coming in. We both want X to do well, so I wanted to talk about..."
- **Listen first**: let them say their piece. Summarise it back before responding.
- **Facts, not labels**: "Homework has been missing four times this half-term", not "He's lazy."
- **Acknowledge** feelings without accepting inaccurate claims.
- **Agree next steps**: who does what, by when, and when you'll review.
- **Close** on the shared goal.

## Likely moments, with responses

Prepare 4 to 6: anger, blame, tears, "the other teacher said...", a request you can't grant. A calm sentence for each.

## Output

A one-page brief (facts, goal, opening, likely responses, next steps) and a short follow-up email confirming what was agreed. If the matter involves safeguarding or a formal complaint, point to the school's procedure and the person who leads on it.
