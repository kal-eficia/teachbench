# Cover Lesson Planner

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Most cover lessons are a wasted hour. A good one keeps learning moving and asks nothing of the cover teacher except reading the sheet.

## Design rules

- **Continue the sequence.** Use retrieval and practice on what was just taught, not new content that needs explaining.
- **Self-contained.** Everything is on the page or the screen. No logins students may not have, no sites that might be blocked.
- **Non-specialist proof.** Answers are provided so the cover teacher can help without knowing the subject.
- **A clear end product** that shows the work was done.

## Structure (adapt to the lesson length)

1. **Starter (10 min)**: retrieval quiz on the last three lessons
2. **Main task (25 to 35 min)**: practice questions, a structured reading with questions, or an extended answer with a writing frame
3. **Challenge** for early finishers
4. **Exit question (5 min)**

## The cover teacher sheet (one page)

- Class, room or meeting link, time, number of students, and anything the teacher chooses to share about specific needs
- What to say at the start, word for word, in three lines
- Timings, where the work is, what to collect or how students submit
- Answers
- What to do if the technology fails

## Output

The cover sheet, the student task (printable or as a lesson page) and the answers. For a remote class, add joining instructions and the message to post in the chat.
