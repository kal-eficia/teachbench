# Stretch and Challenge

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

"Do the next worksheet" isn't challenge. Real stretch asks for deeper or wider thinking about the same topic.

## Ways to stretch (pick by topic)

- **Transfer**: apply the idea in an unfamiliar context ("How would a hospital network differ from a school's?").
- **Reverse**: give the answer, ask for the question or the input that produced it.
- **Explain why, not just how**: why does the method work? When does it fail?
- **Evaluate and judge**: compare two approaches and decide, with criteria.
- **Find the flaw**: a plausible but wrong worked solution or argument to correct.
- **Connect**: link this topic to two others in the course.
- **Next level up**: an A level or first-year university question on the same idea, with a scaffold.
- **Create**: design a question, a test or a teaching explanation for younger students.
- **Open problems** with more than one good answer.

## Rules

- Tie every challenge to the current objective; avoid random puzzles.
- Make it available to everyone, not only those labelled "most able".
- Plan how it's checked; unmarked extension work soon stops being done.

## Output

Three tiers of challenge for the lesson or unit (deeper, wider, beyond the course), each with the task, what a strong response includes, and how it will be checked.
