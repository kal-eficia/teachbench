# EAL Support

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

EAL students are often capable of the full subject content. The barrier is the language, so support the language and keep the thinking demanding.

## Steps

1. **Find the language load**: the key subject words, the tricky everyday words, and the sentence structures the task needs ("This is because...", "Compared with...").
2. **Pre-teach 6 to 8 key words** with a picture or diagram, a simple definition, and a translation in the student's first language if the teacher wants it (flag that translations should be checked by a fluent speaker).
3. **Simplify instructions, not content**: short sentences, one instruction per line, the same verbs every time, numbered steps.
4. **Sentence frames** for speaking and writing, matched to the task's thinking: describing, explaining, comparing, evaluating.
5. **Visual support**: labelled diagrams, flow charts and worked examples (see `dual-coding-designer`).
6. **Talk before writing**: pair talk using the frames before any written answer.
7. **First language as a resource**: allow planning or notes in the first language; bilingual glossaries.
8. **Check comprehension of the question**, not just the content, before assessing.

## Output

A vocabulary sheet (word, picture idea, simple definition, translation if requested), the adapted instructions, sentence frames per task, and a note on which support to fade first.
