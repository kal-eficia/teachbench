# Coursework Guide

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Coursework rewards process and evidence as much as the final product, and boards limit how much help a teacher can give. Guidance has to work inside those rules.

## First, the rules

- Find the board's rules for this component: what feedback is allowed, whether drafts can be reviewed, and the rules on AI tools and authentication. Quote them; don't guess. If you can't confirm a rule, say so and ask the teacher to check the board's document.
- Make clear to students what counts as their own work and how to acknowledge sources and AI use.

## Break it down

1. **Stages and deadlines**: a timeline from proposal to submission with internal deadlines and buffer.
2. **Criteria translated**: for each assessment criterion, what it looks like at the top band, in student language, with examples of evidence.
3. **Checklists per stage**: what must exist before moving on (research log, design, testing evidence, evaluation).
4. **Evidence habits**: dated logs, screenshots, versions saved, reasons written down at the time.

## Feedback that stays within the rules

Where the board allows general feedback: point to the criterion and the gap ("Your evaluation doesn't yet refer to your success criteria") rather than rewriting the work.

## Output

- A student guide: stages, deadlines, criteria in plain language, checklists
- A teacher tracker: student by stage, with status
- Feedback prompts aligned to the criteria
- A short note on the board's rules for help and AI use, with the source
