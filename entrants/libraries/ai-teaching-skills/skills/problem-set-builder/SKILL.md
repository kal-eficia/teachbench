# Problem Set Builder

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Random practice questions build speed. Carefully varied questions build understanding, because students notice what changes and what stays the same.

## Structure

1. **Fluency with variation (8 to 12 questions)**: change one thing at a time so students see the effect. For example, 3/4 of 20, 3/4 of 40, 3/4 of 80, then 3/8 of 80. Ask students to predict before calculating.
2. **Spot the structure**: "What do you notice?" prompts between groups of questions.
3. **Reasoning (3 to 5)**: "Always, sometimes or never true?", "Explain the mistake", "Which is the odd one out and why?"
4. **Problem solving (2 or 3)**: unfamiliar context, more than one step, and at least one where the method isn't obvious.
5. **Challenge (1)**: an open or harder problem for those who finish.

## Rules

- Each question has a purpose: name what it varies or what misconception it targets.
- Numbers chosen to make the structure visible, not to make arithmetic hard.
- Interleave earlier topics in the problem-solving section.
- Units and diagrams where the topic needs them.

## Output

The student set, then a teacher key with answers, the variation or misconception for each question, and the "what do you notice?" points to draw out in discussion.
