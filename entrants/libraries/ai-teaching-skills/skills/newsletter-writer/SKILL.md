# Newsletter Writer

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Parents read newsletters on their phones, often half-way through something else. Short sections, plain words, dates they can't miss.

## Structure

1. **Headline**: one line on the period ("This half-term: networks, spreadsheets and a big project").
2. **What we learned**: 3 to 5 bullets in parent language. No spec codes, no internal programme names.
3. **Coming up**: dates, deadlines, assessments, events, with the day and date.
4. **How to help at home**: one or two actions, saying clearly who does what. Never assume parents can teach the content. ("Ask your child to explain one thing they learned this week" is a parent action.)
5. **Celebrations**: achievements by the class or year, not ranking individuals (names only with the school's consent rules).
6. **One call to action**, if there is one: a form to return, a meeting to book, with a link and a date.
7. **Contact**: how to get in touch.

## Rules

- Under 300 words where possible.
- Warm, confident and plain. No jargon or acronyms without explanation.
- Accessible: short paragraphs, headings, good contrast if designed.
- Check the house rules in the teaching context (for example, the greetings and wording the school uses).

## Output

The newsletter text ready to paste into email or the school's platform, a subject line, and a 2-line text-message version.
