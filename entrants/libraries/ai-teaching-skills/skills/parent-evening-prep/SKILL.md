# Parent Evening Prep

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Five minutes per family goes fast. Parents remember one thing. Make sure it's the right one.

## Inputs

For each student: name, attainment and progress data (grades, scores, recall trends), attendance, homework record, and the teacher's notes. Plus slot length and schedule if known.

Never invent evidence. Where data is missing, say so in the brief.

## The brief, per student (fits on a phone screen)

- **Open with**: one specific strength, with evidence ("scored 9/10 on last week's networks check, up from 4").
- **The one priority**: the single thing that would move them most, as a skill, not a trait.
- **What it looks like at home**: one action, with who does it. "She explains one thing from today's lesson to you at dinner" is a parent action. "He does 10 minutes on the revision app" is a student action. Don't assume parents can teach the content.
- **Numbers to have ready**: current level, target, attendance, homework completion.
- **Likely questions** and a straight answer to each ("Is she on track for a 7?").
- **Tone note** if relevant: a family that's worried, a student who's had a hard term.

## Running the meeting

A shape that fits five minutes: welcome and strength (1 min) · the priority with evidence (2 min) · the home action and who does it (1 min) · questions (1 min). End on something true and hopeful.

## Output

1. A schedule table: time, student, one-line focus.
2. One brief per student, in schedule order.
3. A follow-up list: anything promised to a family, with a date.

## Care

- Talk about work and habits, never personality.
- Plain English: no "AO2", "working towards", internal programme names.
- Briefs about children are private: keep them in the teacher's own files.
