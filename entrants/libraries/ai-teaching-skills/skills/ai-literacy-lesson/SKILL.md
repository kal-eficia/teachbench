# AI Literacy Lesson

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

Students are already using AI. The question is whether they use it to think more or to think less. Teach them to make it a study partner, not a ghostwriter, and to love building with it rather than fear it.

## Lesson ideas (choose and adapt to age)

1. **How it works, simply**: it predicts likely next words from patterns in huge amounts of text. That's why it sounds confident even when it's wrong.
2. **Catch it out**: students ask an AI about something they know well (their football team, their town, a topic from last term) and find the errors. Discuss why they happen.
3. **Prompting that helps learning**: compare "write my essay" with "quiz me on the causes of X, one question at a time, and don't give me the answer until I've tried".
4. **Check before you trust**: verify a claim against a source; spot invented references.
5. **Bias and fairness**: how training data shapes answers; who might be left out.
6. **The rules**: the school's levels of AI use (no AI / AI to check / AI as a tool) and what counts as malpractice in assessed work.
7. **Build something**: a short guided task making something useful with AI (a revision quiz, a simple app idea, a study plan), so students see it as a tool to create with.

## Each lesson

Starter, a short explanation (see `explanation-crafter`), an activity where students use or test AI, a discussion, and an exit question. Note any account or age restrictions on the tools; offer a teacher-led demo where students can't have accounts.

## Output

A lesson pack (plan, student page, activity, exit question) or a short series, matched to the age and the school's AI policy.
