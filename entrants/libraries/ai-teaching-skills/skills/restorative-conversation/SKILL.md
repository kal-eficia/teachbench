# Restorative Conversation

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

A consequence stops a behaviour for now. A restorative conversation repairs the relationship so it's less likely to happen again.

## Before the conversation

- Wait until both teacher and student are calm. Same day if possible, not in front of others.
- Be clear on the facts and on what isn't up for negotiation (the school's consequence stands).
- Plan for 5 to 10 minutes.

## The questions (in order, adapt the wording to the age)

1. "What happened?" (Listen. Don't argue the details.)
2. "What were you thinking at the time?"
3. "How were you feeling?"
4. "Who has been affected, and how?"
5. "What do you need to do to put it right?"
6. "What could you do differently next time it starts to happen?"
7. "What can I do to help?"

## The teacher's part

- Name the behaviour, not the person: "Shouting across the room stopped the lesson."
- Own your part if there was one: "I could have spoken to you privately."
- End with a fresh start: "Tomorrow's a new lesson. I'm looking forward to seeing you there."

## Safeguarding

If anything the student says suggests they may be at risk of harm, stop the restorative script and follow the school's safeguarding procedure straight away.

## Output

A short plan: the facts, the questions adapted to this student and age, the teacher's opening and closing lines, the agreed repair, and a follow-up note.
