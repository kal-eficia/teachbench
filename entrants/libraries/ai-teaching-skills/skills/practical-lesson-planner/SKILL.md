# Practical Lesson Planner

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.

A practical is only worth the setup time if students know what they're finding out and why, and can answer exam questions on it afterwards.

## Plan

1. **Aim and question**: what students will find out, in one line, and the hypothesis or prediction where relevant.
2. **Safety**: the hazards, the precautions and what the teacher checks before starting. Follow the school's risk assessment and, for science, the relevant guidance such as CLEAPSS. Flag anything that needs a technician or a specific risk assessment.
3. **Equipment list** per group, with quantities, for the technician.
4. **Method**: numbered steps, one action each, with diagrams where helpful.
5. **Roles** in groups (equipment, measurer, recorder, timekeeper), rotating.
6. **Variables**: independent, dependent, controlled, where relevant.
7. **Recording**: a results table with units, ready to fill.
8. **Analysis**: graph or calculation, conclusion, evaluation (errors, reliability, improvements).
9. **Exam link**: 3 to 5 exam-style questions on this practical, with a mark scheme.
10. **Alternative**: a video, simulation or data set for students who can't do it hands-on (absent, remote, or no equipment).

## Output

The teacher plan with timings and safety notes, the technician list, the student sheet (method, table, questions), the exam questions with answers, and the alternative.
