# Essay Feedback

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Good writing feedback is specific, tied to the mark scheme, and makes the student do the rewriting.

## Steps

1. **Read against the criteria**: the board's levels or the teacher's rubric. Quote the descriptor that fits best.
2. **Place it in a level** with a reason, as a teacher would ("Level 3: a clear explanation with relevant examples, but the evaluation isn't sustained"). Say if it's on a boundary.
3. **Strengths with evidence**: two, each with a short quotation from the work.
4. **One priority**: the change that would move the work up the most (structure, evidence, analysis, judgement, accuracy).
5. **Show, don't rewrite**: point to the exact paragraph and give a question or a model sentence opening, not a finished paragraph.
6. **Technical accuracy**: list recurring spelling, punctuation or grammar errors once, with the rule, if the mark scheme rewards them.
7. **Rewrite task**: one paragraph to redraft now, using the feedback.

## For drafts of assessed work

Check the coursework rules on feedback (see `coursework-guide`): general feedback only where boards limit help.

## Output

Feedback in this order: level and reason, strengths, the priority with a pointer, accuracy notes, the rewrite task. Under 200 words to the student.
