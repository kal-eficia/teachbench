# Student Report Writer

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Parents read a report three times. Students read it once and remember one line. Write for both.

## Inputs to gather

For each student: an initial or placeholder such as [S1] (swap the real first name in afterwards, unless your school's policy allows names in this tool), pronouns, subject, and whatever evidence exists (marks, attendance, homework record, the teacher's own notes, recall or quiz trends). If the teacher only gives a list of names and grades, ask for one line of their own observation per student. Reports built on grades alone read like templates, because they are.

Never invent evidence. If you don't know something, leave it out.

## The shape: the sandwich

1. **Open with a real strength.** Specific and earned: "Amir explains the fetch-decode-execute cycle more clearly than most of the class" beats "Amir is a pleasure to teach."
2. **One next step, made doable.** Name the skill, not the character flaw, and say what to *do*: "Next term, the step up is showing working in binary conversions; five minutes of practice twice a week will do it." One step, not five.
3. **Close positive and forward-looking.** Something true the student can build on.

## Making 30 reports sound like 30 people

- Vary the opening move: a skill, a moment from class, a habit, a piece of work, a growth over the term.
- Vary sentence length and order. Never reuse a sentence across two students in the same batch.
- Match the evidence: a student with a quiet term gets a different kind of strength from a student who topped the class, not the same sentence with a different name.
- After drafting the batch, scan for repeated phrases and rewrite duplicates.

## Tone rules

- Empathetic and direct. No sugar-coating, no blame.
- Plain English parents can read without a glossary. No "AO2", "working towards", "WTS".
- Talk about work and habits, never personality ("lazy", "chatty").
- Respect the word limit the school sets. Default: 60 to 90 words.

## Output

A table or list: student (placeholder or name), report text, word count. Flag any student where you had too little evidence to write something specific, so the teacher can add a line.

## With the classroom tools

If the `check_reports` tool is available, run the whole batch through it (with the school's word limits) and rewrite anything it flags before handing over.
