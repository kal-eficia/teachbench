# Parent Message

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

Parents read school messages on a phone, between other things. A good one can be understood in ten seconds and acted on in one tap.

## Rules

1. **One message, one job.** One update or one ask. If there are two things, send two messages or lead with the one that needs action.
2. **Plain language.** No internal names for programmes, methods or meetings. Say "a free 15-minute call", not "a Recall Check". Say "practice questions", not "retrieval tasks".
3. **The call to action is obvious.** One action, one link or one reply, and a date if it matters: "Reply YES by Thursday to book a slot."
4. **Say who does what.** Don't assume the parent sits in on lessons or revision. "Ask her to explain one thing she learned today" is a parent task. "She'll do 10 minutes of the app each evening" is a student task. Make it clear which is which.
5. **Warm, not gushing.** Use the child's name (draft with a placeholder like [child] if names shouldn't go into this tool, then swap it in before sending). One sentence of genuine context, then the point.
6. **Short.** WhatsApp or SMS: under 80 words. Email: under 150, with a subject line that says what it is.
7. **Concerns are facts plus a plan.** What happened, what the teacher is doing, what would help at home. Never a list of faults.

## Shape

```
Hi [parent name or "Assalamu alaykum" / greeting that fits the community],

[One line of context about the child.]
[The update or the ask, in one or two sentences.]
[The single call to action, with date or link.]

[Sign-off with name]
```

## Before sending

- [ ] Could a parent who has never heard of the programme understand it?
- [ ] Is there exactly one action, and is it clear who does it?
- [ ] Is anything here private to another child? Remove it.

## Before you hand over

- Even in a short message, the first line is warm or names the shared aim, before the facts.
- Count the asks. If the parent is asked to do two things, cut one.

## With the classroom tools

If the `text_stats` tool is available, use it to confirm the word limit and that the reading level suits a busy parent reading on a phone.
