# Diagnostic Test Builder

> If `teaching-context.md` exists, read it first, follow its house rules, and don't ask for anything it already answers.
>
> **Student data:** use initials or numbers instead of names, and leave out health, SEND diagnoses, safeguarding and family details unless your school's data protection and AI policy allow this tool to handle them. If you can't tell, anonymise first.

A diagnostic test isn't a mini exam. Its job is to find out precisely what to teach, so each question must point at one skill or one misconception.

## Design

1. **List the skills and prerequisites** the coming unit or course depends on (from the specification and earlier stages).
2. **One question per skill** at first, two for the most important.
3. **Mix formats**: short recall, multiple choice with misconception distractors, one short explanation, one applied problem.
4. **Order from easy to hard** so weaker students aren't defeated by question 2.
5. **Keep it short**: 20 to 30 minutes, no surprises, clearly "this helps me teach you, it doesn't count".
6. **Add confidence ratings** so confident errors stand out.

## Output

- The test, student-facing and calm in tone
- A **question-to-skill map**: question, skill or spec point, the misconception each wrong answer shows
- A **results grid** template: students by skills, to fill in green / amber / red
- **How to act on it**: skills most of the class lack become whole-class teaching; skills a few lack become targeted support; confident errors get fixed first
- For a 1:1 student: a simple record to repeat the same test after six weeks (see `six-week-review`)
