# Department meeting: trying AI for differentiated worksheets (45 minutes)

**Assumptions:** the school has a data policy and a list of approved AI tools (check both before the meeting). The three sceptics will try one thing, not a whole scheme. There is a shared folder for the outputs.

## Goal

By the end, every maths teacher has made one differentiated worksheet with AI, has solved every question on it, and has said what they will do next. This is a sensible trial, not a department-wide switch.

## Before the meeting (about 10 minutes of your time)

- Pick one real topic coming up in the next two weeks, such as solving linear equations for Year 9, and one existing worksheet for it.
- Make one differentiated version yourself with AI. Note the errors and the weak questions you find. You will show these.
- Ask each teacher to bring one worksheet they plan to teach soon.

## Running the meeting

| Time | Section | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | Open | Write the three worries on the board: students get lazy, the AI makes errors, checking takes time. Say each one gets answered. |
| 5 to 12 | The idea | Three points only. The AI drafts and the teacher decides. Hard questions stay hard. The teacher solves every question before a student sees it. |
| 12 to 18 | See it | Take your worksheet and ask for three versions: scaffolded, core and stretch. Show one error you caught and one question that stayed too easy. |
| 18 to 32 | Try it in pairs | Each pair takes one of their own worksheets and makes a differentiated version. Pair a sceptic with a colleague who has used AI before. Each pair solves every question and logs any errors. |
| 32 to 39 | Share | Two pairs show their version and their error log. Ask the sceptics what they would keep. |
| 39 to 45 | Commit | Each teacher writes one action for the next two weeks, for example: "I will make the easier sheet for my Year 9 set with AI and solve every question before printing." |

## Answering the three worries

- **Laziness.** The AI does the drafting, not the thinking. Keep the same method in every version. What changes is the number size, the scaffolds and how much is given. Test: could a student still learn the method from the easiest version? If yes, the sheet is doing its job.
- **Errors.** Every answer is solved by the teacher before anything is printed. Make this a department rule for the trial.
- **Time.** The trial is one worksheet on one topic. Time how long it takes. If it is not quicker by the second attempt, drop it.

## Ground rules to state at the start

- No student names, marks or personal details go into any tool unless school policy allows it.
- Only school-approved tools are used.
- The teacher is responsible for everything that goes out.

## Follow-up

- At the next department meeting, spend five minutes on a check-in: what worked, what got in the way, and how many errors were found.
- Save the best prompts and the error log in the shared folder.
- Optional: one teacher observes a colleague's lesson, looking only at how students used the differentiated sheet.
