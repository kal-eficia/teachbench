# Whole-class feedback: How Priestley presents Mr Birling

## Part 1: Student feedback sheet

**What went well**

- **You know the story.** Almost everyone can follow the plot of *An Inspector Calls*, so the next step is to do more with it. That's a good starting point.
- **You made a real link to capitalism.** Several of you connected Birling to capitalism. That's the kind of thinking that moves an essay up a level. Keep it.

**The most important improvement: move from telling to analysing**

Many of you retold what happens. The examiner already knows the story. Every paragraph should make a point about Birling and how Priestley shows it.

**Common errors and how to fix them**

| Code | What we noticed | How to fix it |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Retelling the plot | Start each paragraph with a point about Birling. Use the plot only as evidence for that point. |
| **B** | Quoting without commenting on the language | After every quote, pick one word and say what it shows. Ask: why this word, and not another? |
| **C** | No context | Add one sentence on 1912 (when the play is set) and 1945 (when Priestley wrote it). Ask: how does the difference change what the audience would notice about Birling? |
| **D** | Paragraphs that are too long | If a paragraph covers two points, split it. One idea per paragraph is the aim. |
| **E** | Spelling: Priestley, capitalism, responsibility | See the spelling tips below. |

**Spelling tips**

- **Priestley:** think of the word "priest" followed by "ley".
- **Capitalism:** the root is "capital" with "-ism" added on the end.
- **Responsibility:** it ends in "-ibility". Think of the word "responsible".

**Model paragraph (annotated)**

> Priestley presents Birling as a self-centred businessman who values money above people. In Act One, Birling tells the family that a man "has to mind his own business and look after himself and his own". The verb "mind" makes his view sound like a rule everyone should follow, which shows how confident he is in his beliefs. The repeated "himself" hints that he cares only about his own family. Priestley's audience, watching in 1945 after the Second World War, would have seen this attitude as the very thing that had led to the suffering of the war, which makes Birling's confidence seem dangerous.

*Annotations:*
- Point first: Birling values money above people.
- Quote with a clear reference and short, precise word-level analysis ("mind", "himself").
- Context linked to the audience, not just listed.
- One idea, kept to a single paragraph.

**Your task (10 to 15 minutes)**

Choose one paragraph from your own essay.

1. Find the code that applies to it (A to E). Write the code in the margin.
2. Fix the problem using the advice in the table.
3. If you have a code A or D, split the paragraph into two. Start the second one with a new point about Birling.
4. Swap your rewritten paragraph with a partner and check that it does the job.

## Part 2: Teacher notes (not for students)

**Reteach to the whole class (in the next lesson, about 10 minutes):**
- Turning a quote into analysis: pick one word, explain what it shows about Birling, then link it to Priestley's purpose.
- Using context as a link to Priestley's message, not as a list of facts.
- Splitting long paragraphs at a new idea.

**Spelling to check in the next starter:** Priestley, capitalism, responsibility.

**Strengths to name in class:** the plot knowledge is secure, and the capitalism link is the strongest move in the set. Ask one or two students who made that link to share their sentence.

**Coding individual work:** write only the code letter on each essay, not a full comment. Students use the table to find the fix.
