# One lesson on Curley's wife, for the whole Year 9 class

**Assumptions:** 60-minute lesson, students have read up to and including the chapter where Curley's wife is introduced properly, everyone has a copy of the novel, and the class works on paper with the student page on the board. Check that the quotes below match your edition before the lesson.

**The big idea:** everyone answers the same question with the same task. The difference is how much support each student gets, not what they are asked. Support comes from sentence starters and a quote bank, so no one is handed a different lesson.

**Question for everyone:** *How does Steinbeck make Curley's wife's loneliness matter to the reader?*

## Plan

| Time | Activity | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Recall starter | Answer three questions on earlier chapters, then tick Guessing, Think so or Certain |
| 5 to 20 min | Learn | Read the extract aloud, then annotate the quote bank |
| 20 to 25 min | Check | Four quick multiple-choice questions |
| 25 to 50 min | Apply | Write one paragraph using Point, Evidence, Explain |
| 50 to 55 min | Share | Two students read their paragraph aloud, teacher gives one improvement |
| 55 to 60 min | Exit | One sentence: "Curley's wife is ___ because ___." |

## Quote bank (check against your copy)

Students choose quotes from this list and do not need to find their own. Each quote has a plain-English meaning to help the two students reading at about Year 5 level.

1. "She had full, rouged lips and wide-spaced eyes." Meaning: she has made herself up heavily. Steinbeck describes her through her appearance.
2. "I coulda made somethin' of myself." Meaning: she thinks she could have had a better life. Her dreams have stopped.
3. She is never given a name. Everyone calls her "Curley's wife", so she is defined by her marriage.

## Learn (on screen)

Show the three points. Ask: "What does the reader learn about her from each one?" Pause for answers, then write the best one on the board. Do not reveal the answer to the big question yet.

## Check: four multiple-choice questions

1. Why is Curley's wife never given a name? (C)
   - A. Because she is unimportant in the story
   - B. Because the author forgot
   - C. Because the men see her only as Curley's wife
   - D. Because she asked not to be named
2. Which word best describes her at the ranch? (A)
   - A. Lonely
   - B. Cheerful
   - C. Strict
   - D. Wealthy
3. What does her made-up appearance suggest? (D)
   - A. She is a farmer
   - B. She is a child
   - C. She is happy with her life
   - D. She is trying to be noticed
4. Who is she usually shown talking to? (B)
   - A. The boss
   - B. The men on the ranch
   - C. Her husband's dog
   - D. Nobody at all

Answer key: C, A, D, B. No repeats, runs or staircases.

## Apply: one paragraph

Students write one paragraph answering the big question. Each paragraph needs a point, one piece of evidence from the quote bank, and an explanation of what the evidence shows the reader.

**Three routes to the same task:**

- **Route A (starter bank):** Use the sentence starter "Steinbeck shows she is lonely when..." and the plain-English meanings. Finish the sentence with one quote.
- **Route B (starter only):** Use the starter "Steinbeck makes the reader feel..." and choose your own quote from the bank.
- **Route C (open):** Explain the effect of Steinbeck's choice to leave her unnamed. Then say whether the reader is meant to feel sympathy, anger or both.

Everyone gets the same question. Route A students can move to Route B when they are ready.

## Exit

One sentence on a slip: "Curley's wife is ___ because ___." Use these to decide the first starter next lesson.

## Homework (15 to 20 minutes)

1. Retrieval: list two quotes from the chapter and what each shows about her.
2. Stretch: why might Steinbeck make the reader uncomfortable about her? Write three sentences.

## Teacher notes

**Common misconceptions to listen for:**
- "She is a villain." Ask what the men have denied her, and whether the reader knows her side.
- "Lonely means she has no friends." Ask what she is missing in her marriage and on the ranch.

**Support for the two students reading at about Year 5 level:** read the quote bank aloud with them before the task and pair them with a strong reader for the Apply task. Do not give them a separate task.

**Stretch for students who finish early:** Route C plus a second paragraph comparing her with Crooks or Lennie.

## Mark scheme (Apply paragraph, per student)

- **Point (1 mark):** a clear statement about her loneliness or how the men see her.
- **Evidence (1 mark):** a correct quote from the bank or from the novel.
- **Explain (2 marks):** says what the evidence shows the reader and links it to the big question. One mark for a basic link, two for a link that explores effect.

**Common answers that do not earn the explain mark:** "She is sad." This is a point, not an explanation. Ask "How do you know?" and "What does Steinbeck want us to feel?"

## Solutions sheet (for quick approval in class)

- Recall starter: check against the previous chapter's summary.
- MCQ 1: C. MCQ 2: A. MCQ 3: D. MCQ 4: B.
- Apply: approve any paragraph with a point, a correct quote and a link to the reader.
