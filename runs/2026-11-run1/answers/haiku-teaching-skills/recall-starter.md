# Year 8 science: starter for tomorrow (acids and alkalis)

**Why these questions:** the quiz showed that most of the class confuses compounds with mixtures, and about half can't say what happens to particles when a solid melts. Those two come first. Then the questions cover older work that is due for a check, and the last few lessons. Nothing on acids and alkalis is included, because it hasn't been taught yet.

## Student starter (about 7 minutes)

Answer from memory. No notes, no searching, no AI. For questions 1, 3, 4, 5 and 7, also tick how sure you are: Guessing, Think so, or Certain.

1. Which of these is a compound?
   - A. Air
   - B. Sand mixed with iron filings
   - C. Water
   - D. Salt water

2. When a solid melts, what happens to its particles? Use the words "energy" and "fixed" in your answer.

3. Which part of a cell controls what the cell does?
   - A. Nucleus
   - B. Cell wall
   - C. Cytoplasm
   - D. Vacuole

4. Which of these is a word equation for a chemical reaction?
   - A. Ice → water
   - B. Sugar + water → sugar solution
   - C. Iron filings + sand → a mixture of both
   - D. Magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide

5. Which state of matter has particles that are far apart and move quickly in all directions?
   - A. Solid
   - B. Gas
   - C. Metal
   - D. Liquid

6. Name one structure that a plant cell has and an animal cell does not. Say what it is for.

7. Which method would separate sand from water?
   - A. Filtering
   - B. Using a magnet
   - C. Heating until it melts
   - D. Adding more water

8. Is melting ice a chemical change or a physical change? Give one reason.

## Teacher key (approve answers quickly, with the reason each question is here)

| Q | Answer | Topic | Why it's in the starter |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C (water) | Compounds and mixtures | Most of the class got this wrong in last week's quiz. Air and salt water are mixtures, which is the usual confusion. |
| 2 | Particles gain energy, vibrate more, break away from fixed positions and move about, but stay close together | Particle model, melting | About half the class couldn't answer this. Three weeks ago's lesson, so it's due. Accept "gain energy and move around" as the key idea. |
| 3 | A (nucleus) | Cells | Taught about a month ago, so it's due a check. Cell wall is a distractor because it's in Q6. |
| 4 | D (magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide) | Word equations | Yesterday's lesson, so it's the most recent. A and C are not chemical reactions, and B is a physical change. |
| 5 | B (gas) | Particle model | Three weeks ago. Liquid is a tempting wrong answer because its particles are close together, but they still move. |
| 6 | Cell wall (support), chloroplast (photosynthesis) or large vacuole (stores water). Accept any one with its function. | Cells | Cells are due a check, and this links plant and animal cells. |
| 7 | A (filtering) | Separating mixtures | Links to compounds and mixtures in Q1. Use it to check that mixtures can be separated and compounds can't. |
| 8 | Physical change: the substance stays water and no new substance forms. | Chemical and physical changes | Lesson 3. Students who answered Q2 correctly will often get this one, which is a good sign. |

**Order and topic check:** no two neighbouring questions cover the same topic. The topics run compounds, melting, cells, word equations, particles, cells, mixtures, chemical change.

**Answer pattern check:** the correct MCQ answers run C, A, D, B, A in order. No letter runs in sequence, and no run repeats.
