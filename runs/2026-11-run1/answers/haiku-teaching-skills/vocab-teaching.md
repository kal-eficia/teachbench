# Teaching Key Terms for Year 10 AQA RS (Christianity and Islam)

## Assumptions

- All six words are tier 3 (subject-specific). Each will appear in exam answers, so teach them explicitly, not just in passing.
- Lessons are 50 minutes and students have exercise books.
- Definitions are for AQA-style answers. Students should use the term, then explain it.

## Vocabulary table

| Word | Say it | Student-friendly definition | Exam definition | Word parts | Example | Non-example | Common confusion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tawhid** | TAW-heed | The belief that Allah is one, with no partners or equals | Oneness of God; the central belief of Islam | From Arabic *wahid* (one) | "Muslims say there is only one God, Allah." | Believing in many gods (shirk) | Tawhid vs shirk |
| **Akhirah** | ak-hi-RAH | The life after death, when people are judged | The afterlife or hereafter; the belief in judgement and the next life | From Arabic *akhir* (last, end) | "Muslims believe their actions in this life affect their akhirah." | The present life (dunya) | Akhirah vs dunya |
| **Risalah** | ri-SAH-lah | The message or mission that God gave to prophets | The message or mission of God, revealed through prophets | From Arabic *risala* (message) | "The Qur'an is part of the risalah given to Muhammad." | A prophet's personal daily life | Risalah vs nubuwwah (prophethood, being chosen as a prophet) |
| **Incarnation** | in-car-NAY-shun | The belief that God took human form in Jesus | God becoming flesh; in Christianity, God taking human form in Jesus | Latin *in* (into) + *caro* (flesh) | "Christians believe that in Jesus, God became incarnate." | Jesus as a human prophet, not God in human form (the Islamic view) | Incarnation vs Trinity |
| **Atonement** | at-ONE-ment | Making things right between people and God, often through Jesus' death | Reconciling humanity with God, especially through Jesus' death and resurrection | "At-one-ment": bringing back to being at one with God | "Christians believe that Jesus' death brings atonement for sin." | Saying Jesus only taught good moral lessons | Atonement vs incarnation |
| **Trinity** | TRIN-i-tee | One God in three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit | The Christian belief in one God who exists as three persons | *Tri-* (three) + *unity* | "Christians believe God is the Trinity." | Believing in three separate gods | Trinity vs tawhid (Islam rejects the Trinity) |

## Teaching activity: "Say it, sort it, use it" (10 minutes)

1. **Say it (2 minutes).** Students say each word aloud after you, twice. Model the syllables with a clap for each.
2. **Sort it (4 minutes).** Put six cards on the desks: the words on one set, and the definitions and examples on another. In pairs, students match each word to its definition. Then they sort the non-examples into a "not this" pile.
3. **Use it (4 minutes).** Each student writes one exam-style sentence using one word correctly. Example prompt: "Explain what tawhid means for a Muslim's daily life, using the word tawhid." Take three answers and ask the class to say whether the word is used correctly.

## Keep it alive: six-week retrieval schedule

- **Week 1:** Starter: say the six words and their meanings, cold, with no notes.
- **Week 2:** Starter: match words to definitions on a mini whiteboard. Insist on the correct term in one written answer.
- **Week 3:** Quick quiz: fill in the missing word in five sentences.
- **Week 4:** Write one 4-mark answer that uses at least three of the terms.
- **Week 5:** Mark a student answer for correct term use only. Give students a sentence to improve.
- **Week 6:** Mixed retrieval quiz, with the words used in the questions and not highlighted.

## Teacher notes

- Ask students to use the term first, then explain it. For example, "Tawhid, which means..." rather than "Tawhid is..." on its own.
- Be precise about the difference between Islamic and Christian meanings, and avoid implying that one tradition is a version of the other.
- Support: give a word bank with pictures or simple sentence frames. Stretch: ask students to explain how Christian and Muslim beliefs about God differ, using the Trinity and tawhid in their answer.
