# Making peer feedback on the phone stand design sheets useful

**Assumptions:** Students review each other's design sheets as drafts in the next lesson. Marks stay with you. Students will get about 15 minutes for this.

---

## 1. Success criteria (student language)

Put these on the board or on the sheet. Each one should be something a reviewer can point to on the page.

1. **Two different designs.** I have sketched at least two different stand ideas, each labelled.
2. **Real measurements.** I have given the size of the stand and the material, and I can say why I chose them.
3. **Fits the user.** I can show how the stand holds the phone at a useful viewing angle and stays steady on a desk.
4. **Next step.** I have written what I would change next and why.

---

## 2. Calibrate first (10 minutes)

Show two anonymous model design sheets (or write these two extracts on the board). Students tick each criterion, then compare with your judgement.

**Model A (strong, from a previous year group, anonymised)**
> Design 2 is a folding stand cut from 3 mm acrylic. The slot is angled at about 65 degrees, so the screen sits near eye level for watching videos. Base 90 mm by 60 mm, so it does not slide. Labelled sketch shows the fold line. Next time I would add a rubber strip on the slot to stop the phone scratching.

*Your judgement:* Meets criteria 1, 2 and 3. Criterion 4 is partly met, because the change is named but the reason is thin. Ask students to add the reason ("so the phone does not scratch").

**Model B (gaps)**
> Design 1 is a stand. It is made from wood. It looks good and is easy to make. I think it is good.

*Your judgement:* Meets none of the criteria. The stand has no second design, no measurements, no reason for the choices, and no next step.

**Discussion question:** What exactly is missing from Model B that would let someone improve it? Students should land on "numbers and reasons," not "it's not very nice."

## 4. Sentence starters

Give these to students who are stuck. Students must change at least one word in each starter to fit their own work.

- "You met criterion ___ when you wrote ___ on page ___."
- "Your sketch of Design ___ shows ___, which helps me see ___."
- "To reach criterion ___, add the reason you chose ___ (for example, ___)."
- "I can picture how the stand fits the phone because you wrote ___."
- "Your measurement of ___ is clear. Next, you could explain why you chose it."

Ban "good", "nice" and "well done" on their own. If a reviewer uses one, they must add the criterion and the evidence after it.

---

## 5. The writer responds (5 minutes)

Each writer reads the feedback and picks one comment to act on. They redraft one labelled area of the sheet (usually the measurements or the next step) in the lesson. They then write one line: "I changed ___ because ___."

---

## 6. Teacher check

- Sample three or four feedback forms each lesson and check they are accurate. Did the reviewer's tick match what is on the page?
- Read a couple of forms aloud (without names) that name specific evidence. Praise the precise feedback in front of the class.
- Next lesson, ask two or three students to show their redrafted area and say what changed.

---

## Follow-up task (homework or the next lesson)

Each student writes a short note under their design sheet: "The best feedback I received was ___ because ___. I will use it by ___." Collect these and check they name a specific comment, not a general one.
