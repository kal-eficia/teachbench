# Session plan: Year 5, times tables (6, 7 and 8), 45 minutes online, 1:1

**Assumptions:** There are no notes from the last session, so this plan starts from the times tables that usually cause trouble at this age: 6, 7 and 8. Her 2, 5 and 10 facts are assumed to be secure. Timers are switched off. Each answer is worked out, not raced. If you have notes from a previous session, swap the starter questions for the facts she got wrong.

**Aim for this session:** Give her one reliable way to work out each of the 6, 7 and 8 facts, so she can use a method instead of guessing or panicking.

---

## Plan at a glance

| Time | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 3 min | Check-in and agreement | Friendly start. Agree that there are no timers and that mistakes are how we learn. |
| 3 to 10 min | Retrieval starter | Untimed recall of familiar facts, with a confidence rating. |
| 10 to 22 min | Teach: one strategy per table | Model one method for 6, 7 and 8, using a visual. |
| 22 to 31 min | Guided practice | You and she work through items together. You say the strategy aloud. |
| 31 to 38 min | Independent practice | She solves a short set alone. You watch her method, not the speed. |
| 38 to 42 min | Exit check | Three questions to see whether today stuck. |
| 42 to 45 min | Close and homework | Short, calm, and easy to do alone. |

---

## 1. Check-in and agreement (3 min)

Say something like: "Today we're not racing. There's no timer. If you get one wrong, that's useful information, and we'll look at it together."

Ask her to show a thumbs up or thumbs down: "Can you tell me which tables feel easy and which feel tricky?"

---

## 2. Retrieval starter (7 min, untimed)

Ask these from memory. No counting on fingers at this stage, but if she does, that's fine. Ask her to rate each answer: **Guessing / Think so / Certain**.

1. 2 x 6 = ?
2. 5 x 7 = ?
3. 10 x 8 = ?
4. 4 x 6 = ?
5. 3 x 7 = ?
6. 6 x 6 = ?
7. 2 x 8 = ?
8. 9 x 6 = ?

Keep this calm. Praise the strategy she uses, not only the answer. If she gets one wrong, say "Good try, let's check that in a minute."

---

## 3. Teach: one strategy per table (12 min)

Pick one focus idea. Do not teach all three tables at once.

**Table 6 (double the 3 times table)**
Explain that 6 is 2 x 3, so 6 x 4 = 2 x (3 x 4) = 2 x 12 = 24. Draw a 6 x 4 array and show the two halves.

**Table 8 (double, double, double)**
Explain that 8 is 2 x 2 x 2. To find 8 x 3, start with 3, double it to 6, double it to 12, double it to 24. Show this as a number line with doubling jumps.

**Table 7 (the hardest: use 5 and 2, or known facts)**
Explain that 7 x 6 = 5 x 6 + 2 x 6 = 30 + 12 = 42. Show the split on a bar model. Let her see that she doesn't need to memorise all 7 facts at once.

Model out loud, with a think-aloud: "I'm going to split 7 into 5 and 2, then add the two answers." Keep each explanation under a minute.

---

## 4. Guided practice (9 min)

Work through these together. You say the method, she does the answer. Then she says the method, you check.

1. 6 x 3 (double 3 x 3)
2. 6 x 7 (use 6 x 5 + 6 x 2)
3. 8 x 4 (double, double 4 x 2)
4. 7 x 4 (use 5 x 4 + 2 x 4)
5. 8 x 6 (use 8 x 5 + 8 x 1)

After each answer, ask: "Which strategy did we use?"

---

## 5. Independent practice (7 min)

She solves these alone. Ask her to say her method out loud before she answers. Do not time it. Watch the method, not the speed.

1. 6 x 9
2. 7 x 3
3. 8 x 7
4. 7 x 9
5. 6 x 8

If she is stuck for more than about 20 seconds, say: "Which strategy could help here?" If she is still stuck, show her the method and move on. Come back to it at the start of the next session.

---

## 6. Exit check (4 min)

Ask three questions. Use one from each table.

1. 6 x 5
2. 7 x 8
3. 8 x 3

Ask her to rate her confidence on each. The most useful signal is "Certain but wrong," and the second most useful is "Guessing and right." Name both, calmly.

---

## 7. Close and homework (3 min)

Say: "Five minutes a day is enough. No need to race."

Set one short task: choose one of the 6, 7 or 8 facts she found tricky today. Write it on a card with the strategy on the back. She reads the card once a day, out loud, and tries to answer before she turns it over.

---

## Note for the parent (plain language)

**Today:** We practised the 6, 7 and 8 times tables using doubling and splitting, not memorising. There were no timers. We worked on one strategy at a time.

**At home:** [Child] should spend about 5 minutes a day on the cards, with no timer. She does the work. You can ask her to show you one strategy she used today, for example "How did you work out 7 x 6?" You don't need to teach her the method. Just listen and ask.

---

## Running log (for the next session)

- Ratings for each starter question (Guessing / Think so / Certain).
- Which strategy she used, and where she needed a prompt.
- Which card she chose for homework.
