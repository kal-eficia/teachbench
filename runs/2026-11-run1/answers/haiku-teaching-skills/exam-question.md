# Enzymes: 8-mark explain question (AQA GCSE Combined Science, Foundation)

I've assumed Trilogy Biology (Foundation tier) and that the class has covered enzymes, active sites and the effect of temperature. Parts (b) and (c) ask students to explain, using a "because" chain.

## Student question

A student investigates how the enzyme amylase breaks down starch. She mixes amylase with starch solution at four temperatures. She tests a sample with iodine solution every 30 seconds until the starch has gone. Iodine turns blue-black when starch is present, and stays orange-brown when it is absent.

| Temperature (°C) | Time for starch to disappear (s) |
|---|---|
| 20 | 180 |
| 40 | 60 |
| 60 | 20 |
| 80 | No change after 600 s |

**(a)** Describe how the results show the effect of temperature on the rate of reaction between 20 °C and 60 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the reaction is much slower at 20 °C than at 40 °C. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the iodine solution stayed blue-black at 80 °C. **[3]**

**Total: 8 marks**

## Mark scheme

### (a) Describe (2 marks)

- Rate increases as temperature increases from 20 °C to 60 °C (1)
- Supported by data, e.g. time falls from 180 s to 20 s, so the reaction is faster (1)

**Accept:** "time taken decreases", "the starch disappears quicker", or a correct ratio or fraction of the times.
**Do not accept:** "it gets faster" with no data.

### (b) Explain (3 marks)

- At 20 °C the particles have less kinetic energy, so they move more slowly (1)
- So there are fewer collisions between enzyme and starch molecules each second (1)
- Fewer successful collisions means fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form (1)

**Accept:** "less energy", "move slower", "fewer collisions", "fewer successful collisions".
**Do not accept:** "the enzyme is killed" or "denatured" at 20 °C. The enzyme is not damaged at low temperature.

### (c) Explain (3 marks)

- At 80 °C the enzyme's active site changes shape (1)
- The starch no longer fits the active site, so the substrate is not complementary (1)
- So no enzyme-substrate complexes form and no starch is broken down (1)

**Accept:** "denatured", "active site changes shape", "active site no longer fits the starch", "no complementary fit".
**Do not accept:** "the enzyme dies" or "the enzyme is killed". Enzymes are not alive, so this is not a biological explanation.

## Model answers

**(a)** As the temperature rises from 20 °C to 60 °C, the rate of reaction increases. At 20 °C the starch took 180 seconds to disappear, while at 60 °C it took only 20 seconds.

**(b)** At 20 °C the particles have less kinetic energy, so they move more slowly. This means there are fewer collisions between the enzyme and the starch each second. Because of this, fewer successful collisions happen, so fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form and the reaction is slower.

**(c)** At 80 °C the enzyme is denatured because the temperature is too high. The shape of the active site changes, so the starch no longer fits it. Therefore no enzyme-substrate complexes can form, and the starch is not broken down.

## Teacher solutions sheet (for quick approval in class)

- (a) Look for a rate or time comparison that uses the data (2 marks: trend and figures).
- (b) Look for kinetic energy or movement, then fewer collisions, then fewer successful collisions (3 marks).
- (c) Look for shape change or no fit, then no complex forms (3 marks). Do not award a mark for "killed".

## Common errors

- Students write "the enzyme dies" in (c). Reteach: enzymes are denatured, not killed.
- Students give the answer to (b) as "the enzyme is denatured at low temperature". Reteach: denaturing only happens at high temperatures.
- Students describe the trend in (a) but do not quote the figures. Reteach: use two values from the table.
