# Feedback on your opening

Your opening makes your aim clear. You want to study medicine, and you say why in one sentence. That is the hardest thing to get right at the start, so you have a good base.

**Where it sits:** Admissions tutors look for a real reason to study medicine, evidence that you understand the work, and some reflection on what you learned. Your opening has the first, a little of the second, and not much of the third. That puts it at a developing level, with clear room to move up.

**What's working**
- "Medicine is the perfect career for me because I love science" tells the reader your choice of course straight away.
- "I want to make a difference" gives your reason a purpose, which is what tutors look for.

**The main thing to change**
At the moment, almost any applicant could have written this opening. "Ever since I was a little girl" and "I have always been passionate" tell the reader you are keen, but they don't show it. Swap the general claim for one real moment that made you want to do medicine. Ask yourself: what happened, how old were you, what did you notice about the people involved, and what did it make you want to do next?

**Language to check**
- "Hard-working and dedicated" are claims anyone can make. Let your evidence show them.
- "The perfect career" overstates it. Something like "a career I am sure about" is more honest and more convincing.
- "Always" and "love" lose their force when they repeat, so cut them where you can.

**Your rewrite task**
Write one new opening paragraph of about 80 to 100 words, built around one specific moment. Name the moment in your first sentence, then say what it taught you about medicine. Don't use the words "passionate", "dedicated" or "hard-working" anywhere in it.
