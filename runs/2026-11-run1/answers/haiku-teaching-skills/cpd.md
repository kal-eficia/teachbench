# Staff meeting session: using AI to plan lessons (60 minutes, 15 teachers)

**Assumptions:** the school has an approved AI tool that staff can log into, or you will use a free tool with no student data. Staff are on laptops or tablets. Teachers pair up for the practice task. Nothing needs printing except one handout and one commitment card.

## Goal

By the end of the session, every teacher has used AI on a real lesson they teach next week, seen where it goes wrong, and committed to one thing they will do with it.

## Before the session (15 minutes of your time)

- Pick one real lesson from your own department's scheme of work, the one most of the room teaches next week. Prepare a 4-part prompt for it (subject, year group, what students already know, what they find hard), and run it once so you know what it produces.
- Write the data rule on the first slide: no student names, no pupil data, no safeguarding or SEND details in any AI tool. Use "Student A" or "Year 9 class" instead.
- Print the handout (the 4-part prompt, plus three sample prompts) and the commitment card, one each per teacher.
- Pair teachers in advance. Put sceptics with a confident colleague who will be kind and direct, not with someone who will do the work for them.

## Run of show

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:05 | Why this matters | Ask: "How long did your last lesson plan take on a Sunday night?" Take three answers. Name the problem: planning takes too long and the time is not always well spent. |
| 0:05 to 0:15 | The idea | Three points only. (1) AI drafts quickly; you decide what is right for your class. (2) Good output needs good context, so give it your students and your prior lesson. (3) Always check facts and the level. Say the data rule again. |
| 0:15 to 0:25 | See it | Live model. Type the 4-part prompt for the prepared lesson. Show the output. Then show one thing it got wrong (a fact that was off, a task pitched too young, an example that will not land with your students). Fix it on screen with a follow-up prompt. Say out loud what you checked. |
| 0:25 to 0:50 | Try it (protect this time) | Pairs use the tool on one lesson they teach next week. Task: produce a starter, one explanation, and one exit question. Each pair must also ask the tool to find the weak point in a lesson they already know works, and write down what it got wrong. Walk the room and help the quieter teachers. |
| 0:50 to 0:57 | Share | Two or three pairs show one thing the tool did well and one thing they had to correct. Ask a sceptic to share first if they want to. |
| 0:57 to 1:00 | Commit | Each teacher writes on the card: "In the next two weeks, I will use AI to ______ for ______ lesson." Collect nothing; they keep the card. |

## Handling sceptics

- Do not argue. Ask: "What would you need to see to try it once?" Then give them that.
- Offer the "find the flaw" task as their practice. A sceptic who catches the tool getting something wrong has learned the most in the room.
- Be clear that the aim is one saved hour a week, not replacing their judgement.

## Follow-up

- **Two weeks later (10 minutes at a department meeting):** each teacher says what they tried, what got in the way, and whether they would do it again.
- **Shared prompt library:** one page with the 4-part prompt and three tested examples, saved in a shared folder. Ask two teachers to add one prompt each.
- **Optional:** a short look at one lesson each, focused only on whether the AI-planned starter or explanation worked with the class.

## Handout outline (one side of A4)

1. The 4-part prompt: subject and year; what students already know; what they find hard; what you need (starter, explanation, exit question).
2. Three example prompts for different subjects.
3. Checklist before using any output: facts correct, level right, no student data entered, examples fit your class.

Name: ____________________
In the next two weeks I will use AI to ____________________ for ____________________ lesson.
I will know it worked if ____________________.
