# First mentoring session: 20 minutes

**Assumptions:** this is the first session, so there is no earlier action to review. Her GCSE subject is not named, so the focus is on her revision method, which applies to any subject. Use her initials or a number in any notes you keep.

## Plan at a glance

| Time | Part | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 3:00 | Check-in | Build trust and let her talk first |
| 3:00 to 11:00 | Focus: how she revises | Find out what she does and what it gets her |
| 11:00 to 16:00 | Action | One small change she chooses |
| 16:00 to 18:00 | Strengths and target | Show her what she already does well and where she wants to be |
| 18:00 to 20:00 | Close | Agree the action, the next meeting and the record |

## 1. Check-in (3 minutes)

Keep this light. Let her choose what to talk about first.

- "How has your week been, in your own words?"
- "What's one subject you feel OK about at the moment?"

Do not mention her grades yet. Listen more than you speak.

## 2. Focus: how she revises (8 minutes)

Start with curiosity about her method, not a verdict on it.

- "Walk me through what you did the last time you revised for a test."
- "When you read your notes, what are you hoping they'll do for you?"
- "When you finish a page of notes, how sure are you that you could explain it without looking? Use a scale from 1 to 10."
- "What would make that one point higher?"

Reading notes can feel productive, because the material looks familiar. Familiar is not the same as remembered. You do not need to say this to her. Let her find it out by testing herself in the action step.

If she says she is not motivated, reflect it back and ask one more question:

- "What's the part that makes it hard to start?"

Then move on. Do not argue about motivation in this session.

## 3. Action (5 minutes)

Offer one small change and ask her to choose the shape of it.

- "Would you be willing to try one thing before we meet again? Here's an idea, and you can change it: after you read a page of notes, close the book and write down everything you can remember from it. Then check what you missed."
- "When and where would you do it? For how long?"

Agree one specific version. For example: "Two sessions this week, 10 minutes each, after I've read a page." Write down exactly what she said, in her words.

## 4. Strengths and target (2 minutes)

- "What's something you're good at in school, even if it's small?"
- "If you were getting the grade you're capable of, what would be different in your week?"

Her answers tell you what she values. Use them when you next meet.

## 5. Close (2 minutes)

- "What are you taking away from today?"
- "What might get in the way of the action, and what will you do if it does?"
- Agree a date for the next session.

## Record (one line, keep it short)

`Date | Focus: revision method (re-reading) | Action: [her words, when and how long] | Follow-up: check in on the action at next session, ask how it felt and what got in the way`
