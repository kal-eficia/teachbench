# Challenge for four early finishers: solving equations (Year 8)

**Assumptions:** the class has just finished one-step and two-step equations, and is about to meet equations with brackets and unknowns on both sides. Challenge tasks use only those skills, plus one step beyond. Each tier is for all four students to do in turn, so none is labelled "the most able".

---

## Tier 1: Deeper (same topic, deeper thinking)

**Task A: Find the flaw.** A student solved this. Find the mistake and fix it.

> 3(x + 4) = 21
> 3x + 4 = 21
> 3x = 17
> x = 17/3

**Task B: Find the flaw.**

> 2x + 5 = 17
> 2x = 17 + 5 = 22
> x = 11

**Task C: Choose the best first step.** For 5x − 3 = 2x + 9, which is the better first step: subtract 2x from both sides, or subtract 5x? Explain your choice and then solve.

**What a strong response includes:**
- Task A: the flaw is that the 4 was not multiplied by 3 (the bracket was not expanded). Correct answer x = 3.
- Task B: the flaw is adding 5 instead of subtracting it. Correct answer x = 6. Check: 2 × 6 + 5 = 17.
- Task C: subtracting 2x keeps the x-term positive and gives 3x − 3 = 9, so x = 4. The student should say why this is the easier choice, not just state it.

**How it is checked:** You check the answers at the desk in under two minutes. For Task C, ask the student to explain their first step aloud. Do not accept a bare answer.

---

## Tier 2: Wider (transfer to new contexts and reversed problems)

**Task A: Two numbers.** One number is 5 more than twice the other. The two numbers add up to 41. Write an equation and find both numbers.

**Task B: Perimeter.** A rectangle has a perimeter of 40 cm. Its length is 3 cm more than its width. Find both sides.

**Task C: Reverse.** The answer to an equation is x = −3. Write an equation with x on both sides that has this answer. Then write a short word problem that leads to your equation.

**What a strong response includes:**
- Task A: n + (2n + 5) = 41, so 3n = 36, n = 12, and the other number is 29. Check: 12 + 29 = 41.
- Task B: 2(w + (w + 3)) = 40, so w = 8.5 and the length is 11.5 cm. Check: 2 × (8.5 + 11.5) = 40.
- Task C: an equation such as 4x + 1 = 2x − 5 gives 2x = −6 and x = −3. A strong response checks the equation by substituting x = −3 into both sides. The word problem should match the structure of the equation.

**How it is checked:** Collect Task A and Task B as a photo of their working on the day, or check them at the desk. For Task C, check the equation by substitution. A student who has substituted correctly has proved their answer.

---

## Tier 3: Beyond the course (one step ahead, with a scaffold)

**Task A: Fractions.** Solve x/2 + x/3 = 10. Hint: multiply every term by 6 first.

**Task B: Two unknowns.** Solve both at once:

> x + y = 10
> x − y = 4

Scaffold: add the two equations together first, then substitute back.

**Task C: Explain the impossible.** Solve 2x + 3 = 2x + 5. Explain what your answer tells you about the equation, in one sentence.

**What a strong response includes:**
- Task A: 3x + 2x = 60, so x = 12. Check: 12/2 + 12/3 = 6 + 4 = 10.
- Task B: adding gives 2x = 14, so x = 7 and y = 3. Check: 7 + 3 = 10 and 7 − 3 = 4.
- Task C: subtracting 2x from both sides leaves 3 = 5, which is false, so no value of x works. A strong explanation says the equation has no solution, not that the answer is wrong.

**How it is checked:** Ask the student to explain Task C in one sentence to you, not to a worksheet. Look at the check for Task A and Task B on their working. If they cannot explain why the method works, give them one more equation of the same kind before the next lesson.

---

## Quick routine for the four

Give each student one task from Tier 1 at the start of the lesson, then move to Tier 2 and Tier 3 as they finish. Keep a note on the back of your register of who has done what. The challenge pages stay in the same place each lesson, so students do not need to ask for them.
