# Year 5 Geography: Rivers and the Water Cycle (three-week check)

**Instructions for pupils:** Answer from memory. Don't look at your books. This is not a test you are graded on. It helps your brain remember. Write your answer first, then tick how sure you are.

**Confidence:** after each question, circle one: G = Guessing, T = Think so, C = Certain

---

**1. Water vapour rises into the air and cools, forming tiny droplets that make clouds. What is this process called?**

- A. Precipitation
- B. Infiltration
- C. Evaporation
- D. Condensation

**2. True or false? Rivers get all their water from rain falling straight into them. Explain your answer.**

**3. Fill in the gap: The place where a river starts is called its ________.**

**4. Which word describes the place where two rivers join together?**

- A. Confluence
- B. Estuary
- C. Delta
- D. Meander

**5. Explain in your own words what happens to water after it falls as rain on the top of a hill.**

**6. Water soaks down through the soil and into the ground. What is this called?**

- A. Runoff
- B. Transpiration
- C. Infiltration
- D. Evaporation

**7. Why does a river usually flow faster near its source than near its mouth?**

- A. The land is flatter there
- B. The land is steeper there
- C. The water is warmer there
- D. There is more rain there

**8. Name the process where water leaves the leaves of plants as vapour.**

**9. Slow rivers on flat land often bend into curves. What are these curves called?**

- A. Waterfalls
- B. Gorges
- C. Tributaries
- D. Meanders

**10. Name the three states that water can be in.**

## Answer key (teacher)

| Q | Answer | Topic | Misconception the wrong options reveal | Where taught |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | D Condensation | Water cycle | A: pupils mix up precipitation with the process that forms clouds. C: confuses the rising-vapour step with evaporation from the sea. | Water cycle unit |
| 2 | False. Water also runs over the land (runoff) and soaks into the ground, then reaches the river. | Rivers | Thinking the river is only filled by rain that lands in it | Rivers unit |
| 3 | Source | Rivers | Confusing source with mouth | Rivers unit |
| 4 | A Confluence | Rivers | B: confuses the join with the place the river meets the sea. D: mixes up meander with the place where two rivers meet | Rivers unit |
| 5 | Model answer: The water runs downhill over the surface (runoff), or soaks into the ground and seeps down. It then reaches a river, which carries it to the sea. | Water cycle | Stopping at "it falls" without the next step | Water cycle unit |
| 6 | C Infiltration | Water cycle | A: runoff is water flowing over the surface, not soaking in. B: transpiration is from plants | Water cycle unit |
| 7 | B The land is steeper there | Rivers | A: flat land slows rivers, so the opposite is true. D: more rain does not explain speed | Rivers unit |
| 8 | Transpiration | Water cycle | Mixing it up with evaporation from the sea or soil | Water cycle unit |
| 9 | D Meanders | Rivers | A: waterfalls form on steep, hard rock. B: gorges are deep valleys cut by rivers. C: tributaries are smaller rivers that join a main river | Rivers unit |
| 10 | Solid, liquid and gas | Earlier science | Naming only two states, usually liquid and gas | Earlier science |
