# Meeting prep: parent concerned about [S1] (Year 9)

**Assumptions:** UK secondary school, you teach [S1], the head of year attends, and the aim is a shared plan rather than winning the argument. Check the school's behaviour and complaints policy before the meeting.

## One-page brief

### 1. Facts to check tonight

- **Your three detentions.** Get the dates, the exact reasons recorded, and what you said to [S1] at the time. Confirm each one followed the school's steps (warning, sanction, recording on the system).
- **The two other detentions.** Find out what they were for. If they are about the same things in different lessons, that is a pattern worth raising with the head of year. If they are about something else, say so plainly.
- **The equipment.** Repeated missing kit can have a cause you cannot see from your room: no spare pencil case at home, a timetable clash, a bag problem, or something harder. Ask the head of year whether pastoral records show anything. If anything suggests a welfare or family concern, follow the school's safeguarding procedure and not this brief.
- **Talking over you.** Note how often and in which lessons, and what you did each time.

### 2. Separate facts from feelings

- **Facts:** the sanctions, the reasons, the dates.
- **Interpretations:** "picking on her", "you don't like her", "she comes home crying". These are real to mum, so respond to them, but do not argue with the label.

### 3. Your goal

By the end, mum should leave knowing what happens next, what you will do, what you need from [S1], and that you and the head of year agree. Success is a plan, not a verdict on who was right.

### 4. Fixed and flexible

- **Fixed:** the sanctions already given under school policy, unless the head of year reviews them and decides otherwise. Do not promise to remove them.
- **Flexible:** an equipment routine (a quick kit check at the start of your lesson, a spare pen supply), where [S1] sits, how you will talk to [S1] when she talks over you, and how and when you update home.

### 5. Roles for the meeting

Agree with the head of year before the meeting. A simple split works: you cover what happened in your lessons, they cover the school process and anything about the other detentions. Agree who opens and who closes.

### 6. Opening (about 30 seconds)

> "Thank you for coming in. We both want [S1] to settle and do well, so I wanted us to go through what has been happening and agree a plan. I'll start with what I have seen in my lessons, and then I'd really like to hear your view."

### 7. Likely moments and calm responses

| What you may hear | A calm response |
|---|---|
| "You're picking on her." | "I can see how worried you are, and I want to show you exactly what happened so we're looking at the same facts." |
| Tears | Pause. "Take your time. We can stop for a moment if you need to." Offer water or tissues. |
| "She comes home crying. You clearly don't like her." | "I don't dislike [S1], and I want her to do well in my lessons. What I'm focused on is the missing equipment and the talking over me, because those are getting in the way of her learning." |
| "The other teachers gave her detentions too." | "That's a fair thing to look at. I'd like to understand what happened in those lessons too, and [head of year] is going to look at the whole picture with us." |
| "Take the detentions off." | "I can't remove them myself, but I'll take your concern to [head of year], who will review it. What I can do today is put a plan in place for the equipment and the lessons." |

### 8. Next steps to agree

- A two-week equipment routine with a quick check at the start of your lesson.
- A clear way for [S1] to ask for help or a pen without losing face.
- You will update mum by email after two weeks, whatever the news.
- The head of year reviews the pattern across [S1]'s timetable and reports back.

### 9. Follow-up email (send within 24 hours)

**Subject:** Following up from our meeting about [S1]

Dear [Parent name],

Thank you for coming in today. As we agreed, here is what happens next:

1. [Action, who, by when]
2. [Action, who, by when]
3. I will email you by [date] to let you know how things are going.

We both want [S1] to settle and do well, and I'm glad we could talk it through. If you have any questions before then, please contact me or [head of year].

Kind regards,
[Your name]

**Note to self:** only put in the email what was actually said and agreed in the room. Write up your own notes straight after the meeting while they are fresh.
