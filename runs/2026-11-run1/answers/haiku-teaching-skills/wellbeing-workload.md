# Cutting marking time this term without hurting learning

I've assumed a secondary school in England where marking policy currently asks for detailed written comments on every piece of work.

## 1. Agree the purpose with staff first

Marking is there to change what students do next, not to prove that marking happened. Ask each department to name the three marking tasks that have the biggest effect on learning. Everything else becomes optional. Staff are much more willing to drop a routine they helped choose.

## 2. Triage by purpose

- **Full marking:** end-of-unit assessments and one extended writing piece per half-term. These drive planning, so they get proper attention.
- **Light marking:** classwork and homework. Use ticks, a shared code, or a stamp. Students check against a mark scheme shown on the board.
- **No marking:** practice tasks. Students mark their own or a partner's work against a shown answer, then the teacher scans for the common errors.

## 3. Replace individual comments with whole-class feedback

Read a sample of 6 to 8 books, note the three most common errors and one strength, and write a single feedback sheet. Students spend 10 minutes on a DIRT or redraft task in the next lesson. This usually saves hours, and students act on it more reliably than on a comment they may not read.

## 4. Use a shared code sheet

Agree a short list of codes across the department, for example SP (spelling), EV (needs more evidence), ? (unclear). Teachers write at most one next step per piece. Students learn the codes quickly.

## 5. Move checking into lesson time

- Mini whiteboards and hinge questions show misconceptions as they happen, so fewer books need correcting later.
- Exit tickets (three questions on a slip) give a quick snapshot without marking a whole set.

## 6. Protect the calendar

- Stagger heavy marking across the week so no one has three full sets due on the same night.
- Agree a maximum load per fortnight for each teacher.
- Ask SLT that any new data drop or extra marking task comes with something stopped in return.

## 7. Check it is working

After four weeks, give a three-question retrieval quiz on a topic covered by the whole-class feedback. If results hold or improve, keep the change. If they fall, bring back full marking for that task only.

## 8. Morale

- For every new routine, remove one low-value one, such as double-marking the same work or a sticker chart.
- Say publicly what has been stopped. Staff notice when a change is real.
- Keep a short list of what staff find most useful and review it at each department meeting.
