# Restorative plan: two Year 9 students (Student A and Student B)

The sanction stands and is not up for discussion. This plan is about repairing the relationship and making the next lesson, online or at lunch, go better.

## Before tomorrow

- **Facts first.** Read the online comment and the lunchtime account. Record what you know, what each boy says, and what you can verify.
- **Safeguarding check.** If the comment or the fight involved threats, images, or anything that worries you about either boy's safety, follow the school's safeguarding procedure before any conversation.
- **Format.** Meet each boy separately for about ten minutes first. Only hold a short joint meeting if both are calm and both agree.
- **Timing.** Find a quiet room, not in front of peers or in the corridor.

## Separate meeting: questions in order

Adapt the wording to Year 9. Keep it calm and conversational.

1. "What happened?" Listen. Don't argue the details.
2. "What were you thinking at the time?"
3. "How were you feeling?"
4. "Who has been affected, and how?"
5. "What do you need to do to put it right?"
6. "What could you do differently next time it starts to happen?"
7. "What can I do to help?"

## Your opening (for each boy)

"I want to understand what happened online and at lunch, and then work out how to put it right. The consequence is already done, so today is about what happens next."

## Your part

- Name the behaviour, not the person: "The comment hurt him, and the fight stopped lunch from being safe for everyone."
- Own your part if there was one: "I should have checked in with you both on Monday when things were tense."
- Do not ask either boy to apologise publicly if he is not ready. A private apology is enough to start.

## Possible repairs to discuss

- The post is taken down, and the boy who wrote it confirms this to you.
- Both boys agree one way to handle a disagreement in school, such as speaking to a member of staff first.
- Neither boy posts about the other, and any future problem goes to you, not online.
- A short check-in with you, two days later, to see how things are going.

## Joint meeting (only if both agree)

Keep it to about ten minutes. Each boy says one thing he will do differently. Agree one shared action. Close with: "Tomorrow's a new lesson. I'm looking forward to seeing you both there."

## Follow-up

- Check in with each boy two days later, in a quick corridor conversation.
- Record the facts, the agreed repair and the follow-up date on the school system.
- Review after one week. If the online behaviour continues, involve the pastoral lead and follow the school's policy.
