# A Year 9 history homework that still works when AI is around

An essay titled "Explain the causes of the First World War" is exactly the sort of task a chatbot finishes in seconds, so the learning never happens. Replace it with a three-part task. Students answer from memory first, then check an AI paragraph for errors, then explain their thinking out loud. Each part has a different AI level, stated clearly.

## The task (student-facing)

**Part 1: No AI (10 minutes, notes closed)**
Write down everything you remember about the causes of the First World War. Sort your points under the letters MAIN: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism. Choose the three causes you think mattered most, and write one sentence for each explaining why.

**Part 2: AI to check (15 minutes)**
The paragraph below was written by an AI tool. It contains three factual mistakes. Find them, correct each one using your textbook or class notes, and write one sentence saying what a reader would wrongly believe because of each mistake.

> The First World War began in 1915 after Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Austria was assassinated in Vienna. Britain declared war on Germany on 4 August 1914 after Germany invaded Belgium. The Treaty of Versailles, signed in 1914, made Germany pay for the war.

**Part 3: Explain it live (2 minutes)**
Without notes, tell your teacher which cause from MAIN was the most important and why. Your teacher will ask one follow-up question.

**What to hand in:** your Part 1 notes, your corrected paragraph with the three explanations, and a note of what you said in Part 3.

## Teacher section

**AI level by part:** Part 1 is no AI. Part 2 is AI to check. Part 3 is a live explanation with no devices.

**Planted errors in Part 2:** "1915" (the war began in 1914); "Vienna" (Franz Ferdinand was shot in Sarajevo); "signed in 1914" (the Treaty of Versailles was signed in 1919).

**Marking guide (credits thinking):**
- Part 1: 1 mark for each of three causes, 1 mark for each reason that links the cause to events.
- Part 2: 1 mark for each error found and corrected from a source, 1 mark for each accurate explanation of what it would mislead.
- Part 3: 2 marks for an explanation that picks one cause and links it to specific events, plus a sensible answer to the follow-up.

**Why this protects learning:** Part 1 forces recall before any tool is used. Part 2 needs close reading and a textbook, which a pasted answer can't supply. Part 3 can't be handed to a machine, and a student who can explain one cause aloud has learned it.
