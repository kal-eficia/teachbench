# Year 8 Lesson (1 hour): How AI Chatbots Work and How to Use Them for Learning

## Assumptions

- Students are about 12 to 13 and in a UK-style secondary school.
- Students don't have their own AI accounts, so the teacher runs a live demo on the board or projector. Students use the demo and their own notes. If the school allows supervised student use, the teacher can run the activity in pairs.
- No prior AI knowledge is assumed.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the lesson, students can:
1. Explain in simple terms that a chatbot predicts likely words from patterns in text, which is why it can sound confident and still be wrong.
2. Spot at least two mistakes in an AI answer about something they know well.
3. Write a prompt that asks the chatbot to help them learn, not just to give the answer.

## Lesson plan

**Starter (5 minutes): Guess the next word.** Put the start of a sentence on the board, such as "The cat sat on the...". Students call out likely endings. Ask: "How did you know which word comes next?" Link this to how a chatbot works.

**Explain (10 minutes): How a chatbot works.**
- A chatbot has read huge amounts of text. It doesn't look things up in a book. It predicts the most likely next word, again and again.
- So it can produce fluent, confident text that is wrong.
- Use a simple analogy: a very good autocomplete that has read a library, but has no way of checking its own facts.

**Activity 1 (15 minutes): Catch it out.** In pairs, students ask the demo chatbot about something they know well: a football club's last season, their town, or a topic from last term. They record:
- one thing that is correct
- one thing that is wrong or made up
- one thing they can't check

Then discuss: Why did the chatbot get that wrong? Did it sound sure? Would a source show it?

**Activity 2 (15 minutes): Prompts that help learning.** Show two prompts on the board:
- "Write my homework answer about the causes of the First World War."
- "Quiz me on the causes of the First World War, one question at a time. Don't give me the answer until I've tried."

Students compare what each prompt would do to their learning. Then they write one prompt of their own for a topic they're studying, and the teacher runs it live.

**Discussion (8 minutes): Rules and honesty.** Go through the school's rules on AI use: no AI, AI to check, or AI as a tool. Ask: when would using AI count as cheating in an assessment? Keep it factual and avoid shaming.

**Exit question (5 minutes).** Students answer on a slip or in their books:
"Give one reason a chatbot can be wrong, and one way you would check an answer before you trusted it."

## Teacher notes

- Misconception to listen for: "The AI knows the truth, so it can't be wrong." Address it directly with the catch-it-out results.
- Misconception to listen for: "AI is only for cheating." Show the quiz prompt as a real study use.
- Differentiation: give a prompt card with a sentence starter for students who find writing prompts hard. Give stretch students a question on bias: whose views might be missing from the chatbot's answers, and why?
- Keep the demo short and live. Pre-check the topic questions in the demo before the lesson, so the errors you want to discuss appear.
