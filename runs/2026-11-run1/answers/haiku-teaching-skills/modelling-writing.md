# Modelling an analytical paragraph: "The Eagle" (Tennyson)

**Assumptions:** one lesson of about 50 minutes, Year 7, the class has the poem printed. The poem is Tennyson's "The Eagle" (1851):

> He clasps the crag with crooked hands;
> Close to the sun in lonely lands,
> Ring'd with the azure world, he stands.
> The wrinkled sea beneath him crawls;
> He watches from his mountain walls,
> And like a thunderbolt he falls.

**Paragraph structure for students:** Point (name the technique and its effect), Evidence (a short quotation), Explain (zoom in on one word), Link (back to the poem's effect or the question).

---

## I do: teacher models (about 7 minutes)

**Teacher script (say it aloud as you write on the board):**

"The question asks how Tennyson presents the eagle, so I'll start with the first line. I could write 'Tennyson shows the eagle is nice.' Hmm. 'Nice' is a feeling, not a technique, and I haven't quoted anything. I need a technique word and a quotation. Let me fix that.

"Look at the verb 'clasps'. Why did he choose that verb rather than 'holds'? A clasp is a firm, close grip, so I'm going to explain that the eagle is holding on to the rock with control. 'Crooked' is an adjective I want to zoom in on. Hooked hands look like talons, so I'll write that the reader can picture sharp claws, which makes the eagle seem dangerous as well as lonely.

"Now let me check: does this link back to the question? Yes, it shows the eagle as powerful, which is how Tennyson presents it."

**Model paragraph on the board:**

> Tennyson uses the verb "clasps" to show how the eagle holds on to the rock. A clasp is a firm, close grip, which suggests the eagle is strong and in control of its high position. The adjective "crooked" adds a sharper image, because hooked hands look like talons, so the reader pictures a bird ready to grab its prey. This makes the eagle seem powerful and dangerous rather than simply alone.

---

## We do: guided practice (about 15 minutes)

**Quotation:** "The wrinkled sea beneath him crawls."

**Teacher prompts, in order (students suggest each step; you question and refine):**
1. "What is happening to the sea in this line? Is the sea really moving like an animal?"
2. "Which word makes you think of an animal? What is that technique called?" (Personification, through the verb "crawls".)
3. "Why would the poet make the sea crawl rather than move? What does that do for the eagle?" (It makes the sea look slow, low and far beneath the eagle, so the height of the eagle is emphasised.)
4. "Let's build the sentence together."

**Writing frame on the board:**
> Tennyson personifies the sea by using the verb "______", which suggests ______. This makes the eagle seem ______ because ______.

**Check before release:** Ask each student to show you one sentence on a mini whiteboard. Ask a few students to read theirs aloud and say which part of the frame they used.

---

## You do: independent practice with support (about 15 minutes)

**Task A (with the writing frame and sentence starters available):**
Write a paragraph on the line "He watches from his mountain walls."

**Model answer:**
> Tennyson uses the verb "watches" to show that the eagle is patient and alert. Watching suggests it waits calmly for the right moment, so the reader sees it as controlled rather than frantic. This matters because the poem presents the eagle as powerful, and that power is used carefully.

**Task B (no frame, support removed):**
Write a paragraph on the line "And like a thunderbolt he falls."

**Model answer:**
> Tennyson uses a simile, comparing the eagle's fall to a thunderbolt. A thunderbolt is sudden and powerful, so the reader senses the speed and force of the dive. The effect is that the attack feels dramatic and impossible to stop, which makes the ending exciting.

---

## Success criteria (student-facing)

- I can name a technique (for example: verb, adjective, simile, personification).
- I can quote a short phrase from the poem accurately.
- I can explain what the technique makes the reader think or feel, using one word from my quotation.
- I link my point back to the eagle's power or to the question I was asked.
- I check my paragraph against the four parts before I hand it in.
