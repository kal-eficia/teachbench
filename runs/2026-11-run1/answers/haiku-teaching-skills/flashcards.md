# Rates of reaction: flashcards for Year 11 chemistry

Q: What must happen for a reaction to take place, according to collision theory? | A: Reacting particles must collide with enough energy and in the correct orientation.
Q: What is collision theory? | A: The idea that reactions happen when reacting particles collide with enough energy and in the correct orientation.
Q: What is the activation energy? | A: The minimum energy that colliding particles must have for a reaction to happen.
Q: What is a successful collision? | A: A collision with energy at or above the activation energy and in the correct orientation.
Q: What is an unsuccessful collision? | A: A collision that does not have enough energy or the wrong orientation, so no reaction happens.
Q: What is the rate of reaction? | A: How quickly reactants are used up or products are formed.
Q: How do you work out the mean rate of reaction? | A: Quantity of product formed (or reactant used) divided by the time taken.
Q: What units are used for rate when gas volume is measured in cm³ and time in seconds? | A: cm³/s.
Q: Why does increasing temperature speed up a reaction? | A: Particles move faster, so they collide more often and more collisions have energy at or above the activation energy.
Q: Does raising the temperature lower the activation energy? | A: No. It gives more particles enough energy to reach the activation energy.
Q: Why does increasing concentration speed up a reaction? | A: There are more particles in the same volume, so collisions happen more often.
Q: Does a higher concentration mean the particles have more energy? | A: No. It means more particles per unit volume, not more energy per particle.
Q: Why does increasing the pressure of a gas speed up a reaction? | A: The gas particles are squeezed into a smaller volume, so they collide more often.
Q: Does changing pressure affect reactions involving only solids or liquids? | A: No, pressure has little or no effect on solids and liquids.
Q: Why does increasing surface area speed up a reaction? | A: More particles are exposed on the surface, so more collisions can happen at the same time.
Q: Give an everyday example of surface area speeding up a reaction. | A: A powder reacts faster than a lump of the same mass.
Q: What is a catalyst? | A: A substance that speeds up a reaction and is not used up in it.
Q: How does a catalyst speed up a reaction? | A: It provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy.
Q: Does a catalyst change the energy of the reactants or products? | A: No. It only lowers the activation energy.
Q: Is a catalyst used up at the end of a reaction? | A: No, it is chemically unchanged at the end.
Q: Why does a reaction slow down as it goes on? | A: The reactant concentration falls, so collisions become less frequent.
Q: On a graph of mass or volume against time, what does a steeper gradient show? | A: A faster rate of reaction.
Q: On a graph of product against time, what does a flat line show? | A: The reaction has stopped or the rate is zero.
Q: How can rate be measured by collecting a gas? | A: Measure the volume of gas produced at regular time intervals using a gas syringe or an upturned measuring cylinder.
Q: How can rate be measured when a precipitate forms? | A: Place a cross under the flask and time how long it takes for the cross to disappear from view.
Q: How can rate be measured when a gas escapes and mass is lost? | A: Put the flask on a balance and record the mass at regular intervals.
Q: Is the rate of reaction the same as the amount of product? | A: No. Rate is how fast product forms. Amount is the total product made.
Q: Is activation energy the energy released by a reaction? | A: No. It is the minimum energy needed to start the reaction.
Q: What does a successful collision need that an unsuccessful one lacks? | A: Enough energy and the correct orientation.

**How to use these cards:** say the answer out loud before you flip the card. Sort the cards into "know" and "not yet". Go back to the "not yet" pile the next day, then every few days until you know them all. Eight weeks is enough time to do this about four times.
