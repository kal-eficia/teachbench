# Supporting a Year 4 pupil with ADHD in class (quick, low-prep strategies)

**Assumptions:** you have a class of around 30, one shared timer, and the pupil's support plan or SENCO advice is already in place. These are things you can do without extra planning time. Check the support plan first, because any agreed adjustment in it comes before these.

## Help him start tasks

- **Start him yourself, for 30 seconds.** Before he begins, go over to him and do the first question or first sentence together. Then leave him to finish. This is the most useful single move.
- **Break the task into 5 to 10 minute chunks.** Write three ticks on his sheet or a small checklist, one per chunk. Tick them as he completes them. He can see the end.
- **Use a visible timer.** Set it for the first chunk. A quick "beep, next chunk" is enough.
- **Seat him near you, with a clear view of the board** and away from the door or the busiest table.

## Calling out

- **Agree one signal.** For example, a hand-raise card on his desk. Praise him the moment he uses it, even if you cannot take his answer yet.
- **Take his answer later.** Write it on a "parking board" at the front, then go back to it in the plenary. He knows he has been heard.
- Give **specific praise** for waiting: "You waited and your idea was on the board, well done."

## Getting up

- **Give him a real job.** Collecting books, handing out whiteboards, or wiping the board at set times. Keep the job short and tied to the routine.
- **Give him a movement pass.** A card he can hand you once or twice a lesson, for a quick stretch or a trip to the window or sink.

## Save time

- **Make one checklist template** for all tasks, with three chunks and a tick box. Use it for the whole class. The other pupils often use it too.
- **Use a 30-second check-in** at the start of the day to set one target for the morning. Keep it the same each day.

## Fading

- Keep the start check and the chunks through the term, since they help the whole class.
- Reduce the movement pass once he can wait for a break without it, usually after several weeks.
- Keep the calling-out signal as long as it works. If it stops working, check with the SENCO before changing it.

## Note on sharing

Keep his details between you, the SENCO and his family. Do not mention his diagnosis to the class.
