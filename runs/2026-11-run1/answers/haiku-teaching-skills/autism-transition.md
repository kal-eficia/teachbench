# Preparing him for a cover day and a museum trip

Some of this depends on what he already knows and what his support plan says, so I've assumed he has a timetable he's used to, a trusted adult in school, and some known signs that a change is getting too much. Adjust the details to what you know about him.

## Before the week (ideally Monday or Tuesday of the week before)

**1. Tell him in a calm, one-to-one moment.**
Keep it short and literal. Say what will happen, in order, and what will stay the same.

**2. Make a visual "week ahead" card.**
Use pictures or symbols if he uses them. Show each day in a row:
- Mon and Tue: [cover teacher's name] takes your lesson. Your form, lunch and home time stay the same.
- Thu: museum trip. Meet at [place] at [time], travel on [bus or coach], back at school by [time].

Put one line beside each day about what stays the same. Keep the card in his planner or on his desk.

**3. Make a "stays the same" list.**
Write down the things that won't change: his form room, his seat, his lunch routine, the end-of-day routine, and his named adult. Read it with him.

**4. Brief the cover teacher with a one-page profile.**
Include:
- How he likes instructions: written, numbered, one step at a time, with the task on the board.
- Warnings before any change ("In five minutes we will stop").
- What helps when he gets overwhelmed (for example, a quiet space, a named adult, or a break card he can hold up).
- The name of the adult he trusts and how to reach them if needed.
- Anything he finds hard about unexpected noise or touch.

If the cover teacher can meet him for five minutes before the lesson, that helps a lot.

## On cover days

- Keep the lesson structure the same as his normal lessons, with the order of activities shown on the board at the start.
- Give written, literal instructions. Avoid idioms, and say "Please write three sentences" rather than "Have a go at this".
- Give him a defined role in group work, or the option to work alone.
- Give a five-minute warning before each transition.
- Let him take a break card or go to his named adult without having to explain.

## The museum trip

**Before:**
- Look at the museum's website for the entrance, toilets, a quiet room and the layout. Share the basic route with him in pictures or a short numbered sequence.
- Give him a simple timeline for the day: arrive, first gallery, lunch, second gallery, bus back.
- Agree a signal he can use if he needs to leave a room (a card, a hand signal or a word).
- Ask whether he would like ear defenders or headphones for busy spaces. Check the museum allows them.

**On the day:**
- Pair him with his named adult, or with a trusted peer if that's his preference.
- Plan a quiet space or a bench away from the group for lunch and breaks.
- Keep the same timeline on a card in his pocket and tick off each stage as it happens.
- Keep instructions short and literal. If he becomes distressed, reduce language, give him space and use the agreed signal. Don't stop him from leaving the room.

**After:**
- Ask him one or two simple questions about the day (for example, "What was your favourite object?") rather than a long debrief.

## What changed and why

- Visual timetable and "stays the same" list: give him the predictability he needs for the change.
- Cover teacher profile and warnings: cover adults don't know him, so the structure must be explicit.
- Museum pre-visit and signal: reduce surprises and give him a way to communicate needs without explaining.
- Challenge: he still does the same learning and the same tasks on cover days. The route changes, not the destination.

## When to fade each support

- The "week ahead" card can be dropped once he's taken two or three changes calmly, usually after the next half-term.
- Warnings before transitions can be gradually reduced in the next few weeks, as he shows he's coping without them.
- Keep the cover teacher profile on file for future cover days. It takes minutes to update.
- Keep the named adult and quiet-space arrangement in place until you and his support team agree otherwise.
