# Adapted Task: The Great Plague in London (Year 8)

*Teacher note: print Source A and Source B in the layout below (sans-serif font, 14 pt, 1.5 line spacing, left-aligned, short paragraphs). The original task's wording and thinking are kept. Only the route through it changes.*

---

## Your mission

You are a historian. You will compare two sources about the Great Plague in London in 1665, then decide which one is more useful for an enquiry into the impact of the plague.

**Key words** (say them out loud before you start):
- **Source**: a piece of evidence from the past
- **Compare**: say what is the same and what is different
- **Useful**: helpful for answering your question
- **Enquiry**: a careful investigation

---

## Step 1: Read Source A (10 minutes)

- Read Source A once, all the way through.
- Tick one thing you understand.
- Circle one word you don't know. Your teacher will help you with it.

*Option: your teacher can read it aloud, or you can listen to an audio version.*

---

## Step 2: Read Source B (10 minutes)

- Read Source B with the same steps as Step 1.
- Tick one thing you understand.
- Circle one word you don't know.

---

## Step 3: Fill in the comparison grid (15 minutes)

Work through the grid one row at a time. Write short answers, or say them to your teacher and they will type them.

| Question | Source A | Source B |
|---|---|---|
| How did Londoners react? (find one example) | | |
| Who wrote it, and when? | | |
| What might make this source biased or incomplete? | | |

**Sentence starters** (use one):
- "Source A shows that..."
- "Source B suggests that..."
- "The biggest difference is..."

---

## Step 4: Use what you already know (10 minutes)

Think about what you already know about the plague. Choose one fact you know, and write or say it in one sentence.

**Starter:** "I already know that... This helps me because..."

---

## Step 5: Choose the more useful source (15 minutes)

Pick **Source A** or **Source B** as more useful for the enquiry into the impact of the plague.

Use this 3-part structure:
1. **My choice:** "I think ___ is more useful because..."
2. **Evidence:** one detail from the source
3. **My own knowledge:** one fact you already know

**Sentence starters:**
- "I think ___ is more useful because it tells me..."
- "However, ___ is less useful because..."
- "My own knowledge also shows that..."

*Option: record your answer as a short voice note, or talk it through with your teacher.*

---

## Finish (5 minutes)

Check your work with this list:
- [ ] I read both sources.
- [ ] I filled in at least two rows of the grid.
- [ ] I chose one source and gave one reason.
- [ ] I used one fact from my own knowledge.

---

## Teacher notes (for you only)

- **What changed:** the reading is split into two short steps with a tick and a circle each. The comparison is a grid, so students don't need to hold the whole task in their heads. Key words are defined before the start. Each step has a time limit. Students can speak or record their answers. The thinking stays the same: compare, judge usefulness, and use own knowledge.
- **What stayed the same:** the two sources, the enquiry focus, and the requirement to explain the choice with own knowledge.
- **Fading:** in the next task, remove the grid and the sentence starters first. Keep the key words and the checklist. Later, remove the step-by-step timings.
- **Check:** confirm with the student's support plan or SENCO advice before using the audio, voice note and reading options, as well as the layout.
