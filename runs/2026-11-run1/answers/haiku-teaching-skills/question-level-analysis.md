# Year 11 Foundation Mock: Where to Focus in the 10 Lessons

## Assumptions

- Each question is worth 3 marks (84 available across 28 students) except Q3 and Q14, which are worth 2 marks (56 available).
- The figures are the marks lost across the whole class, so the main issue is shared, not confined to a few students.
- The data doesn't show whether each error was a knowledge gap or an exam-technique problem. I've made a judgement from the question type, and you can check it by looking at 5 or 6 scripts per question.

Four questions (Q17, Q19, Q21, Q9) account for 254 of the 359 marks lost, so they should get most of your time.

## Plan for the 10 lessons

**Lessons 1 and 2: Probability trees (Q17).** Reading the branches, multiplying along a path, adding paths for "or", and what changes when items are taken without replacement. Use a diagram before any numbers, and make students check that each set of branches adds to 1.

**Lessons 3 and 4: Compound interest (Q19).** Lesson 3 builds the multiplier idea (1.05 means 105%, and each year multiplies the amount), with the contrast against simple interest. Lesson 4 covers depreciation and finding the number of years, plus calculator use for powers.

**Lesson 5: Pythagoras (Q21).** Identify the hypotenuse first, then substitute. Practise rearranging for a shorter side (subtract the squares) and checking the answer is sensible.

**Lesson 6: Ratio sharing (Q9).** Add the parts, divide the total by that number, then multiply by each part. Include one question with a total given in a context (for example, £84 shared in the ratio 3:4).

**Lesson 7: Area of compound shapes (Q7).** Split into rectangles and triangles, then decide whether to add or subtract a cut-out. Units squared must be stated.

**Lesson 8: Linear equations with unknowns on both sides (Q12).** Use a balance model, then move the smaller unknown term first. Always substitute back to check.

**Lesson 9: Pie charts and fractions of amounts (Q14 and Q3).** Angle = (value ÷ total) × 360. Fractions of amounts go in as a quick starter.

**Lesson 10: Timed mixed practice.** A 40-minute mixed set from Lessons 1 to 9, with students working out why each answer is right. Mark it in class and use the results to decide what to reteach next.

## Intervention

- Whole-class reteach for Q17, Q19, Q21 and Q9. These affect most of the class.
- Put students who scored zero on three or more of the eight questions into a small group for 2 short sessions a week. Check the mark sheet for these students and don't rely on a memory of who they are.
- Add two command-word starter questions each lesson. For example, "Explain why you multiply along a branch", which pushes students to justify a method and not just produce a number.
