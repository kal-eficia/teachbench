# Feedback on the Year 9 lesson

*Written for the early career teacher. Based on your notes: strong relationships, clear explanation, 25 minutes of teacher talk, mostly volunteer answers, and an end task too hard for about a third of the class.*

---

Hi,

Thank you for letting me sit in on your Year 9 lesson. Here is what I saw and what I'd like us to work on.

## What worked

**1. The relationships.** Students were settled and comfortable with you. You greeted them by name, and you made it easy for them to ask for help. That is the foundation for everything else, and it's clearly already in place.

**2. The explanation.** Your explanation of the topic was clear and well ordered. Students who were following closely could see where each idea was going. Keep that.

## One priority: getting every student thinking, not only the volunteers

For about 25 minutes, the students were mostly listening. Questions were answered by the same few volunteers. This means most of the class was not thinking hard for much of the lesson, even though they were in the room and paying attention.

**The action step:** Every 5 to 7 minutes, stop talking and give a 30-second task that everyone must do. For example: "Write down the one word that describes this idea" or "Show me with your fingers, one to three, how sure you are." Then take an answer from the whole class before you go on. Use "think, then talk" with a partner before you pick anyone to answer.

**Why this matters:** A short task every few minutes means you can see what the whole class understands. It also means students who never volunteer are doing the thinking, not just watching.

**Practice in the feedback conversation:** Let's rehearse the 30-second task together. You say the instruction, I play a student who doesn't know the answer, and we see how it feels to wait three seconds before moving on.

## A second point to talk through

The end task was too hard for about a third of the class. We don't need to fix this in the feedback, but it is worth looking at together. Start with: "What did you notice about the students who finished the task early, and the ones who didn't?" Your answer may already point to a fix, such as a first question that everyone can answer, with a harder one for those who want it.

## Follow-up

- Next time I'm in, I'd like to see at least three of the "everyone thinks first" moments in the lesson.
- Success looks like every student responding at least once in a 10-minute stretch, not only the volunteers.
- Let's check in after your next observed lesson, and look at the end task together before it's taught.

Thanks again for a warm and clear lesson. The next step is small and very doable.

---

## Conversation plan

**Opening questions**
- "How do you think the lesson went?"
- "What did you notice about who was answering?"
- "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"

**Rehearsal:** Practise the 30-second task aloud, with the wait time built in.
