# Eight-month NEA plan for Year 13 OCR A level Computer Science

**Assumptions:** OCR H446, students start in December, and the centre's submission deadline falls at the end of the eight months (July). OCR sets the submission deadline for centres and it is often earlier, usually in spring. Check the date with your exams officer. If it is earlier, fold months 7 and 8 into the buffer. I could not check OCR's current NEA document from here, so confirm the help rules below against it before you brief students.

## What help you can give

- **You can:** explain the assessment criteria and what they reward, answer questions about the requirements, point students to sources, and give general feedback on work in progress tied to the criteria.
- **Example of allowed feedback:** "Your evaluation doesn't yet refer to your success criteria." Stop there and let the student decide what to change.
- **You cannot:** write any part of the work, supply designs, algorithms, code, test data or evaluation text, or fix code or wording for a student.
- **Authentication:** every student signs a declaration that the work is their own. Keep dated notes of the help you gave each student, because you will need to show that the work is theirs.
- **AI:** students must not submit AI-generated work as their own. Confirm the exact wording on AI use and acknowledgement in OCR's current guidance and make sure students know it before December.

## Stage plan

| Month | Stage | Students should have by the end of the month |
|---|---|---|
| Dec | Proposal | A chosen problem with a named user or client, and a short proposal the teacher has signed off |
| Jan | Analysis | Stakeholder and problem description, measurable objectives, success criteria, research on existing solutions, and evidence from any interview or survey |
| Feb | Design | Solution structure, data structures, key algorithms, interface designs, and a test plan with test data written before coding starts |
| Mar | Development, sprint 1 | Core features working, with dated version saves and a short log of problems and fixes |
| Apr | Development, sprint 2 | Remaining features, with testing evidence recorded as each feature is built |
| May | Complete development and testing | Finished solution, full testing log with expected and actual results, and fixes shown |
| Jun | Evaluation | Each success criterion assessed with evidence, user or client feedback, and what they would change |
| Jul | Write-up, check and submit | Tidied write-up, a final check against the mark scheme, the authentication declaration, and a buffer for problems |

## Keeping them on track

- **Internal deadlines two weeks early.** Set each stage's internal deadline two weeks before the real one, so a slip doesn't cost the stage.
- **Stage gates.** No development starts until the design is signed off. No evaluation starts until testing is complete.
- **Evidence every week.** A 20-minute weekly slot where each student shows their dated log, states this week's target and names what comes next.
- **Catch-up for slow starters.** Any student with no analysis evidence by the end of January gets one-to-one time within the next fortnight.
- **Stop the last-month trap.** The students who leave everything to the end usually lose the testing and the evaluation. Make testing evidence part of every development sprint and make an evaluation draft due in June.

## Feedback prompts, linked to the criteria

1. "Your objectives can be tested. Which one could you check with a test right now?"
2. "Your design shows the algorithm. Does it show how the data is stored?"
3. "Your testing log has the expected results. Where is the actual result for the boundary case?"
4. "Your evaluation says the solution works. What evidence from your testing shows that?"
5. "Your write-up explains what you did. Does it explain why you chose that approach over the alternative?"
