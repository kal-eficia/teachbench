# A level Economics: 15-minute retrieval starter (Year 13)

**Assumptions:** Year 13, exam in five months. The class studied micro last year, so the starter mixes macro (this term) with micro (last year) to keep old content alive. Ten short-answer questions. Allow about 12 minutes to answer and 3 minutes to go through the key together.

## Student sheet

**Instructions:** No notes, no textbook, no phone. Write what you remember. Next to each question, write Certain, Think so, or Guessing.

1. **(Last year's micro)** Write the formula for price elasticity of demand. **[1]**
2. **(Macro)** Write the equation for aggregate demand and name its four components. **[2]**
3. **(Micro)** Define a merit good and give one example. **[2]**
4. **(Macro)** Define cyclical unemployment. **[2]**
5. **(Micro)** Explain why demand for petrol tends to be inelastic in the short run. **[3]**
6. **(Macro)** Distinguish between demand-pull inflation and cost-push inflation. **[2]**
7. **(Micro)** Explain how a maximum price set below the equilibrium price can create a shortage. **[3]**
8. **(Macro)** Using an AD and AS diagram, explain the effect on the price level and real output of an increase in aggregate demand when the short-run aggregate supply curve is upward sloping. **[3]**
9. **(Macro)** Explain how an increase in Bank Rate could reduce inflation. **[3]**
10. **(Micro)** Explain how a tax on a good with a negative externality can move the market closer to the socially optimal output. **[3]**

**Total: 24 marks**

---

## Teacher key

Use this to check answers quickly as a class. Full marks are shown in brackets, with marking points for each.

**1. Price elasticity of demand [1]**
PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price. The reason for this question: last year's micro, needed for the exam and easy to forget over a summer.

**2. Aggregate demand [2]**
AD = C + I + G + (X − M). Components: consumption, investment, government spending, net exports. The reason: the base of all macro diagrams this term.

**3. Merit good [2]**
A good that society values more than the free market provides, so it is under-consumed. Example: education, healthcare. Accept: any sensible example. The reason: definitions from micro are often confused with public goods, so this checks the difference.

**4. Cyclical unemployment [2]**
Unemployment caused by insufficient aggregate demand in the economy, during the downswing of the business cycle. The reason: a common mix-up with structural and frictional unemployment.

**5. Petrol and inelastic demand [3]**
Any three of: few close substitutes in the short run; many people need it to get to work; it is a small share of budgets, or people have no quick alternative; habits and car dependency. The reason: applies elasticity to a real case, which is a common exam requirement.

**6. Demand-pull and cost-push inflation [2]**
Demand-pull: AD rises faster than the economy's capacity to supply. Cost-push: costs of production rise (for example energy or wages) and shift SRAS left. Both are needed for the mark. The reason: the most common inflation question in exams.

**7. Maximum price and shortage [3]**
Three marks: the maximum price is below equilibrium; quantity demanded rises; quantity supplied falls, so a shortage results. The reason: links the micro price mechanism to intervention, which they studied last year.

**8. AD shift on upward-sloping SRAS [3]**
Three marks: AD shifts right; the price level rises; real output (real GDP) rises. The reason: the key AD/AS diagram question for this term.

**9. Bank Rate and inflation [3]**
Three marks: higher Bank Rate raises the cost of borrowing; consumption and investment fall; AD falls, which reduces demand-pull pressure on prices. Accept: saving becomes more attractive. The reason: links monetary policy to the transmission mechanism.

**10. Tax on a negative externality [3]**
Three marks: the tax raises the private cost of producing or consuming; the supply curve shifts up (left); output falls towards the socially optimal level. Accept: the tax internalises the externality. The reason: links micro market failure and government intervention, which was last year's content.

**Suggested timing:** 12 minutes for answers, then about 3 minutes to go through the key. Ask students to write their Certain, Think so, or Guessing rating next to each question. Look most closely at confident-and-wrong answers, as these show the misconceptions to reteach.
