# Year 10 electricity quiz: what went wrong and what to do

Class-level analysis only. Counts are from your 26 students.

## Summary: fix this first

**Current is "used up" as it passes through a bulb (Q3, Q5).** This is the priority. Nine students were confident and wrong, and the same idea is behind the parallel-bulb answers. It won't fix itself.

---

## Misconception 1: current gets smaller or is used up (Q3)

- **The wrong idea:** "The current gets used up by the bulb, so less comes out the other side."
- **Evidence:** 14 of 26 students. 9 of them were very confident.
- **Why it's appealing:** The bulb is clearly doing something, and "used up" fits everyday talk about batteries and food.
- **The fix (5-minute reteach):**
  1. Draw a series loop with two ammeters, one before the bulb and one after it.
  2. Ask students to predict both readings. Then show them the readings are the same.
  3. Explain: charge flows round the circuit and isn't lost. The bulb transfers *energy* from electrical to light and heat. The current stays the same.
  4. Repeat with "energy is used up, but charge is not".
- **Hinge question (answer C):** Two ammeters are placed in a series circuit, one before a bulb and one after it. What do they read?
  - A) The second reading is smaller, because the bulb uses up the current
  - B) The second reading is larger, because the bulb adds current
  - C) The two readings are the same
  - D) The second reading is zero, because the bulb blocks the current
- **Revisit:** The next two starters.

## Misconception 2: parallel bulbs are dimmer than one bulb (Q5)

- **The wrong idea:** "Adding a second bulb in parallel shares the current out, so each bulb gets less."
- **Evidence:** 11 of 26 students. Most were not confident, which suggests they were guessing. Treat this as a misconception that is not yet secure rather than a confident error.
- **Why it's appealing:** The current does split at the junction, so "sharing" feels natural.
- **The fix (5-minute reteach):**
  1. Show a single bulb and a parallel pair on the same supply. Ask: "What is the voltage across each bulb?" Each bulb sees the full supply voltage.
  2. Explain: current splits between the branches, but each branch gets the full voltage. Each bulb has the same brightness as the single bulb.
  3. Contrast with series, where the voltage is shared between bulbs and each is dimmer.
- **Hinge question (answer A):** Two identical bulbs are connected in parallel to a battery. Compared with one bulb on the same battery, how bright is each bulb?
  - A) The same brightness
  - B) Dimmer, because the current is shared
  - C) Brighter, because there are two of them
  - D) Dimmer, because the battery is used up faster
- **Revisit:** The next starter.

## Misconception 3: a voltmeter measures current (Q6)

- **The wrong idea:** "A voltmeter measures current."
- **Evidence:** 4 students said current; 3 left it blank. The blanks are a sign of low confidence or a gap in what was taught, not a slip.
- **Why it's appealing:** Both meters look alike, and students often mix up which quantity each one measures.
- **The fix (5-minute reteach):**
  1. Put the names next to their symbols: ammeter (A, series, current) and voltmeter (V, parallel, voltage).
  2. Use a memory phrase: "Volts go across, amps go through." Ask students to say it back.
  3. Give a three-line test: which meter goes in series, and what unit does each read?
- **Hinge question (answer D):** A student wants to measure the voltage across a bulb. Where should the voltmeter be connected?
  - A) In series with the bulb
  - B) Between the battery and the switch
  - C) In series with the battery
  - D) In parallel across the bulb
- **Revisit:** The next two starters.

## Misconception 4: a battery stores electricity or charge (Q8)

- **The wrong idea:** "A battery stores electricity (or charge)."
- **Evidence:** 12 of 26 students. 8 were confident and wrong.
- **Why it's appealing:** "Battery" and "electricity" go together in everyday speech, and the word "store" feels right.
- **The fix (5-minute reteach):**
  1. Ask: "What is the battery *giving* the circuit?" Students usually say electricity. Ask again: "Is it giving charge, or energy?"
  2. Explain that a battery stores chemical energy. Chemical energy is transferred to electrical energy in the circuit, and then to light and heat in the bulb.
  3. Contrast: charge flows, energy transfers.
- **Hinge question (answer B):** A battery is connected to a bulb. What energy store is the battery's energy transferred from?
  - A) Electrical energy
  - B) Chemical energy
  - C) Charge
  - D) Light energy
- **Revisit:** The next two starters.

## Misconception 5: units of resistance (Q9)

- **The wrong idea:** Resistance is measured in amps (2 students) or volts (1 student).
- **Evidence:** 3 of 26 students. The numbers are small and look like slips, not a shared misconception.
- **The fix:** A quick reminder card: current = amps, voltage = volts, resistance = ohms. Include this in the next starter. No separate reteach is needed.

---

## One-line summary for the teacher

Fix the idea that current is used up (Q3), because nine students were confidently wrong and it drives the parallel-bulb answers (Q5). Then address the energy-versus-charge confusion in the battery question (Q8).
