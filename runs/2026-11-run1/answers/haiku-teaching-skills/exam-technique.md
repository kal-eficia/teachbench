# Lesson: how to win the marks on 9-mark "assess" questions (AQA GCSE Geography, Year 11)

**Assumptions:** 60-minute classroom lesson, students already know the content. You pick one case study they have studied for the model question. I could not check the current AQA mark scheme from here, so confirm the level descriptors and the 9-mark allocation against the AQA specification you teach before the lesson.

**Lesson aim:** students write a 9-mark "assess" answer that weighs up factors, reaches a supported judgement, and uses case study evidence, within about 10 minutes.

## Why students lose these marks

- They describe both sides and never weigh them up. "Assess" asks which matters more, or how far, not just what happens.
- They give no judgement, or give one with no reasons.
- They drop the case study and write general points.
- They run out of time and the conclusion is missing or rushed.

## Lesson plan

| Time | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Command word sort | Show five questions on the same topic, asking "describe", "explain", "assess", "evaluate", and "to what extent". Students say what each one demands. Use the AQA glossary to settle any disagreements. |
| 5 to 15 min | Think-aloud model | Model an "assess" answer with a timer. Plan for 60 seconds: two or three factors, a point for each, then which factor matters most and why. Write it live, saying each decision aloud. Stop at each paragraph and say why it earns its marks. |
| 15 to 25 min | Mark the model | Give three answers to the same question: weak (one-sided, no judgement), middle (both sides, weak judgement), and strong (weighed factors, judgement, case study data). Students mark them using the level descriptors and say what would lift each answer up a level. |
| 25 to 35 min | Timed single | One 9-mark question in 9 minutes, with 1 minute to plan. Students write it in silence, then highlight the sentence where they make their judgement. |
| 35 to 45 min | Upgrade it | Students take their own paragraph and make it stronger: add a judgement, add one piece of case study evidence, and link back to the question. |
| 45 to 55 min | Exit | Students write one sentence that weighs up two factors for a different question. Check a few on the way out. |
| 55 to 60 min | Buffer | Questions, or time for a student who needs more support. |

## Three model answers (for "Mark the model")

**Weak (Level 1 style):** "Tourism has good and bad effects. It creates jobs for local people. It also causes pollution from traffic. So tourism is both good and bad for the area."

**Middle (Level 2 style):** "Tourism brings jobs and money, which helps local people. However, the number of visitors causes erosion on footpaths and more traffic. Both are important, so tourism has a mixed impact on the area."

**Strong (Level 3 style):** "The most significant effect of tourism on [your case study] is the money it brings in, because visitor spending has funded new facilities and jobs. This outweighs the environmental costs, such as erosion of the footpaths. However, the benefits are shared unequally, as most jobs are seasonal and low paid. On balance, the economic benefits are the most important, but only if the local community shares in them." *(Replace the bracketed place with your case study and check the figures against your own notes.)*

## What students should check in their own answer

- Two or three factors, each developed with a link to the question.
- At least one piece of case study evidence for each factor.
- A judgement that weighs the factors, with a reason why one matters more or why the answer depends on something.
- A judgement in the final sentence, not just at the start.

## Homework

One more 9-mark "assess" question on a different topic, written in 9 minutes. Students use the checklist above to review it and bring it back for a teacher check in the next lesson.
