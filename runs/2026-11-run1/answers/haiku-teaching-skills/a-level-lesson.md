# A level Psychology (AQA): Milgram's study of obedience

*Assumptions: 70 minutes, 14 students, the class has covered Asch (conformity) last week. Lesson pack is in markdown, so the student page is written as formatted text to paste into the class document. Evaluation scaffold is PEEL (Point, Evidence, Explain, Link).*

---

# Teacher plan

**Objectives (AQA wording):** Describe and evaluate Milgram's (1963) study of obedience, including its procedure, findings, ethics and validity. Link the study to Asch (1951) as an example of social influence.

| Time | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 6 | Starter (recall) | Three questions on Asch. Students tick a confidence level for each: Guessing / Think so / Certain. |
| 6 to 20 | Learn | Milgram's aim, procedure and results, with the four prods and the learner as a confederate. |
| 20 to 27 | Learn 2 | Variations (proximity, remote instructions) and a quick pair discussion: "Why did people keep going?" |
| 27 to 32 | Check | Four MCQs. Cold-call on the answers, asking "why not the others?" |
| 32 to 55 | Apply | Evaluation: strengths and limitations, with a PEEL frame. Pairs draft, then write alone. |
| 55 to 65 | Model | Teacher models one evaluation paragraph live, thinking aloud, then students compare with their own. |
| 65 to 70 | Exit | "Name one strength and one limitation of Milgram's study, and say why it matters." |

**Misconceptions to listen for**
- "Milgram proved everyone would obey." Fix: 65% went to 450V, so 35% stopped. Ask what that tells us about the range of behaviour.
- "The study was unethical, so it's worthless." Fix: separate the ethics from the validity. Ethics affects what we can do now; validity is about what the findings tell us.
- "Asch and Milgram are both just about peer pressure." Fix: Asch is about groups and conformity to peers; Milgram is about obedience to an authority figure.

**Differentiation**
- Support: PEEL sentence frames printed on the page; the first paragraph is written with the teacher.
- Stretch: ask students to argue whether Milgram's study tells us more about obedience or about the situation, and link to Asch.

**Note for next lesson's starter:** Ask what makes an authority figure more or less powerful over people (links to the next topic).

## Learn: Milgram (1963)

**The aim.** Milgram wanted to see how far ordinary people would obey an authority figure who told them to hurt someone.

**The procedure.** Participants thought they were taking part in a memory experiment. They were given the role of "teacher". Each time the "learner" got an answer wrong, the teacher had to give an electric shock, going up from 15 volts to 450 volts in steps. The learner was actually an actor, and the shocks were not real. The experimenter, in a grey lab coat, gave prods when a participant wanted to stop:
- "Please continue."
- "The experiment requires that you continue."
- "It is absolutely essential that you continue."
- "You have no other choice, you must go on."

**The results.** In the original study, 65% of participants (26 out of 40) went all the way to 450 volts.

**Variations.** Milgram changed the situation to see what affected obedience. When the learner was in the same room, obedience dropped to 40%. When the experimenter gave instructions by phone rather than being present, obedience fell to 21%.

## Apply: evaluation

**Strengths** (things that make the study credible)
- The procedure was standardised, so every participant got the same prods in the same order.
- The results have been replicated. Burger (2009) found similar levels of obedience up to 150 volts, with safeguards in place.

**Limitations** (things that weaken the study)
- Only 40 men took part, so the findings may not generalise to women or to other groups.
- The lab setting may not be like real life, so the results may lack ecological validity.
- Some participants may have worked out that the shocks were not real, which affects the findings (demand characteristics).
- Participants were deceived and some experienced distress, which breaks ethical guidelines.

**Your task (8 marks):** Evaluate Milgram's study of obedience. Use PEEL for at least two points.

**PEEL frame**
- **P (point):** One strength of Milgram's study is ...

- **E (explain):** This matters because ...
- **L (link):** Therefore, the study ... / This links to Asch because ...

# Mark scheme (teacher only)

**Question:** Evaluate Milgram's study of obedience. (8 marks)

**Marking points**
- Two developed evaluation points, each made with a strength or limitation, a supporting detail from the study, and an explanation of why it matters. Up to 4 marks each.
- Credit for links to other research (e.g. Burger, 2009) and for explaining the effect on validity or ethics.
- Accurate use of the study's details (the 40 men, the prods, the 65% figure).

**Common answers that do not get the mark**
- "Milgram's study was unethical" with no link to the study's details or to validity.
- "The study is not valid because it was in a lab" with no explanation of why this matters.
- Listing points with no explanation.

**Model answer (paragraph)**
One strength of Milgram's study is that the procedure was standardised, so every participant received the same prods in the same order. For example, the experimenter always used one of four set phrases. This matters because it allows the study to be repeated, and it means differences in obedience can be attributed to the situation rather than to changes in the procedure. Therefore, the findings are more reliable. However, the study used only 40 men, so the findings may not generalise to women or to other groups of people.
