# Year 4 Electricity: six-week unit (one hour a week)

**Assumptions:** the class works in pairs or threes. Your kit has roughly eight of each item (cells, bulbs, wires, switches, buzzers), enough for every group to build a circuit. Adjust the group sizes if you have fewer. Cells must be used with the holders or as shown in your kit, and students should never short-circuit a cell with a bare wire across both ends.

## Overview

| Week | Lesson | Objective | Starter (retrieval) | Main activity | Check |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Conductors and insulators | Sort materials into conductors and insulators by testing them in a circuit | Week 1: "What must be in a complete circuit?" | Test paper, foil, coins, rubber, pencil lead, plastic | Predict, test, then record in a two-column table |
| 3 | Switches | Explain how a switch opens and closes a circuit | Week 2: "Name one conductor and one insulator." Week 1: "Why must the loop be complete?" | Build a switch into a circuit and test it | Exit ticket: "What does the switch do?" |

## Lesson detail

**Lesson 1: Making a bulb light.** Start with a challenge: "Can you make the bulb light using one cell and one wire?" Most groups will fail, and that is the point. Then show the complete loop. Students draw what they built. Teaching point: the electricity must travel around a complete loop from the cell and back.

**Lesson 2: Conductors and insulators.** Students predict first, then test each material by placing it in the gap in the circuit. Record in two columns. Teaching point: metals let electricity pass, and plastic, rubber and wood do not.

**Lesson 3: Switches.** Students add a switch to their loop. Teaching point: an open switch breaks the loop, and a closed switch completes it.

**Lesson 4: Series circuits.** Students compare one bulb and two bulbs in one loop. Ask them to predict brightness first. Teaching point: in a series loop, the bulbs share the electricity, so each one is dimmer, and if one breaks the loop, all the bulbs go off.

**Lesson 5: Buzzers and devices.** Students build a buzzer circuit and find out what makes it work. Then they sort everyday items into "uses electricity" and "does not." Teaching point: electrical devices need a complete loop and a power source.

**Lesson 6: Design a switch.** Students choose a simple model, such as a night light or doorbell. They build it, test it, then explain how the switch controls it. Use a checklist: the circuit is complete, the switch works, the model can be explained.

## How you will know what they have learned

- **Every lesson:** a two-minute exit ticket with one question. Keep the answers in a list so you can see who needs help next lesson.
- **Week 3 and Week 5:** a short retrieval starter that brings back earlier content, so you can see what has stuck.
- **Week 6 assessment:** a practical checklist. For each group, tick whether they: build a complete circuit from a diagram, make a switch that works, name at least one conductor and one insulator, and say in their own words what the switch does.
- **Individual check:** in Week 6, ask each child to draw one circuit and explain it to you. Keep a simple record (secure, developing, not yet).

## Key vocabulary

circuit, cell, bulb, wire, switch, conductor, insulator, open circuit, closed circuit, series, buzzer

## Safety note

Keep cells and bulbs in their holders. Do not connect a cell directly across its own terminals with a wire, because it can get hot.
