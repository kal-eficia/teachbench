# Year 9: Solving Equations with the Unknown on Both Sides

## Student practice sheet

**Before you start:** solve each equation, then check your answer by putting it back into both sides. Show every line of working.

### Part A: Fluency (solve each one)

Some of these change one thing at a time. Predict what will happen to x before you calculate it.

1. 3x + 2 = x + 10
2. 3x + 2 = x + 12
3. 3x + 2 = 2x + 12
4. 5x + 3 = 2x + 15
5. 5x + 3 = 2x + 9
6. 5x − 3 = 2x + 9
7. 5x + 3 = 2x − 6
8. 4x + 7 = 7x + 1
9. 2x + 9 = 7x − 6

**What do you notice?** Look at questions 1 to 3, then 4 and 5. What happened to x when the number on the right changed? What happened when the x-term on the right changed?

### Part B: Reasoning

10. Sara solves 3x + 4 = x + 10 like this:
    3x + x = 10 + 4
    4x = 14
    x = 3.5
    Find Sara's mistake and show the correct solution.

11. Decide whether each statement is always, sometimes or never true. Explain your choice in one sentence.
    a) 2x + 5 = 2x + 9 has a solution.
    b) 3x + 4 = 3x + 4 has a solution.
    c) Every equation of the form ax + b = cx + d has exactly one solution.

12. Four equations are shown. Three of them have the same solution. Which one is the odd one out? Solve all four to find out, then explain why it is different.
    A) 4x + 1 = x + 10
    B) 2x + 8 = 5x − 1
    C) 6x − 2 = 3x + 7
    D) 4x − 2 = 2x + 6

### Part C: Problem solving

13. A square has sides of length 2x + 1 cm. An equilateral triangle has sides of length x + 3 cm. The square and the triangle have the same perimeter. Find x, then find the perimeter.

14. Amir has £(4x + 5) and Bilal has £(x + 29). They have the same amount of money. Find x, then find how much each of them has.

15. Company A charges a fixed fee of £5 plus £3 per mile. Company B charges £20 plus £2 per mile. Let m be the number of miles. How many miles do both companies charge the same amount? How much is it?

### Challenge

16. The equation ax + 3 = 2x + 15 has the solution x = 4. Find the value of a. Check your answer by substituting both x and a into the equation.

## Teacher key

| Q | Answer | What it tests or varies | Common error to listen for |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | x = 4 | Baseline: unknowns on both sides, positive values | Moving the smaller x-term to the wrong side |
| 2 | x = 5 | Constant on the right goes up by 2, so x goes up by 1 | Adding the constant on the wrong side |
| 3 | x = 10 | The x-term on the right goes from 1x to 2x: the gap is now 1x | Leaving the bigger x-term on the wrong side |
| 4 | x = 4 | Bigger gap between the x-terms (3x) | Sign errors when moving terms |
| 5 | x = 2 | Only the constant on the right changes | Forgetting to subtract the constant on both sides |
| 6 | x = 4 | Sign of the constant on the left changes (−3) | Keeping +3 when moving across or misreading the sign |
| 7 | x = −3 | Negative constant on the right gives a negative answer | Losing the negative sign when dividing |
| 8 | x = 2 | The bigger x-term is on the right: move 4x across, not 7x | Sign slip when subtracting: writing 4x − 7x = 1 − 7 as −3x = 6, giving x = −2 |
| 9 | x = 3 | Same as 8 with a negative constant on the right | Errors subtracting −6 |

**Part B answers**

10. Sara's error: when she moves x across, she adds it. It should be 3x − x = 10 − 4, so 2x = 6 and x = 3. Check: 3(3) + 4 = 13 and 3 + 10 = 13 ✓.
11. a) Never (5 cannot equal 9). b) Always (both sides are the same, so every value of x works). c) Sometimes (it fails when the x-terms are equal: it has no solution or infinitely many).
12. A) x = 3. B) x = 3. C) x = 3. D) x = 4. The odd one out is D, because it's the only one with x = 4. The other three share x = 3.

**Part C answers**

13. 4(2x + 1) = 3(x + 3), so 8x + 4 = 3x + 9, so 5x = 5 and x = 1. Square side 3 cm, perimeter 12 cm. Triangle side 4 cm, perimeter 12 cm.
14. 4x + 5 = x + 29, so 3x = 24 and x = 8. Each has £37.
15. 5 + 3m = 20 + 2m, so m = 15. Both charge £50.

**Challenge answer**

16. Substitute x = 4: 4a + 3 = 8 + 15, so 4a = 20 and a = 5. Check: 5(4) + 3 = 23 and 2(4) + 15 = 23 ✓.

## Teacher notes

- **Spot the structure discussion (after Part A):** the key idea is that changing one number changes x in a predictable way. Ask students to say why, using the balance model.
- **Support:** give a balance diagram for Q1 to Q5. Ask students to write each step on a new line.
- **Stretch:** Q16 is the first step into "solve for a parameter", which is a useful bridge to Year 10.
