# Questions to ask: finding the nth term of a linear sequence (Year 8)

Replace "does everyone understand?" with questions that every student answers, not just the three who always do. Use these routines throughout:

- **Ask, pause, name:** ask the question, count to five silently, then name a student from the class list. Don't take hands.
- **Show me:** everyone writes an answer on a mini whiteboard and holds it up at the same moment.
- **Talk, then pick a pair:** students discuss with a partner for 30 seconds, then you pick a pair to report.
- **Keep a tally:** a quick tick on a seating plan each time a student answers. Check it at the midpoint. Ask the quiet students a question first.
- **Wrong answers are data:** thank the student, then ask another whether they agree and why.

Use the sequence **4, 7, 10, 13, 16** and **3, 7, 11, 15** in the questions below.

## Starter (retrieval, about 5 minutes)

**Q1:** "What is the difference between each term in 3, 7, 11, 15?"
- **Purpose:** recall the difference from last lesson.
- **Who to ask:** a student who usually stays quiet (ask with a name, after the pause).
- **Expected answer:** 4.
- **Likely wrong answer:** 3 (taking the first term rather than the gap between terms).
- **Follow-up:** "Check 7 minus 3, then 15 minus 11. Do they match?"

**Q2 (show me):** "What is 6 times 4, and what is 6 times 5?"
- **Purpose:** warm up multiplication used in nth-term work.
- **Expected answer:** 24 and 30.
- **Likely wrong answer:** 22 or 26 (adding 6 instead of multiplying).
- **Follow-up:** "Where did 24 come from? Show me the multiplication."

## Introduce the idea (about 8 minutes)

**Q3 (cold call):** "The sequence goes 3, 7, 11, 15. If you multiply the position by 4, you get 4, 8, 12, 16. How is each term different from those?"
- **Purpose:** show that the multiplier comes from the difference, but the starting value still needs fixing.
- **Who to ask:** a student seated near the back, named after the pause.
- **Expected answer:** each term is one less than the multiple of 4 (3 is 4 minus 1, 7 is 8 minus 1, and so on).
- **Likely wrong answer:** "they're all 4 bigger" (that's only true for the first term, not for all of them).
- **Follow-up:** "What number do you subtract to make it work? Test it with position 1."

**Q4 (probing):** "If I say the rule is 4n, what does position 1 give? Is that the first term?"
- **Purpose:** exposes the constant term.
- **Expected answer:** 4 is not 3, so the rule needs to take off 1: 4n minus 1.
- **Likely wrong answer:** "it's fine, it's close."
- **Follow-up:** "Test it with position 2. Does 4n minus 1 give 7?"

## Hinge question (about 3 minutes, the turning point)

**Q5 (show me on whiteboards):** "The sequence is 4, 7, 10, 13. Which is the nth term?"
- A: 4n
- B: n + 3
- C: 3n + 1
- D: 3n + 4

**What each option reveals:**
- **A (4n):** uses the first term as the multiplier. Reteach: the multiplier is the difference between terms.
- **B (n + 3):** uses the position and ignores the difference. Reteach: check the gap between terms.
- **C (3n + 1):** correct. Check: position 1 gives 4, position 2 gives 7.
- **D (3n + 4):** adds the first term as a constant instead of working it out. Reteach: test position 1 with the rule.

**What to do next:**
- Most students correct: move on to the guided task.
- Most students choose A or B: go back to the difference and do one more example together.
- Most students choose D: test the rule with position 1 on the board, then ask the class to explain the mistake.
- Split: talk, then show again.

## Guided task (about 10 minutes)

**Q6 (probing):** "The sequence is 5, 8, 11, 14. Who thinks the nth term is 3n + 2? Who thinks it's 3n + 5? Convince your partner."
- **Who to ask:** pick a pair, then ask one student from each answer to explain.
- **Expected answer:** 3n + 2.
- **Likely wrong answer:** 3n + 5 (test with position 1: 3 + 5 = 8, not 5).
- **Follow-up:** "How do you know? Test it with position 1."

## Independent practice (about 10 minutes)

**Q7:** "Find the nth term of 2, 5, 8, 11, and test it with position 4."
- **Expected answer:** 3n − 1; position 4 gives 11.
- **Likely wrong answer:** 3n + 2 (the constant is wrong).
- **Follow-up:** "What does position 1 give? Is that the first term?"

## Stretch (for students who finish early)

**Q8:** "Sequence A is 5, 8, 11, 14. Sequence B is 10, 13, 16, 19. Without working out both nth terms, how do they compare?"
- **Expected answer:** each term in B is 5 more than the matching term in A, so B's nth term is A's nth term plus 5.
- **Follow-up:** "Why does changing the starting value shift the whole sequence?"

## Exit check (about 2 minutes, show me)

**Q9:** "Write the nth term of 2, 6, 10, 14 on your whiteboard."
- **Expected answer:** 4n − 2.
- **Use it to decide:** anyone who gets it wrong gets one more question in the next lesson's starter.
