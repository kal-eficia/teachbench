## Teacher key

| Who or what? | When? | Key idea or change | Why did it matter? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Galen and the four humours | Galen's ideas dominated for centuries, into the medieval period | The body contains four humours (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile). Illness is an imbalance between them. | Doctors trusted ancient texts. Later thinkers, especially Vesalius and Harvey, challenged Galen's ideas. |
| The Black Death | 1348 to 1349 in England | A plague that killed a large share of the population. There was no effective cure. | Medieval explanations were religious or based on miasma (bad air). Shows the limits of medieval medicine. |
| Vesalius | 1543 | *De humani corporis fabrica*, based on his own dissections. Corrected errors in Galen's work. | Shifted medicine from relying on ancient texts to direct observation of the body. |
| Paré | c.1545 | Stopped treating gunshot wounds with boiling oil. Used ligatures to tie blood vessels. | Showed that a gentler, more careful method could work better, and that practice could challenge old treatments. |
| Harvey | 1628 | Blood circulates around the body, pumped by the heart (*De Motu Cordis*). | Challenged Galen's idea that blood ebbed and flowed from the liver. Accepted by many as a major step forward. |
| The Great Plague | 1665 (London) | Public health measures, including shutting up infected houses with a red cross and the words "Lord have mercy upon us" on the door. | Shows that people tried to control the plague, but the cause (fleas and bacteria) was still not understood. |
| The Norman Conquest | 1066 | William of Normandy defeated Harold at Hastings. | Changed who ruled England. Reminder of an older topic that needs spacing. |
