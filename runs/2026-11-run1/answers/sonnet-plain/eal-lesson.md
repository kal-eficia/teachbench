# Including a Year 9 student with early-stage English in your photosynthesis unit

**Short version:** she already knows science and she can already think scientifically. What she lacks right now is English, not understanding. Plan so she can show what she knows without needing much English, give her a way to connect new English words to ideas she already holds, and build her English through the topic.

**Assumptions:** you do not speak Dari or Pashto, she can read and write in at least one of them, and she is fairly new to the Latin alphabet. Check this quietly with her or with any records from admissions. If she has had limited schooling or interrupted literacy, adjust accordingly.

## 1. Find out what she already knows (before the lesson)
- Photosynthesis is likely a topic she has met. Afghan school science typically covers plants, light, and basic chemistry, and chemical formulas (CO2, H2O, O2) are written in Latin letters worldwide, so these are familiar anchors.
- Give her a quick, low-language probe in the lesson before: a simple labelled picture of a plant, sun, water, air, with arrows, and ask her to point to or label what she can in Dari or Pashto. Allow a bilingual dictionary or translation app.
- Ask a friendly bilingual colleague, TA, or older student (if one exists) to check what she says. If you have access to your school's EAL team or local authority EAL service, ask for support and for any translated glossaries.

## 2. Use her languages as a strength
- Let her write notes, labels and sketches in Dari or Pashto first, then add the English word beside them. This keeps her thinking at Year 9 level.
- Use a translation tool for key terms, but check them. Machine translation can be wrong for scientific words, and Dari and Pashto sometimes use different terms or borrow different words. If possible, have a Dari or Pashto speaker confirm the main vocabulary list once, then reuse it all term.
- Many science terms (oxygen, carbon dioxide, glucose, chlorophyll) have international roots and may be recognisable by sound. Point this out as a bridge.
- Do not insist she speaks English in front of the class. Let her speak when ready. Comprehension comes before speaking.

## 3. Reduce the language load without reducing the thinking
**Before the lesson (prepare once):**
- A one-page **key vocabulary sheet** with: English word, picture, her-language translation, simple definition. Limit to about 10 words: photosynthesis, chlorophyll, light, carbon dioxide, water, glucose, oxygen, leaf, stomata, energy.
- The **word equation** as a visual, with icons under each word:
  carbon dioxide + water → (light, chlorophyll) → glucose + oxygen
- The **symbol equation** (6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2). She may recognise this already, and it gives her a way to show she knows the science.
- A **labelled diagram** of a leaf showing inputs and outputs.

**During the lesson:**
- Pair her with a patient, reliable classmate (a buddy who explains with gestures and pictures, not just words). If another student shares her language, seat them together for the main tasks, with a clear purpose, and rotate her partners sometimes so she hears English too.
- Say the key sentence with a gesture or picture each time, and keep your sentences short. Write key words on the board as you say them.
- Give her **sentence frames** for tasks:
  - "Plants need ______ and ______ for photosynthesis."
  - "Photosynthesis makes ______ and ______."
  - "The leaf takes in ______ from the air."
- Provide the same tasks as everyone else, but scaffolded: for example, a card-sort matching picture, word and her-language label; labelling the diagram; completing the word equation from a word bank; sequencing the steps with pictures.
- Show the practical (e.g. pondweed bubbling or testing a leaf for starch) clearly. Practical work is a great equaliser: she can observe, record, and show understanding with little English.

**Questioning:**
- Use closed or choice questions at first ("Does the plant take in carbon dioxide or oxygen?"), then moving to short phrases, then sentences.
- Allow thinking time, allow her to answer on a mini whiteboard or by pointing.

## 4. Assess what she knows, not her English
- Let her demonstrate understanding through labelled diagrams, flow charts, matching tasks, drawing and explaining, or a short answer written first in Dari or Pashto.
- For any written end-of-unit check, give a modified version: diagram labelling, sequencing, matching, and a word bank for short answers. Gradually remove supports as her English grows.
- Track progress in both science understanding and English use. Celebrate when she uses a new word correctly.

## 5. Support after the lesson
- Give a simple **recap sheet** with the diagram, word equation and glossary for use at home. If she has a family member who can read in Dari or Pashto, include the translated terms.
- Offer a short, manageable homework: label a diagram, or find three plants at home and draw what they need. Make sure homework does not depend on resources she cannot access.
- If your school has an EAL coordinator, share the glossary and diagram so other subjects can build on it.

## 6. Wellbeing and relationships
- She may have experienced disruption or trauma. You do not need to ask about it, but keep a calm, predictable routine and avoid putting her on the spot.
- Learn how to pronounce her name correctly and use it. Greet her warmly each lesson.
- Check in privately now and then. A buddy for breaks and clubs matters as much as the lesson adaptations.
- Remember that a silent period is normal in the early stages of learning a new language and does not mean lack of understanding.

## 7. Quick lesson outline (example, one hour)
| Time | Activity | Support |
|---|---|---|
| 0-10 | Starter: look at a plant picture, predict what it needs | Pictures, buddy, her-language labels |
| 10-25 | Teacher explanation with diagram and word equation | Printed copy of diagram, glossary, gestures |
| 25-40 | Practical or demo, record observations | Observation table with pictures and word bank |
| 40-55 | Sequence/match/label task, then short written explanation | Sentence frames, bilingual dictionary |
| 55-60 | Plenary: one fact each on a whiteboard | She can draw or write in her language, plus English word |

## What success looks like
By the end of the unit she should be able to point to or label the inputs and outputs of photosynthesis in English, use at least the 10 key words in a sentence or phrase with support, and show you she understands the process in whatever way she can. Her English will grow quickly when the content is familiar and the support is consistent.
