# AQA GCSE Combined Science (Biology), Foundation Tier: Enzymes

**Total: 8 marks**

*Assumptions: the question uses the AQA command words "State", "Explain" and "Suggest" at foundation level. The first part is scaffolded so that students build up to the 4-mark "explain" answer.*

---

## Question

A student investigated the effect of temperature on the enzyme **amylase**. Amylase breaks down starch.

The student mixed amylase solution with starch solution in test tubes at different temperatures. She measured the time taken for all the starch to be broken down.

**Table 1**

| Temperature in °C | Time taken for all the starch to be broken down in seconds |
|---|---|
| 20 | 200 |
| 30 | 110 |
| 40 | 50 |
| 50 | 150 |
| 60 | Starch was not broken down after 300 seconds |

**(a)** Enzymes are made of a type of molecule.
What type of molecule are enzymes made of? Tick **one** box.

☐ Carbohydrate   ☐ Fat   ☐ Protein   ☐ Sugar

**[1 mark]**

**(b)** At which temperature was amylase most effective?
Use Table 1.

Temperature = ______ °C

**[1 mark]**

**(c)** The reaction was slower at 20 °C than at 40 °C.
Explain why.

**[2 marks]**

**(d)** No starch was broken down at 60 °C.
Explain why.

*Think about what happens to the enzyme, what that changes, and what the effect is on the starch.*

**[4 marks]**

---

## Mark scheme

**(a)** Protein **[1]**

**(b)** 40 (°C) **[1]**

**(c)** *(2 marks)*
- At 20 °C the particles (enzyme and starch/substrate) move more slowly **[1]**
- so there are fewer collisions between the enzyme and the starch (per second) / fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form **[1]**

Accept: less energy / less kinetic energy for "move more slowly".
Do not accept: "the enzyme is not working" or "the enzyme is dead/inactive" without reference to collisions or movement. Do not accept "the enzyme is denatured" at 20 °C.

**(d)** *(4 marks, one mark for each point)*
1. 60 °C is too high / the temperature is above the optimum **[1]**
2. The enzyme (amylase) is denatured **[1]**
3. The shape of the active site changes **[1]**
4. The starch (substrate) no longer fits the active site / enzyme-substrate complex cannot form, so the starch is not broken down **[1]**

Allow: "enzyme loses its shape" or "enzyme changes shape" for point 2 or 3 only if it is clear the active site is affected for point 3.
Do not accept: "the enzyme is killed / dies / melts / burns". These are not credited.
Do not accept: the enzyme "stops working" as a standalone answer for point 2.

---

## Notes for teachers (helping students with "explain")

**Why students lose marks on "explain":** they describe (what happened) but do not give a reason (why). Marks for explain questions come from the link between a cause and an effect.

**A scaffold to teach:** *Point, because, so.*
- Point: "At 60 °C the enzyme is denatured..."
- Because: "...because the high temperature changes the shape of the active site..."
- So: "...so the starch no longer fits and cannot be broken down."

**Typical errors to address**
- "The enzyme dies": enzymes are not alive; they are denatured.
- "The enzyme melts or burns": the shape of the active site changes.
- Saying it "stops working" without saying why.
- Using "denatured" in (c). It does not apply at low temperatures; the enzyme is not damaged, there are just fewer collisions.

**Quick classroom use**
1. Give students part (d) cold and have them mark each other's answers with the four points.
2. Then give them the sentence frame above and let them rewrite.
3. Ask them to write their own "explain" question about pH and mark it with the same approach (a different change in conditions, the same reasoning).

**Extension for confident students:** add a 2-mark question: "Suggest why the student would be more confident in her conclusion if she repeated the experiment at each temperature." *(Repeat to identify anomalies / calculate a mean, so results are more reliable.)*
