# Adapting the Civil War extended answer for three students

**Assumptions:** the three students are in your mainstream Year 8 class, none has been described as having an EHCP with specific arrangements (if they do, follow those first), you have not been given the three sources so I have not adapted their content, and you want to keep the same historical challenge (a reasoned judgement) for all three students. I have set out whole-class changes first, because most of these help every student and mean nobody is singled out, then specific adjustments for each student, and finally a ready-to-use version of the task.

## The key principle

Keep the thinking the same (weigh evidence, reach a judgement), and change how the student gets into the task and how they show it. The history is the challenge. Reading load, open-endedness and working memory should not be.

## Before the lesson (about 15 minutes of prep)

1. **Send the task and sources to the autistic student (and, if appropriate, the other two) the day before**, with a note on what the lesson will look like. Advance notice of an open-ended task reduces anxiety more than almost anything else.
2. **Make the sources easier to read, not easier to understand:**
   - Print on cream or pastel paper, at least size 12 to 14 sans-serif font, with generous line spacing, left-aligned (not justified).
   - Break long sources into short paragraphs, number the lines, and define two or three hard words in the margin (for example "Ship Money: a tax on coastal towns", "Parliament", "divine right").
   - If possible, record the three sources as audio, or read them aloud to the class at the start.
3. **Prepare a planning frame and a word bank** (see below) for everyone.
4. **Prepare a model** of a PEEL paragraph on a different topic (for example "Henry VIII was mainly to blame for the break with Rome"), so the structure is visible.

## Whole-class changes (so nobody is singled out)

- **Split the 30 minutes into visible steps with times on the board:**
  - 5 min: read and highlight the sources (or listen, then read)
  - 5 min: fill in the planning frame
  - 15 min: write
  - 5 min: check against the checklist
- **Give a planning frame to everyone** (a simple table: Reason / Evidence from source or own knowledge / Was Charles to blame, yes or partly or no).
- **Give a sentence-starter bank** on the board or a card, for PEEL and for the judgement.
- **Say what "how far do you agree" means in plain language:** "You can agree, disagree, or agree partly. Whichever you choose, explain why using evidence. There is no single correct answer, but your answer must be backed up."
- **State what a good answer looks like**, as a short checklist: 3 or 4 paragraphs, each uses a source or own knowledge, includes at least one paragraph on something other than Charles (for example Parliament or religion), and ends with a judgement.
- **Allow different ways of showing the work** (see below), so the written format is a choice, not a barrier.

## Student with dyslexia

**The barriers:** reading three sources, getting thoughts onto paper quickly, spelling and handwriting, and working memory during writing.

**Adjustments:**
- **Reading:** audio of the sources, or read aloud by a partner, teaching assistant or you at the start. Pre-highlight key sentences in each source, or give the student a short "what to look for" prompt per source.
- **Planning:** use the planning frame, with bullet points rather than full sentences. Let them plan on a laptop, with voice notes, or with a mind map.
- **Writing:** laptop with spellcheck or speech-to-text, or a scribe or reader if this is already their arrangement. Their notes can be turned into paragraphs without penalty for spelling.
- **Time:** extra time (an extra 5 to 10 minutes, or the task finished at home or in a later lesson) if that is within the school's arrangements.
- **Marking:** mark the history (evidence, reasoning, judgement), not spelling, punctuation or handwriting. Say this to the student before they start.
- **Support structures:** a word bank with key terms already spelt correctly (Parliament, Ship Money, Catholic, Puritan, religion, taxation, divine right), visible on the board or on a card on their desk.

## Student with ADHD

**The barriers:** starting, sustaining attention for 30 minutes, holding the question in mind, and finishing.

**Adjustments:**
- **Make the first step tiny and immediate:** "Read Source A and underline one thing Charles did." Starting is often the hardest part, so give them a clear first action.
- **Chunk the task** with the visible timings above. A visual timer on the desk or board is useful. Give a tick-box checklist to complete each stage.
- **Check in briefly at each stage** (a thumbs up, a quick look at the plan) rather than waiting until the end.
- **Movement and fidgets:** allow a quick job or short break between stages (handing out papers, a short walk), and use of a fidget if it helps.
- **Seating:** near you, away from doors and windows, and away from students who are likely to distract.
- **Reduce visual clutter:** one source on the desk at a time, with the question on top, in a larger font.
- **Offer a choice about order:** they may write the judgement paragraph first if that gets them started, then fill in the others.
- **Praise specific behaviour:** "You've got your plan done in the time, well done" is more useful than general praise.

## Autistic student who gets anxious about open-ended tasks

**The barriers:** ambiguity ("how far do you agree"), "your own knowledge" (what counts?), an unclear end point, unexpected change, and not knowing what "good" looks like.

**Adjustments:**
- **Remove ambiguity:** give a concrete structure, such as "Write 4 paragraphs: 1 on Charles, 1 on Parliament, 1 on religion, 1 conclusion." Explain what "own knowledge" means: "anything we have learnt in lessons, from our notes or the textbook".
- **Define "how far":** "Choose one: Charles was mostly to blame, partly to blame, or not mainly to blame. Then give reasons."
- **Provide a finished example** (the Henry VIII model) so they can see what is expected, and a clear checklist for completion. "You are finished when you have done every item on this list."
- **Advance notice** (as above), including a quiet start and the timings. If possible, they can see the question and the planning frame the day before.
- **Offer a choice that reduces uncertainty:** for example, "you can use the sentence starters or write in your own words".
- **Allow a calm option:** a quiet seat, headphones if they help, and a pre-agreed way to signal they need a moment (a card on the desk), so they can step out of difficulty without having to explain it.
- **Keep the language literal and avoid idioms.** For instance, say "give your opinion and back it up" rather than "have your say".
- **Do not remove the judgement.** Reaching a view is part of the learning, so make it safe: "There's no wrong choice as long as you explain."

## Ready-to-use adapted task (for the whole class, with optional support)

> **Question:** "Charles I was mainly to blame for the outbreak of the Civil War." How far do you agree?
>
> **What you need:** Sources A, B and C (pages 42 to 44), your notes, and this sheet.
>
> **Your time (30 minutes):**
> 1. 5 min: Read the sources and highlight one useful point in each. (Audio available.)
> 2. 5 min: Fill in the planning table.
> 3. 15 min: Write your answer. Aim for 3 or 4 paragraphs.
> 4. 5 min: Check your work against the list at the bottom.
>
> **How far do you agree? This means:** choose a view (agree, partly agree or disagree) and explain why, using evidence. There is no single correct view.
>
> **Planning table:**
>
> | Reason | Evidence (source or my knowledge) | Is it Charles's fault? (yes / partly / no) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Charles's actions | | |
> | Parliament | | |
> | Religion | | |
>
> **PEEL reminder:**
> - **P**oint: "One reason for the Civil War was..."
> - **E**vidence: "Source A shows that..." or "I know that..."
> - **E**xplain: "This matters because..."
> - **L**ink: "This shows that Charles was / was not mainly to blame because..."
>
> **Finish with a judgement:** "Overall, I agree / partly agree / disagree because..."
>
> **Check your work:**
> - [ ] I used at least two sources
> - [ ] I used something from my own knowledge
> - [ ] I looked at more than just Charles
> - [ ] I gave a judgement at the end
> - [ ] I explained my judgement
>
> **Stuck?** Put a card on your desk or ask for a hint. Spelling and handwriting are not marked.

## During the lesson

- Start with the whole class by reading the question aloud, explaining "how far" in plain terms, and going through the timings and the model paragraph. Spend 3 to 4 minutes on this.
- Circulate to the three students first after the planning stage, because the plan is the point where things most often go wrong.
- If the autistic student is still anxious after five minutes, offer a smaller start ("write just the first sentence of the first paragraph") instead of insisting on the whole plan.

## After the lesson

- Give feedback on the history first (what evidence they used, how they weighed it), not on presentation.
- Ask each student what helped. Their answers will tell you what to keep for the next task, and it will make them more confident about asking for support.
- Check with your SENCo about any formal arrangements, and share what worked.
