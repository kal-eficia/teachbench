# Year 5 geography retrieval check: rivers and the water cycle

## Assumptions
- A 20 to 25 minute check, done independently on paper in silence, three weeks after the unit. That gap is deliberately long, so it shows what has stuck.
- The unit covered the water cycle, river features from source to mouth, and how rivers shape the land. Adjust if you taught specific rivers such as the Thames or Nile; there is a spare question for that at the end.
- 20 marks, with a mix of recall, short answers and a diagram.

## Pupil sheet

**Name: ______________   Date: ___________**

### Section A: Quick questions (1 mark each)

1. What is the name of the place where a river begins?
2. What is the name of the place where a river meets the sea?
3. What do we call a smaller river that joins a bigger one?
4. Which word means water turning from a liquid into a gas (water vapour)?
5. Which word means water vapour turning back into tiny drops of liquid to form clouds?
6. Circle the correct word. Rain, snow and hail are all types of: **precipitation  /  evaporation  /  erosion**
7. True or false? Rivers flow uphill from the sea to the source.

### Section B: Short answers

8. (2 marks) Put these in order, from the start of a river to the end. Write 1 to 4.
   - mouth ___
   - source ___
   - middle section (where it gets wider) ___
   - river flows downhill through the valley ___

9. (2 marks) Explain what happens to water in the sea when the sun shines on it.

10. (2 marks) Explain why it often rains on the side of a mountain.

11. (2 marks) What is erosion? Give one example of erosion that a river can cause.

12. (1 mark) Name one way people use rivers.

### Section C: Diagram

13. (3 marks) Here is a blank water cycle drawing. Add these labels in the right places: **evaporation, condensation, precipitation**. (Draw a sun, sea, cloud, mountain and arrows for the pupils to label.)

### Section D: Think about it

14. (3 marks) A river is very low in the summer after weeks without rain. Explain why this might happen, using the words **evaporation**, **precipitation** and **flow**.

**Total: 20 marks**

---

## Teacher answers and marking

**Section A (7 marks)**
1. Source.
2. Mouth.
3. Tributary.
4. Evaporation.
5. Condensation.
6. Precipitation.
7. False.

**Section B (9 marks)**
8. 1 mark for source first and mouth last. 2 marks for fully correct: source = 1, flows downhill through the valley = 2, middle section = 3, mouth = 4. (If your unit used upper, middle and lower courses, accept that order.)
9. 1 mark: the sun heats the water. 1 mark: it evaporates, turning into water vapour (a gas) that rises.
10. 1 mark: moist air is pushed upwards by the mountain. 1 mark: it cools and condenses into clouds, which then produce rain (relief rainfall). Accept "air rises, cools, and makes clouds".
11. 1 mark: wearing away of rock and soil. 1 mark: a valid example, for example the river cuts a V-shaped valley, wears away the banks, or carries away pebbles and soil.
12. Any one: drinking water, travel and transport, fishing, farming or irrigation, hydroelectric power, leisure, washing. (1 mark)

**Section C (3 marks)**
13. 1 mark for each correct label: evaporation (arrow rising from sea or river), condensation (at or near the cloud), precipitation (rain or snow falling from the cloud).

**Section D (3 marks)**
14. 1 mark: little or no precipitation means less water enters the river. 1 mark: the warm weather causes evaporation from the river, lakes and soil. 1 mark: so the amount of water that flows downstream is lower. Award marks for a clear, linked explanation even if the wording is different.

## Using the results
- Mark quickly and record each question as right, partly right or wrong for each child. Look at the class pattern before individuals.
- Typical gaps to expect after three weeks: mixing up evaporation and condensation, forgetting "tributary", and describing precipitation as only "rain".
- Retake the weakest items as a low-stakes quiz in a week, and give feedback so that pupils see what they gained.
- For pupils who need support, read the questions aloud and allow the diagram to be completed from a word bank.
- If you taught particular rivers or vocabulary (meander, floodplain, delta, confluence), add 3 to 5 questions on those.
