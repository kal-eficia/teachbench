# Why do we have seasons?

*For Year 7 (ages 11 to 12). Includes a script you can read or adapt, a demonstration that needs only a torch and a ball, and a few questions to check the misconception has gone. Assumptions: about 20 to 25 minutes; the class knows the Earth orbits the Sun once a year and spins once a day.*

## The idea in one sentence

**The Earth is tilted. As it travels round the Sun, the tilt means that for part of the year our half of the Earth leans towards the Sun (summer) and for part of the year it leans away (winter).**

It is *not* about how far we are from the Sun.

---

## Start by testing the "closer to the Sun" idea

Ask: **"If summer happens because the Earth is closer to the Sun, what should be true in Australia when it's summer in the UK?"**

Give them a minute. Then reveal:

- When it's summer in the UK, it's **winter** in Australia. Both countries are on the same planet, so they're roughly the same distance from the Sun. Distance can't be the reason.
- The Earth is actually **closest to the Sun in early January**, in the middle of the UK's winter. It's about 147 million km away then, and about 152 million km away in early July. That's a difference of only about 3%, which is too small to matter much.

So the "closer in summer" idea is a very sensible guess, but the evidence shows it's wrong.

---

## The real explanation (script)

**1. The Earth is tilted.**
The Earth spins on an imaginary line called its **axis**, running from the North Pole to the South Pole. This axis is not upright. It is tilted at about **23.5 degrees**.

**2. The tilt always points the same way.**
As the Earth orbits the Sun, the axis keeps pointing towards the same spot in space (close to the North Star). It doesn't wobble to face the Sun as we travel round.

**3. So the tilt sometimes leans us towards the Sun, and sometimes away.**
- In **June**, the northern half of the Earth (the Northern Hemisphere, where the UK is) is tilted **towards** the Sun. It's summer for us. At the same time, the Southern Hemisphere is tilted away, so it's winter in Australia.
- In **December**, it's the other way round. The north is tilted **away** from the Sun, so it's winter in the UK and summer in Australia.
- In spring and autumn, neither half leans strongly towards or away from the Sun.

**4. Why does leaning towards the Sun make it warmer? Two reasons.**

**(a) Sunlight hits at a steeper angle.**
In summer the Sun is **higher in the sky**. The sunlight shines more directly down, so it is **concentrated onto a smaller area** and heats the ground more strongly. In winter the Sun is low, and the same amount of light is **spread over a bigger area**, so each patch gets less heat.

**(b) The days are longer.**
In summer the Sun is above the horizon for longer, so the ground and air have more time to heat up. In winter the days are short, and the long nights give the land time to cool.

## Demonstration (5 minutes, no computers)

**You need:** a torch (or your phone torch) and a ball, globe or orange. Draw a "UK" dot on it with a marker.

1. Darken the room a little. Hold the torch at the same height and distance for each step.
2. Tilt the ball so the dot leans **towards** the torch. Shine the torch. Ask students to describe the spot: it should be **small and bright** (concentrated).
3. Tilt the ball so the dot leans **away**. The spot should look **larger and dimmer** (spread out).
4. Walk the ball in a circle round the torch, **keeping the tilt pointing the same way** (a student can hold a pencil through the ball as the axis to help). Pause at the "summer" and "winter" positions.

Common mistake to look out for: students tilt the ball towards the torch the whole way round. Remind them the axis stays pointing in the same direction.

---

## Quick check (mini whiteboards or hands)

Ask these, and look for anyone still writing "closer to the Sun".

1. True or false: *The Earth is closest to the Sun during the UK summer.* (**False**: it is closest in January.)
2. It's July in the UK. What season is it in Australia? (**Winter**)
3. What causes the seasons? (**The tilt of the Earth's axis**, not the distance from the Sun.)
4. Give **two** reasons summer is warmer than winter in the UK. (**Sun higher in the sky, so more concentrated heat; longer days.**)
5. Extension: What do you think the seasons would be like if the Earth's axis was **not tilted at all**? (**There would be no seasons**, because each place would get roughly the same amount of sunlight all year.)

## Word bank to display

axis, tilt, orbit, hemisphere, summer, winter, direct, concentrated, spread out, daylight hours

## One-line summary for their books

*We have seasons because the Earth's axis is tilted by about 23.5 degrees, so throughout the year each hemisphere leans towards or away from the Sun, changing the angle of the sunlight and the length of the day.*
