# Year 11 foundation maths mock: where to focus in 10 lessons

## Assumptions
- Each question is worth 2 marks (56 available across 28 students) or 3 marks (84 available).
- Your figures are the only data I have, so this covers eight questions, not the whole paper. If other questions lost marks, add them to the table.
- "Marks lost" means all marks lost, including students who started the question and dropped one mark.

Four topics (17, 19, 21, 9) account for about 70% of the marks lost. That is where the gain is.

## Where I would spend the 10 lessons

Rank by marks lost, then by how quickly the topic can be fixed and how often it is examined. Foundation papers reward method marks, so partial credit is available even to students who will not get the final answer.

**Priority 1: Ratio (Q9) and Pythagoras (Q21), 5 lessons in total**
- Both are high-frequency, method-driven topics, and both lost around 70%. They are the quickest wins.
- Ratio: "total parts, one part, multiply" as a fixed routine, with bar models for sharing. Then ratio given one amount, and problem-style contexts.
- Pythagoras: label the hypotenuse first, then decide whether you add or subtract the squares. Most errors are using the wrong operation, so include "is my answer sensible?" checks (hypotenuse must be the longest side).

**Priority 2: Compound interest (Q19) and probability trees (Q17), 3 lessons in total**
- These lost the most (79% and 83%) but are probably the hardest and sit towards the end of the paper, so some students may simply not have reached them. Check the scripts for blanks versus wrong attempts before you commit lesson time.
- Compound interest: teach the multiplier method (for example 1.03 to the power of 3) as one repeatable procedure, with the calculator button sequence rehearsed. Simple percentage increase and repeated percentage change should come first.
- Probability tree: aim for the marks that are easy to get. Completing branches that sum to 1 and multiplying along branches is attainable for most students even if the "and/or" final step is not.

**Priority 3: Compound area (Q7), 1 lesson**
- About half the marks were lost. Teach "split into rectangles, find missing lengths, add". The missing-length step is usually where marks go.

**Priority 4: Mixed retrieval and exam technique, 1 lesson**
- Cover linear equations (Q12), pie charts (Q14) and fractions of amounts (Q3) with short starter retrieval across the other lessons rather than whole lessons. They lost 36%, 39% and 21%, so most students already have them.
- Spend the final lesson on a short mixed paper, with a focus on attempting every question and showing method.

## Suggested sequence
1. Ratio: sharing in a ratio, bar models
2. Ratio: given one part or difference, mixed problems
3. Pythagoras: finding the hypotenuse
4. Pythagoras: finding a shorter side, deciding which
5. Pythagoras and ratio in context, exam-style practice
6. Compound interest: percentage multipliers, then repeated change
7. Probability trees: completing and reading trees, then calculating
8. Compound shape area, with missing lengths
9. Mixed retrieval: linear equations, pie charts, fractions of amounts
10. Timed mixed paper and feedback

Add 5 minutes of retrieval from the weaker earlier topics to every lesson start (spaced practice beats one block).

## Before you commit
- Check blanks versus wrong answers on Q17 and Q19. If many are blank, the issue is time or confidence, and the fix is "get the easy marks" teaching, not new content.
- If the class is mixed in attainment, group by which topic each student lost most marks on and run short targeted sessions. A whole-class lesson on trees is wasted on a student who scored full marks on Q17.
- For a student on the grade 3/4 boundary, ratio, Pythagoras and area are the safer bets, so do not abandon them to chase the harder topics.
