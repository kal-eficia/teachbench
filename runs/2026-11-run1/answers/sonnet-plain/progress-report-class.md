## Summary

Every topic has improved compared with baseline, and the average has gone from about 36% to about 59%. Cells (72%) and Infection and response (65%) show the strongest progress, with gains of over 30 points each.

**Organisation (58%)** has improved by 18 points but is still below the other two, so it needs consolidating.

**Bioenergetics (41%)** is the main concern. It has the lowest score and the smallest improvement (+11), and it is the only topic still below 50%. The topic includes photosynthesis, respiration and the associated required practicals, which have a heavy mathematical and conceptual load.

One caution: a class average can hide a spread, so I will look at the individual results to see whether a few students are pulling it down or whether it is a class-wide gap.

## Next steps

1. **Bioenergetics (priority).** Re-teach the key ideas (photosynthesis and respiration equations, limiting factors, aerobic vs anaerobic) using shorter chunks. Add a retrieval starter on this topic several times a week for the next fortnight.
2. **Diagnose first.** Carry out a question-level analysis on the Bioenergetics test to find whether the gaps are in knowledge, graph and calculation skills, or exam technique, then target the lessons accordingly.
3. **Organisation.** Review the questions answered poorly and revisit them in spaced retrieval tasks, rather than re-teaching the whole topic.
4. **Keep Cells and Infection and response secure.** Include a few questions from each in regular low-stakes quizzes so the gains aren't lost.
5. **Support.** Identify students scoring below 40% for small-group intervention, and share what worked well in Cells with the department.
6. **Re-test.** Give a short re-test on Bioenergetics and Organisation in about four to six weeks to check the impact.

I am happy to share the question-level analysis and the intervention list once complete.
