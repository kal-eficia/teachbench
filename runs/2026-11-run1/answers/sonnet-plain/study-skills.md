# 50-minute Year 10 session: how to revise effectively

**Assumptions:** a form time or PSHE-style session in a classroom with a projector, mixed subjects, GCSE mocks or exams ahead. Students have phones but you can ask them to be away for the session. No special equipment. You can adjust the examples to the subjects your students take.

## Aims
By the end, students can:
- Explain why highlighting and rereading give a false sense of knowing.
- Use two evidence-based methods: **retrieval practice** and **spaced practice**.
- Make a realistic revision plan for the coming week that includes phone boundaries.

## The one big message
**"If revision feels easy, it's probably not working. Effort that feels a bit hard is the sign of learning."**

## Plan

### 1. Hook (5 min): "Do you know it, or does it just look familiar?"
- Show a map of Europe or a short diagram (for example, a labelled heart or a periodic table section) for 30 seconds. Take it away.
- Students try to draw or list what they remember, in silence.
- Debrief: "Looking at it felt easy. Recalling it was harder. That gap is the lesson."

### 2. Why highlighting and rereading let us down (7 min)
- Explain, in plain language: rereading makes text feel **familiar**, and familiar feels like "I know it". But the exam asks you to **pull information out**, not recognise it.
- Quick poll: "Hands up if highlighting is your main method." Reassure them: most students do this, and it is not a character flaw.
- Short evidence story: research on student study habits (for example Dunlosky and colleagues' review, 2013) rated highlighting and rereading as low-utility, and practice testing and spaced practice as high-utility. Say it simply and avoid overclaiming. Their value depends on the task, but as a main strategy they underperform.

### 3. Strategy 1: Retrieval practice (10 min)
**What it is:** closing the notes and trying to recall.

**Do it now (live practice):**
1. Give students a short piece of content (one paragraph or a diagram from a subject they all study, or let them choose a page from their own notes).
2. Read it for 2 minutes.
3. Cover it. **Write down everything you remember** in 3 minutes (a brain dump).
4. Check against the notes in a different colour and add what was missing.

**Variations to list on the board:**
- Blank-page brain dump.
- Flashcards, with the question on the front and recall *before* flipping.
- Past-paper questions with notes closed.
- Explain it aloud to a family member or a pet.
- "Quiz yourself" apps or tools that make you answer first.

### 4. Strategy 2: Spacing (8 min)
- **Idea:** several short sessions beat one long session. Memory fades, and each time you retrieve it again, it lasts longer.
- Show the **forgetting curve** as a simple sketch: memory drops quickly, then each review flattens the curve.
- **Simple schedule:** learn today, review tomorrow, then in 3 days, then in a week, then in 2 to 3 weeks.
- Activity: students place one topic from tomorrow's lesson into a small spacing grid across the next two weeks (a table with days across the top).
- Mention **interleaving** briefly: mixing topics in one session is harder but works better than doing one topic for hours.

### 5. The phone problem (8 min)
Do this honestly and without lecturing. Students know phones distract them, so help them solve it.

- **Quick experiment idea:** ask students to estimate their daily screen time and then check on their phone (they may have it out for 1 minute for this).
- **What the research suggests (stated carefully):** having a phone visible, even silenced, can reduce attention on a task. Multitasking while studying usually leads to slower, poorer work.
- Practical options (students choose one):
  - Phone in another room, or in a bag across the room, during revision blocks.
  - **Short focus blocks:** 25 minutes of work, 5 minutes of break (phone use allowed in the break only).
  - Turn on Do Not Disturb or a focus mode, and log out of social apps.
  - Use a study tool or timer on a separate device, or a classic kitchen timer.
  - Revise with a friend who holds you accountable.
- Be realistic: "You don't have to be perfect. Aim for one phone-free 25-minute block today."

### 6. Build a plan (9 min)
Each student completes a one-page **"My next 7 days"** sheet:
- Pick **two subjects** to focus on.
- Choose **one retrieval method** for each (e.g. flashcards, brain dumps, past-paper questions).
- Put **three short sessions** into a weekly grid (time and place).
- Write their **phone rule** for those sessions.
- Add the **spacing**: which topic they will return to in 2 days and again in a week.

Students share their plan with a partner, who asks one question: "How will you know if it's working?"

### 7. Plenary and exit ticket (3 min)
On a slip or in a form, students write:
1. One thing they will **stop** doing (for example, "just highlighting").
2. One thing they will **start** doing.
3. One phone rule they will try this week.

## Follow-up
- **Next week:** a 5-minute check-in: "Did you try your plan? What worked?"
- **Homework:** do one 20-minute retrieval session and bring one thing they learned.
- **Share with parents:** a short note summarising the strategies, to help support at home.

## Resources to prepare
- Short content passage (or use their own notes).
- Slide with the forgetting curve.
- "My next 7 days" planner (one side of A4).
- Exit slips.

## Tips for delivery
- **Model a failure:** do the brain dump yourself on a topic and show you forgot some. It normalises the effort.
- **Avoid shaming phone use.** Use "most of us" language and include yourself.
- **Address "I've always done highlighting and got OK grades."** Say that it may work a bit for some, but combining it with retrieval is better, and exams at GCSE ask for more recall than before.
- **Keep it active:** there is no more than 8 minutes of teacher talk at any point.
- **Check for students with extra needs:** some may need printed templates, extra time on the brain dump, or the option to dictate.

## Possible adaptations
- **Shorter slot (30 min):** drop the planning section and set it as homework.
- **Longer or double session:** add a past-paper practice using retrieval, then a peer review.
- **Subject-specific version:** use a topic they are actually studying for the live brain dump.
