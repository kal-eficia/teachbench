# Preparing an Autistic Year 7 Student for a Cover Teacher and a Museum Trip

*Assumptions: the student is not in crisis about school generally, you know him reasonably well, he has a SENCo or support staff you can involve, and his parents are happy to be part of the plan. Every autistic child is different, so adapt anything below to what you already know works for him. Where possible, ask him what would help.*

## The principle

Unpredictability is usually what is hard, more than the change itself. The more he knows in advance (what, where, when, who, how long, and what happens if something goes wrong), the less there is to fear. Give information early, in a format he likes, and repeat it calmly.

## Start now: tell him early, and tell him yourself

- Tell him as soon as the plan is confirmed, not the night before. A familiar adult delivering the news privately is better than learning it in front of the class.
- Choose a calm moment, not a transition or busy time.
- Keep the message simple and concrete. For example: "On Tuesday and Wednesday, Mr Patel will teach your lessons because I'm away. Your timetable stays exactly the same. On Friday, our class is going to the museum."
- Say what will stay the same as well as what will change. This is often the most reassuring part: same room, same timetable, same classmates, same seat, same break and lunch.

## For the two days with a cover teacher

**Prepare the cover teacher**
- Give the cover teacher a short, practical briefing (with parent and student consent) on what helps him. For example: give clear instructions in writing, avoid sudden changes, don't cold-call him, let him have his usual seat, and allow him to use his usual break strategy.
- Include his strengths and interests, as well as what he finds hard. A line like "He loves trains and is brilliant at maths" gives the cover teacher an easy way in.
- Tell the cover teacher plainly what to do if he becomes distressed (for example, offer a quiet space or let him go to a named adult) and who to call.
- If possible, use a cover teacher he already knows, or arrange for a familiar adult (a TA or form tutor) to be present for the first lesson.

**Prepare him**
- Give him the **name and a photo** of the cover teacher, if possible, and a short note in the cover teacher's own words: "Hello, I'm Mr Patel. I like football. I'm looking forward to meeting your class."
- Provide a **visual timetable** for each of the two days that shows lessons, teachers and rooms. Mark which lessons have a new teacher.
- Leave **clear, written work** that follows the usual routine so lessons feel familiar.
- Agree a **signal or card** he can use if he needs a break, and a place to go.

## For the museum trip

**Give him as much information as possible, in advance**
- A **social story or visual itinerary** is excellent: what time we leave, how we travel, who sits where on the coach, how long it takes, what we will see, where we eat lunch, when we come back, and what we do if plans change.
- **Show him the place.** Look at the museum website together, including photos of the entrance, the galleries, the toilets, the café and the quiet areas. A virtual tour or video is ideal.
- Tell him **exactly what to bring**: packed lunch, a drink, headphones, a fidget item, a spare change of clothes if relevant.
- Tell him **which adults** are in his group and who he can go to.
- Explain the **rules and expectations** concretely: where we walk, whether we can touch exhibits, and how loud we can be.

**Think about sensory and social demands**
- Check for noise, crowds, bright or flashing lights, echoing rooms, hand dryers, school coach smells and unexpected sounds. Ask the museum if they have a quiet room or a low-sensory time or map.
- Allow him to wear **headphones or ear defenders** and bring a comfort item.
- Plan **choices about groupings**: sit with a friend or with a familiar adult on the coach.
- Build in **breaks** and agree where he can go if overwhelmed.
- Give him a **job or purpose** (for example, photographer, or finding specific objects on a checklist). A clear task can reduce anxiety and make the visit more meaningful.
- Warn him that things may run late or that an exhibit may be closed, and agree what we will do ("we'll look at something else, and that's fine").

**Involve parents and the SENCo**
- Phone or email his parents about the plan so they can reinforce it at home and tell you what has worked before.
- Do a short risk assessment with his needs in mind and ensure staffing is adequate.
- Consider a **short rehearsal**, such as a walk through the route to the coach point.

## The days themselves

- **Morning check-in** on each of the two cover days with a familiar adult: "Here is today's plan; how are you feeling?"
- Keep routines such as seating, equipment and start-of-lesson tasks the same.
- Use a **brief end-of-day check-in** to let him say how it went and to catch worries early.
- On the trip, brief the adults before leaving, and confirm that someone has his plan, parent contact and a copy of the itinerary.
- If plans change, tell him straight away, calmly and clearly, and offer an alternative.

## After

- Debrief with him: what went well, what was hard, what would help next time.
- Let his parents know how it went.
- Record what worked so that future cover and trips are easier to plan.

## A short checklist

- [ ] Told him early, in person, in a calm moment
- [ ] Said clearly what stays the same
- [ ] Briefed the cover teacher (with consent)
- [ ] Visual timetable for both cover days
- [ ] Photo and name of cover teacher shared
- [ ] Social story or itinerary for the trip
- [ ] Looked at the museum online together
- [ ] Sensory needs checked and quiet space identified
- [ ] Break signal and a trusted adult agreed
- [ ] Parents and SENCo informed
- [ ] Check-ins planned during and after
