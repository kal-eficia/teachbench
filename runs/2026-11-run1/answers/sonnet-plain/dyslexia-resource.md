# Adapted source task: The Great Plague (Year 8, dyslexia-friendly)

**Assumptions:** the student can access the same ideas as classmates but needs reduced reading load, clear structure and some support for writing. I have kept the two key thinking skills of the original: **comparing** what two sources suggest, and **judging usefulness** with **own knowledge**. I have not changed the sources themselves, as I don't have them, so see the notes on adapting them below.

## What stays the same (the thinking)
1. Work out what each source suggests about how Londoners reacted.
2. Compare the two: similarities and differences.
3. Decide which is more useful for an enquiry into the impact of the plague, and justify it using own knowledge.

## What changes (the access)
- Shorter, chunked task with numbered steps.
- Simple, direct wording.
- Source text broken into short pieces with key words explained.
- Sentence starters and a planning grid, so effort goes into thinking, not organising.
- Choice of how to answer (writing, bullet points or speaking).

## Adapted task sheet (student version)

**Layout notes:** use a dyslexia-friendly sans-serif font such as Arial, Verdana or Calibri at 12 to 14 pt, with 1.5 line spacing, left-aligned (not justified), cream or pale pastel paper or background, and plenty of white space. Avoid italics and underlining, and use bold sparingly for key words.

### The Great Plague, 1665: how did Londoners react?

**Your mission:** Be a history detective. You will look at two sources and decide which helps us understand the plague best.

**Time:** about 30 to 40 minutes. You can take breaks between steps.

---

#### Step 1: Know the words (2 minutes)

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| Plague | A deadly disease that spread quickly |
| Source | Something from the past that tells us about history |
| Extract | A short piece taken from a longer text |
| Historian | A person who studies the past |
| Reaction | What people did or felt when something happened |
| Useful | Helps us answer our question |

*(Add any words from your actual sources that may be hard, with simple meanings.)*

---

#### Step 2: Read Source A (a diary from 1665)
*Teacher: use the original text but with the helps below.*

- Read it in **short chunks**, or ask for it to be read aloud or use a reading tool.
- Mark with a **coloured pencil** anything that shows what people **did** or **felt**.

**Answer in a sentence or a few words:**
1. Who wrote Source A, and when?
2. What did Londoners **do** in this source?
3. How did Londoners **feel**?

---

#### Step 3: Read Source B (a modern historian)
- Same method: short chunks, reading help, colour marking.

**Answer:**
1. Who wrote Source B, and when?
2. What does this source say Londoners **did**?
3. How does it say they **felt**?

---

#### Step 4: Compare (use the grid)

| | Source A (diary, 1665) | Source B (historian) |

| How Londoners felt | | |

**Now answer:**
- **One way they are the same:** "Both sources show that ..."
- **One way they are different:** "Source A says ... but Source B says ..."

*Starter help: "Source A suggests that Londoners ... . Source B suggests that ... ."*

---

#### Step 5: Which is more useful?

Think about our question: **What was the impact of the plague on London?**

Use this checklist to help you decide. Tick or write a short note.

| Question | Source A | Source B |
|---|---|---|
| Was the writer there at the time? | | |
| Does it show what one person saw, or the bigger picture? | | |
| Did the writer have time to research? | | |
| Could the writer be biased or have a reason to exaggerate? | | |

**Now bring in your own knowledge.** Which of these things do you know about the plague? Choose two or more and say whether each source does or does not show them:
- Closing up infected houses and marking them with a red cross
- People leaving London (especially the rich)
- Plague doctors and ideas about what caused the disease
- Mass burials
- Number of deaths
- Effect on trade and jobs

---

#### Step 6: Write your answer

Choose **one** way to answer:
- **A:** Write 6 to 8 sentences using the frame below.
- **B:** Use bullet points under the three headings below.
- **C:** Record your answer or explain it to your teacher.

**Answer frame**

1. **What the sources suggest:**
   "Source A suggests that Londoners ... because it says ... . Source B suggests ... because it says ... ."
2. **Compare:**
   "One similarity is ... . One difference is ... ."
3. **Which is more useful:**
   "I think Source ... is more useful for finding out about the impact of the plague because ... . From my own knowledge, I know that ... , and this source ... ."
4. **A limit:**
   "However, Source ... is less useful because ... ."

**Challenge (optional):** What other source would help you? Why?

## Notes for the teacher

### Adapting the sources
- Keep the same content wherever possible, but you may **trim long passages** to the sentences that carry the evidence needed for the question. This keeps the same thinking without a heavy reading load.
- Provide the sources **in a larger font** with line spacing, and split them into numbered lines so the student can find their place. Put key unfamiliar words in a short glossary beside the text, not in the text.
- Offer **audio** of both sources (read aloud by you or text-to-speech), so reading accuracy is not the barrier.
- A seventeenth-century diary can have old spellings. Where it matters, give a **modernised version** alongside the original, and tell the student you are doing so.

### Supports
- **Planning grid** (Step 4) means the student doesn't need to hold both texts in memory.
- **Sentence starters** reduce the demand of getting started, without writing the answer for them.
- **Choice of response:** oral, bullet points or full writing. Assess the history, not the spelling or handwriting.
- **Chunking and breaks:** give each step on a separate sheet or a separate screen if that helps.
- **Assistive technology:** speech-to-text or a laptop if the student normally uses them.
- **Extra time** where appropriate.

### Marking
Mark against the original thinking skills:
- Do they identify what **each source** suggests?
- Do they **compare** accurately?
- Do they make a **judgement** about usefulness and **support it with own knowledge**?
Do not penalise spelling, grammar or handwriting. Feedback can be verbal.

### Check with the student
- Ask what helps most: coloured overlays, audio, bullet points.
- Ask whether the font and layout feel right for them, as preferences vary a lot.
