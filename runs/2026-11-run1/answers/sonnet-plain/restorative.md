# Restorative conversation: two Year 9 boys (fight after an online comment)

**Assumptions:** The sanctions are finished, both boys have agreed (or at least not refused) to take part, the comment was posted on a platform the school can see evidence of, and there is no ongoing safeguarding or bullying pattern that needs a different route. If there is a history of repeated targeting, or either boy is frightened of the other, check with your safeguarding lead first and use a shuttle model (you speak to each boy separately) instead of a face-to-face meeting.

## Before the meeting (today)

1. **Speak to each boy separately for 5 minutes.** Ask: "Are you willing to sit down with [name] and talk it through?" Make clear it is voluntary, it is not another punishment, and nothing they say will be used to add a sanction. If either says no, don't force it. Do a one-to-one restorative chat with each instead.
2. **Check what each wants from it.** Ask each: "What would you most like to say, and what would you most like to hear?" Note it down so you can steer.
3. **Tell them what will happen:** where, how long (20 to 25 minutes), who will be there, and the order of speaking.
4. **Tell both boys the ground rules now**, so there are no surprises:
   - One person speaks at a time.
   - No insults, no name-calling, no bringing in other people.
   - You can ask for a pause at any time.
   - What's said stays in the room, except anything that raises a safeguarding concern (be honest about this).
5. **Check the practicalities.** Book a quiet, neutral room (not your classroom desk or the head's office). Seat them at an angle, not directly opposite. Have water, tissues and a clock. Make sure a colleague knows where you are. Decide a time that avoids the start of lunch or the end of the day, when they will want to leave or will be tired.
6. **Check the online side.** Is the comment still up? Ask the boy who posted it to take it down before the meeting, or at least be ready to do so straight afterwards. Check whether others shared or reacted to it, as that may need a separate follow-up.

## Your stance

You are neutral and curious. You are not a judge. Don't reopen who is "right". The sanction dealt with accountability; this conversation is about understanding impact and repairing the relationship so they can share a corridor, a class and a friendship group without trouble. Use a calm, slow voice and plain language. Keep your questions open.

## The meeting (about 25 minutes)

**1. Opening (2 min)**
Thank them for coming. Remind them of the ground rules and the purpose: "We're not here to decide who was right. We're here to understand what happened and how to move forward."

**2. Boy A tells his side (4 min), boy B listens**
Use the standard restorative questions:
- What happened, from your point of view?
- What were you thinking at the time?
- What have you thought about since?

Boy B just listens. If he interrupts, hold up a hand and say, "You'll get your turn."

**3. Boy B tells his side (4 min), boy A listens**
Same questions. Keep the order fair: if the one who posted the comment goes first, make sure the other boy gets the same time and attention.

**4. Impact (5 min)**
Ask each in turn:
- Who has been affected by what happened, and how?
- What has been the hardest part for you?
- How did it feel to see the comment? How did it feel to be in the fight?

Then ask each to say back what the other said, in their own words: "What did you hear [name] say?" Reflecting back is where the real shift often happens. Help with sentence starters if they stall: "I heard you say that... I didn't realise..."

**5. Repair (6 min)**
Ask:
- What needs to happen to put things right?
- What would you each need from the other from now on?
- What could you do differently if you felt annoyed with each other again?

Let them come up with the actions. Make it concrete: for example, the comment is deleted, no posting about each other, they agree what to do if one of them is annoyed (walk away, tell a named adult, say it face to face), and agree how they will act around the same friends. Write the agreement in their words, on one side of paper, and ask both to sign it. Keep it short, specific and achievable. Don't ask them to be friends, only to be respectful.

**6. Close (2 min)**
Ask each: "Is there anything else you want to say?" Thank them. Agree the follow-up date. Make sure they leave separately, or together if they choose, and not straight into a group of onlookers. Give them a note or a pass for their next lesson so they aren't questioned.

## Useful phrases if it goes wrong

- If one boy gets defensive: "I'm hearing that you feel [X]. Can we come back to what happened for [name]?"
- If someone says "I don't care": "It sounds like this is hard to talk about. Take a minute." Then: "What would make it easier?"
- If it heats up: "Let's pause. Take a breath." Offer a break. If it is not recoverable, end calmly and try again with separate conversations.
- If the apology is thin ("sorry, whatever"): "What are you sorry for, specifically?" Don't push for a performance, but a vague one rarely repairs anything.

## After the meeting

- Give them both a copy of the agreement and keep one on file.
- Tell the form tutor and relevant heads of year what has been agreed, so staff can quietly look out for them. Keep it low-key.
- Check in separately with each boy after two days, and again after about two weeks: "How has it been? Anything not working?"
- Keep an eye on social media, group chats and the corridor in the following weeks.
- Log it on your system as a restorative meeting and record the outcome.
- Consider whether a short follow-up with the wider year group about online conduct is needed (without naming anyone).

## Quick prompt card for the day

1. What happened?
2. What were you thinking then, and what are you thinking now?
3. Who was affected, and how?
4. What do you need to move on?
5. What will you each do differently?
6. What happens if it goes wrong again, and who will you go to?
