# Knowledge Organiser: The Norman Conquest, 1066

**Year 7 History | Big question: How did William of Normandy win England in 1066, and how did he keep it?**

---

## 1. Key people

| Person | Who they were | Why they matter |
|---|---|---|
| **Edward the Confessor** | King of England, 1042 to January 1066 | Died with no children, so nobody had a clear right to the throne |
| **Harold Godwinson** | Earl of Wessex, the most powerful noble in England | Chosen as king by the Witan (the king's advisers) the day after Edward died |
| **William, Duke of Normandy** | Powerful ruler of Normandy (northern France) | Claimed Edward had promised him the throne, and that Harold had sworn to support him |
| **Harald Hardrada** | King of Norway, a famous Viking warrior | Claimed the throne through an old agreement between earlier kings |
| **Edgar Ætheling** | Edward's young great-nephew, a teenager | Had royal blood but was too young and had little support |
| **Tostig Godwinson** | Harold's exiled brother | Joined Hardrada to try to take revenge on Harold |

---

## 2. The four claimants

| Claimant | Basis of claim | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harold Godwinson** | Named by Edward on his deathbed (said to be); chosen by the Witan; crowned at once | Already in England; rich and powerful; English nobles backed him | Not royal blood; had to defend against two invasions |
| **William of Normandy** | Edward's promise; Harold's oath (Harold had visited Normandy in 1064) | Strong army; the Pope blessed his invasion | Had to cross the Channel; no English support |
| **Harald Hardrada** | An old agreement between earlier kings of Norway and England | Fearsome army; Tostig helped him | Weak claim; no English support |
| **Edgar Ætheling** | Royal blood (Edward's great-nephew) | Closest blood relative | Too young; no power or army |

## 4. The Battle of Stamford Bridge (25 September 1066)

- Harold marched his army about **185 miles north** in around four days.
- He caught Hardrada's army by **surprise**; many Vikings were not wearing armour.
- Hardrada and Tostig were both **killed**. Out of about 300 Viking ships, only about 24 were needed to carry the survivors home.
- **Result:** An English victory, but Harold's army was tired and had lost men.
- **Bad news:** Within days, Harold heard that William had landed in the south.

---

## 5. The Battle of Hastings (14 October 1066)

**The armies**
- **English:** Mostly **infantry** fighting on foot. They formed a **shield wall** on top of **Senlac Hill**.
- **Normans:** Cavalry (knights on horseback), archers and foot soldiers.

**What happened**
1. The Normans' **archers** shot uphill; the shield wall held.
2. The Norman **cavalry** charged but could not break through.
3. Some English soldiers chased the retreating Normans down the hill and broke the shield wall. This may have been a Norman trick (a **feigned retreat**), or a mistake.
4. The Normans attacked the gaps. **Harold was killed** (tradition says by an arrow to the eye; historians are not certain).
5. The English army collapsed.

**Why William won**
- Harold's army was **tired** after the march south, and had already fought at Stamford Bridge.
- William used a **mixed army** (archers, cavalry, infantry). Harold had very few archers and almost no cavalry.
- The English **broke the shield wall** when they chased the Normans.
- Harold was **killed**, leaving the English without a leader.
- **Luck:** The wind kept William's fleet in port for weeks, but changed after Harold's men went home.

## 6. How William kept control

### Castles
- **Motte and bailey castles:** A **motte** is a mound of earth with a wooden tower on top. The **bailey** is a fenced area below it.
- Built quickly (sometimes in days) so Normans could control an area and **scare the local people**.
- Later rebuilt in **stone** (for example, the Tower of London).
- About **500 castles** were built in William's reign.

### The Feudal System
Everyone owed something to the person above them.

```
KING (owns all the land)
   |  gives land in return for loyalty, money and knights
BARONS and BISHOPS (about 200 powerful nobles)
   |  gives land in return for soldiers
KNIGHTS
   |  given land to live on in return for fighting
PEASANTS (villeins, serfs)
   work the land and owe labour and food
```

- Key idea: **land in return for loyalty and service.**
- At the **Oath of Salisbury (1086)**, all major landowners swore loyalty directly to William.

### The Domesday Book (1086)
- A huge **survey** of England ordered by William in 1085.
- Officials asked: *Who owns the land? How much is it worth? How many people, animals and ploughs are there?*
- The name comes from "Doomsday" (the Day of Judgement): its decisions could not be appealed.
- **Purpose:** So William knew what he owned and how much **tax** he could collect.
- It is still used by historians today.

### The Harrying of the North (1069 to 1070)
- Rebellions broke out in the north of England.
- William's army **destroyed** crops, animals and villages. Thousands died from starvation.
- **Message:** Rebel and you will be crushed.

## 7. Key vocabulary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Claimant** | Someone who says they have the right to be king |
| **Witan** | The council of English nobles who advised the king |
| **Earl** | A powerful English noble who ruled a large area |
| **Shield wall** | Soldiers standing shoulder to shoulder with shields locked together |
| **Cavalry** | Soldiers who fight on horseback |
| **Infantry** | Soldiers who fight on foot |
| **Feigned retreat** | Pretending to run away to trick the enemy |
| **Motte and bailey** | A type of early Norman castle |
| **Feudal system** | A system where land is exchanged for loyalty and service |
| **Baron** | A powerful noble who held land directly from the king |
| **Knight** | A mounted soldier who held land in return for fighting for his lord |
| **Peasant** | A poor farm worker |
| **Survey** | A careful study that collects detailed information |
| **Harrying** | Attacking and destroying |
| **Conquest** | Taking over a country by force |

## 9. Think like a historian

- **Cause:** Why did William win at Hastings? (Think: luck, tiredness, tactics, mistakes.)
- **Consequence:** What changed for ordinary English people after 1066?
- **Significance:** Was the Battle of Hastings the most important event of 1066? Why, or why not?
- **Sources:** The **Bayeux Tapestry** was made by the Normans' side. How might that affect what it shows?
