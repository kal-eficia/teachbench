# Year 3: Introducing column addition with one exchange (47 + 36)

**Class:** 30 children | **Time:** 60 minutes | **Prior learning:** adding tens and ones mentally without crossing a ten

**Assumptions:** you have Dienes (base 10) or place value counters in sets of 2 or 3 per table, mini whiteboards, and a place value grid (Tens | Ones) printed or drawn. All calculations stay within 100 and involve exactly one exchange (ones to tens).

## Learning objective
I can add two 2-digit numbers using the column method when I need to exchange 10 ones for 1 ten.

## Success criteria (child-friendly)
- I line up my tens and ones.
- I add the ones first.
- If I make 10 or more ones, I swap 10 ones for 1 ten and show it in the tens column.
- I add the tens, including the one I swapped.

## Key vocabulary
column, tens, ones, exchange (or "regroup"), digit, total, place value

## Resources
- Dienes or counters (1 set per pair is enough)
- Place value mats (Tens | Ones)
- Mini whiteboards and pens
- Lined or squared paper for the practice
- Slides or board with the worked example

## Timings at a glance
| Time | Phase |
|---|---|
| 0-10 | Starter: link to what they know |
| 10-25 | Teach: concrete, then pictorial, then written |
| 25-35 | Guided practice with whiteboards |
| 35-52 | Independent work (three levels) |
| 52-60 | Plenary: spot the mistake |

## 1. Starter (10 min): "Make it, say it"
- Quick-fire mental adds with no exchange, on whiteboards: 30 + 20, 43 + 5, 52 + 24, 61 + 35. Hold up boards together.
- Then pose one problem that does cross: **"What is 8 + 5?"** Take the answer (13). Ask: "How many tens and how many ones is 13?" Record 1 ten and 3 ones. This is the whole idea of the lesson in miniature.
- Tell them: "Today we'll use this to add bigger numbers."

## 2. Teaching input (15 min)
Move through three stages. Keep each stage short and keep children active (they build as you build).

**Stage 1: Concrete (about 5 min).** Problem: *Amira has 47 stickers and gets 36 more. How many now?*
- Pairs make 47 and 36 on their mats with Dienes (4 tens and 7 ones, 3 tens and 6 ones).
- Ask: "Which do we add first, the tens or the ones?" Settle on **ones first**.
- Combine the ones: 7 + 6 = 13 ones. Ask: "Can we leave 13 ones in the ones column?" (No, too many.)
- Swap 10 ones for 1 ten rod. Place the new ten in the tens column. 3 ones left.
- Add the tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8 tens. Total: 83.

**Stage 2: Pictorial (about 3 min).** Draw the same on the board using sticks for tens and dots for ones. Circle the 10 dots and draw an arrow to the tens column.

**Stage 3: Written column method (about 7 min).** Model on the board, thinking aloud:

```
   4 7
 + 3 6
 -----
     3     (7 + 6 = 13, write 3 ones)
 1         (carry 1 ten, written small under the tens column)
```
Then add the tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8, giving **83**.

Teaching points to stress:
- Digits line up: tens under tens, ones under ones.
- The exchanged ten is written **under the answer line in the tens column** (pick one position and stay consistent all lesson, because mixed conventions confuse children).
- Link every written step back to the Dienes: "That small 1 is the ten rod we swapped for."
- Do one more together with different numbers (e.g. 28 + 35 = 63), children building and recording, you recording on the board.

## 3. Guided practice (10 min)
Mini whiteboards, one question at a time. Children build with Dienes if they want, then write the column method. Show boards on "3, 2, 1".
1. 25 + 38 = 63
2. 46 + 17 = 63
3. 54 + 28 = 82
4. **Contrast question:** 42 + 35 = 77 (no exchange). Ask: "Why didn't we need to swap this time?" (Ones make 7, fewer than 10.)

Use the answers for quick assessment. Note anyone who writes the 1 in the wrong column or forgets to add it, and gather them with you at the start of independent work.

## 4. Independent work (17 min)
Choose the group by the guided practice, not before. Children can move up or down.

**Support (about 8-10 children), with adult for the first 5 minutes.** Dienes and place value mat stay on the table. Column method with a pre-drawn grid.
- 14 + 28 = 42
- 25 + 17 = 42
- 36 + 25 = 61
- 19 + 23 = 42 (note: answers may repeat by chance, so shuffle questions across the set if you notice a pattern)
- 47 + 15 = 62
- 28 + 36 = 64

**Core (about 12-14 children).** Column method, no grid, with Dienes available.
- 38 + 45 = 83
- 57 + 26 = 83
- 29 + 34 = 63
- 65 + 17 = 82
- 48 + 25 = 73
- 36 + 48 = 84
- Mix of two questions with **no** exchange (e.g. 41 + 36 = 77, 52 + 26 = 78) so children have to decide.

**Challenge (about 6-8 children).** Reasoning and application.
- Missing digit: 4 _ + 3 8 = 7 3 (the missing digit is 5... check: 45 + 38 = 83, so choose "4 _ + 2 8 = 7 3", missing 5)
- Error spotting: "Sam says 39 + 24 = 513. What did he do wrong? What is the right answer?" (He wrote 9 + 4 = 13 and 3 + 2 = 5 without exchanging; correct is 63.)
- Word problem: "A school bus has 37 children and a second bus has 46. How many in total? Is that more or fewer than 90?"
- Investigate: "Find three different pairs of 2-digit numbers that total 71 and need one exchange."

## 5. Plenary (8 min): "Spot the mistake"
Show three worked examples on the board. Children vote with thumbs up/down on whiteboards, then explain:
1. 36 + 27 with the carried ten forgotten (answer 53, should be 63).
2. 48 + 25 with digits misaligned.
3. 54 + 29 done correctly (83), so it is not always wrong.

Close with each child saying to a partner: "When do I need to exchange?" Take two or three answers.

## Common misconceptions to watch for
- Writing 13 in the ones column (two digits in one column).
- Forgetting to add the carried ten into the tens column.
- Adding the carried ten but writing it in the wrong column.
- Starting with the tens column and getting confused (reinforce "ones first").
- Misaligned digits when the numbers are written freehand.

## Assessment and follow-up
- Collect the guided practice responses and independent work to sort groups for tomorrow.
- Next lesson: practise with and without exchange, then move to word problems. After that, introduce exchanging in the tens column (e.g. 67 + 58).

## Adaptations
- **SEND/support:** keep concrete resources available, pre-drawn grids, one step at a time with a checklist card (Ones first, Exchange?, Tens).
- **EAL:** use gestures and the visual stages; sentence stem "I made __ ones, so I swapped for __ ten."
