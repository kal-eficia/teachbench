# Year 4 Science: Electricity (6 Weeks, 1 Hour per Week)

## Assumptions
- Working to the England primary national curriculum for Year 4 electricity. The statements covered are:
  - identify common appliances that run on electricity
  - construct a simple series electrical circuit, naming the basic parts (cells, wires, bulbs, switches, buzzers)
  - identify whether or not a lamp will light in a simple series circuit, based on whether the lamp is part of a complete loop with a battery
  - recognise that a switch opens and closes a circuit and associate this with whether or not a lamp lights
  - recognise some common conductors and insulators, and associate metals with being good conductors
- Kits are small: cells, bulbs, wires, switches, buzzers. For the conductors lesson you will need to gather a few extra items (paper clips, coins, foil, plastic ruler, wooden lolly sticks, rubber, a pencil lead or wooden pencil, fabric). Ask the class to bring items, or collect from the stationery cupboard.
- Pairs or groups of three working with one kit each. If you have fewer kits, run the practical in groups of four with roles rotating (builder, tester, recorder, reporter).
- A class of about 30. Adjust the number of kits to suit.
- **Safety:** only use the low-voltage cells in the kits. Never use mains electricity, and do not let children test sockets or plugs. Keep the safety message at the start of Week 1 and refer back to it.

## Big ideas for the unit
1. Electricity makes things work when there is a **complete loop** (circuit) including a power source.
2. A switch **opens or closes** the loop, so it turns things on and off.
3. Some materials let electricity flow (**conductors**) and some do not (**insulators**).
4. Scientists test fairly, predict, observe, then explain.

## Common misconceptions to listen for
| Misconception | How you will address it |
|---|---|
| "The bulb only needs one wire from the cell" | Let them try it and see it fail, then show the loop. |
| "Electricity is used up as it goes round" | Compare bulb brightness at different points, or use the "loop of children passing a ball" model. |
| "Electricity travels from both ends and meets in the middle" | Build, test, and describe the circuit in a drawing with arrows. |
| "All metals are conductors" / "All shiny things are conductors" | Test painted metal, foil, and a shiny plastic item. |
| "Switches 'let electricity through'" | Describe a switch as completing or breaking the loop. |
| "A bigger bulb needs a bigger cell" | Leave to Year 6; keep Year 4 focused on complete circuits. |

## Week-by-week plan

### Week 1: What uses electricity? Staying safe
- **Learning intention:** I can name things that use electricity and explain how to stay safe.
- **Starter (10 min):** Show a mixed set of pictures or objects (torch, kettle, wind-up toy, candle, battery radio, solar calculator, bicycle). Students sort: electrical / not electrical. Ask "How do you know?" Listen for ideas about power sources.
- **Main (35 min):** Sort appliances into mains-powered and battery-powered. Discuss where the electricity comes from. Safety rules: never touch plugs or sockets, never put anything in a socket, keep electricity away from water, and only ever use batteries from school in lessons. Pairs create a safety poster or a three-line "electricity safety rules" list.
- **Plenary (15 min):** "Odd one out" quiz. Which of these is not electrical: radio, hairdryer, bicycle? Why?
- **Prior knowledge check:** a short "draw and label what you think is inside a torch" exercise, kept in books to compare with Week 6.

### Week 2: Building a simple circuit
- **Learning intention:** I can build a circuit that lights a bulb and name its parts.
- **Starter (10 min):** Introduce the parts: cell, bulb, wire, switch, buzzer. Hold each up, students repeat the name and say what they think it does.
- **Main (40 min):** Challenge: "Make the bulb light." Give pairs one cell, one bulb, and two wires. Let them explore. After 10 minutes, ask pairs to draw what worked and what didn't. Share on the board. Introduce the idea of a **complete loop**. Show how to draw the circuit with a pictorial sketch.
- **Plenary (10 min):** "Will it light?" Show three drawings of circuits (one complete, two with gaps). Boards up: yes or no, and say why.

### Week 3: Circuit symbols and a bulb that won't light
- **Learning intention:** I can use symbols to draw a circuit and find out why a circuit does not work.
- **Starter (10 min):** Recap the loop. Show three "broken" circuits on the board and ask students to predict what is wrong with each.
- **Main (40 min):** Teach standard circuit symbols (cell, bulb, switch, buzzer, wire). Students draw the circuit they built last week using symbols and a ruler. Then do a "fault-finding" activity: you give each group a circuit with one deliberate fault (loose wire, bulb not screwed in, reversed-connection buzzer, flat cell). Groups find and fix the fault, recording what they found.
- **Plenary (10 min):** Groups share the faults they found. Collect a class list of "reasons a circuit might not work".

### Week 4: Switches
- **Learning intention:** I can explain how a switch controls a circuit.
- **Starter (10 min):** "What happens when you press a light switch at home?" Collect ideas. Show a simple switch and a model made from a paper clip and two drawing pins.
- **Main (40 min):** Add a switch to a working circuit. Students explain what happens when the switch is open and closed. Then challenge: use one switch to turn a buzzer on and off, or make a "burglar alarm" or a "quiz buzzer". Groups design a simple device, build it, and test it. Record with a symbol drawing.
- **Plenary (10 min):** "Open or closed?" Teacher holds up a card of a switch position. Students answer whether the bulb will light.

### Week 5: Conductors and insulators
- **Learning intention:** I can test materials to find out if they let electricity flow.
- **Starter (10 min):** Ask "Why are wires made of metal but covered in plastic?" Pair talk. Introduce the words conductor and insulator.
- **Main (40 min):** Set up a test circuit with a gap where each material can be placed. Students first **predict** (conductor or insulator) for each material, then test and record in a table. Include a few surprising items (painted metal, foil, a coin, a pencil with lead). Ask what they notice about metals. Make sure the test is fair: same circuit, same contact points, same cell.
- **Plenary (10 min):** Review predictions against results. Which predictions were wrong? Why might that be? (For example, a painted metal object may not conduct because of the paint.)

### Week 6: Putting it all together and showing what I know
- **Learning intention:** I can design, build and explain a circuit, and use what I have learned about electricity.
- **Starter (10 min):** Revisit the Week 1 drawing of the torch. Students add to or correct it, using a different coloured pen to show what they have learned.
- **Main (35 min):** Design challenge: "Make a working model of a [torch / doorbell / night-light / quiz buzzer] that has a switch." Students sketch a design with a symbol diagram, build it, test it, and explain how it works to another pair.
- **Plenary (15 min):** Short end-of-unit quiz (see below) and self-reflection: "Three things I know now that I did not know in Week 1."

## How you will know what they have learned

### Week-by-week assessment (formative)
- **Week 1:** Sorting and discussion. Note who confuses mains and battery power, and who cannot give a safety rule.
- **Week 2:** Can each pair build a working circuit? Do their drawings show a complete loop? Take a photo of each pair's circuit and drawing.
- **Week 3:** Do symbol drawings match the circuit built? Can they identify faults and explain the fix?
- **Week 4:** Can they say, in their own words, why the bulb goes out when the switch is open?
- **Week 5:** Is the test fair? Do their conclusions match their results? Do they use the words *conductor* and *insulator* correctly?
- **Week 6:** The design challenge and quiz below.

### Evidence to collect
- A "science book" page each week (drawing, prediction, result, explanation).
- Photos of circuits built.
- A short recorded or noted explanation from each student by Week 6 (just 30 seconds: "Tell me how your circuit works").

### End-of-unit quiz (10 minutes, Week 6)
Answer in books. Mix multiple choice and short answers.
1. Name three parts of a circuit. (Cell, wire, bulb, switch, buzzer, any three.)
2. Draw the symbol for a cell, a bulb and a switch.
3. A bulb is connected to a cell by two wires but does not light. Give two possible reasons. (Gap in the circuit, a loose connection, a flat cell, a faulty bulb.)
4. What does a switch do?
5. Which of these is an insulator? Copper wire / Plastic / Iron nail / Aluminium foil. (Plastic. Move the correct option's position so that it is not always the same across versions.)
6. Why are the wires in a plug covered in plastic?
7. Name one safety rule about electricity.
8. Explain why a circuit with an open switch will not work. Use the word *loop*.

### Looking at the evidence
- **Secure:** can build and draw a complete circuit, explain open and closed switches, name conductors and insulators and give a reason, and explain a fair test.
- **Developing:** can build a circuit with help, names most parts, but gets confused about how the loop and the switch work together.
- **Needs more:** cannot yet build a working circuit independently. Plan a short catch-up session in small groups using the Week 2 task again.

### Stretch (for students who finish early)
- Add a second bulb in the circuit. What happens to the brightness? (Introduces series circuits and sets up Year 6.)
- Design a circuit that has a bulb and a buzzer controlled by a single switch.
- Invent a test to find which household items conduct.

## Resources checklist
- Cells, bulbs, bulb holders, wires with crocodile clips, switches, buzzers (about one kit per pair).
- A tray of test materials (paper clip, foil, coin, plastic ruler, wooden stick, eraser, fabric, pencil, copper wire, aluminium foil).
- Large circuit symbol cards and a set of "faulty circuit" setups prepared before Week 3.
- Cards with drawings of working and non-working circuits.
- Prepare the Week 6 design challenge materials: card, tape, foil, paper fasteners.

## Quick tips
- Test the kits before Week 2, replacing flat cells and spare bulbs.
- Mark each kit with a number so you can trace faults.
- Use "talk partners" to reduce arguments over who builds.
- If a bulb fails, have a "spare bulb station" so groups don't wait.
