# Intervention plan: English Language Paper 1, Question 4 (evaluation)

## Assumptions
- AQA-style Paper 1, Question 4 (20 marks): students evaluate a statement about a literary fiction extract, supporting their view with textual references. You have 6 sessions of 30 minutes with six students, so each session needs one clear target.
- The students are below target, but I do not know why. Session 1 diagnoses this and the plan flexes after that.
- Use one extract throughout the early weeks, so students are not also decoding new text every time.

## What usually goes wrong on Q4
- Retelling the story or summarising instead of evaluating
- Not engaging with the statement ("to what extent do you agree...")
- Choosing quotations that are long or do not prove the point
- Naming techniques without explaining their effect
- Writing only one perspective, or an unsupported "I agree" with no argument
- Running out of time because the answer is unplanned

## Weekly plan

### Week 1: Diagnose and decode the question (30 min)
- 5 min: read a short extract together.
- 10 min: each student writes a 10-minute-style answer paragraph cold (or uses a recent one). Collect it.
- 10 min: take the statement apart together. Underline the key word (for example "the writer makes the setting seem threatening") and rewrite it in their own words as a question: "Is it threatening? How? Where does it change?"
- 5 min: exit task, students write their own definition of "evaluate" in one sentence.
- Afterwards: sort the six scripts by the main fault above. This decides who needs what in Weeks 2 to 5.

### Week 2: Choose strong evidence (30 min)
- 5 min: model a statement and show three quotations of different quality. Which is best and why? (Short, precise, rich in one or two words.)
- 15 min: pairs choose 3 short quotations, 5 words or fewer, for a given statement, then justify each in one sentence.
- 10 min: each student writes one sentence that embeds a quotation into their own argument.

### Week 3: From technique to effect (30 min)
- 5 min: quick recap on evidence.
- 15 min: practise one frame, "The word '...' suggests... which makes the reader feel/imagine..., so I agree/disagree because...". Do three rapid rounds from the same quotation, each time at a deeper level.
- 10 min: write a full paragraph. Teacher circulates and gives live feedback.

### Week 4: Agreeing and challenging, "to what extent" (30 min)
- 5 min: model a paragraph that agrees and then a paragraph that qualifies ("however, towards the end...").
- 15 min: students plan an answer to a new extract in a three-part structure: opening view, one or two supporting points (agree), one point that complicates or challenges.
- 10 min: peer read of plans against a short checklist (statement addressed, short quotations, effect explained, a "however").

### Week 5: Timed practice (30 min)
- 3 min: plan.
- 20 min: write a full answer under timed conditions (aim for 4 to 5 paragraphs; shorter in practice, with the fuller version completed at home if needed).
- 7 min: students highlight where they addressed the statement, a quotation, and an effect. Gaps are obvious.

### Week 6: Fresh paper, feedback, and next steps (30 min)
- 20 min: a fresh timed Q4 on a new extract.
- 10 min: mark against the levels descriptors together, comparing with Week 1. Each student sets one target for their next Q4.

## Assessment and tracking
- Keep each student's Week 1 and Week 6 answers. Mark both with the same levels grid so progress is visible.
- Keep a one-line note after each session of what each student did well and the next small step.

## Practical tips
- Give a one-page "Q4 recipe" they keep: statement into a question, 3 short quotations, effect sentence frame, a "however".
- Pair stronger writers with students who retell, so they hear evaluative language.
- If one student's issue is reading comprehension or writing stamina rather than evaluation, swap in a short comprehension or sentence-building task in Weeks 2 to 4 for that student.
- Set one short optional task a week, for example find three quotations for a statement.
