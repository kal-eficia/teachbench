Tropical rainforests are full of different plants and animals. This is because it is always hot there. It also rains a lot. More than 2,000 millimetres of rain fall each year. That is about as tall as a door!

A rainforest has four layers. Each layer is a different home. Plants and animals in each layer have changed to live with the amount of light and wetness (humidity) there.

1. **Emergent layer:** This is the top layer. The tallest trees poke out above everything else.
2. **Canopy:** This is a thick roof of leaves and branches. Many animals live here.
3. **Understorey:** This is the dark, damp middle part under the canopy.
4. **Forest floor:** This is the ground. Very little light reaches it.

People are cutting down rainforests. This is called deforestation. They do it to make farms that sell food and other things, to cut trees for wood (logging), and to build roads and other things like towns. This hurts the rainforest in three big ways:

- The forest is broken into small patches, so animals lose their homes. This is called habitat fragmentation.
- Some plants and animals only live in one rainforest. If their home goes, they can disappear forever. These are called endemic species.
- Trees store carbon. When they are cut down, the carbon goes into the air. This makes the Earth warmer and adds to climate change caused by people.
