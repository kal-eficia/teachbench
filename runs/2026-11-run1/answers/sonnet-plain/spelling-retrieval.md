# Year 2 spelling: 'kn', 'gn', 'ge' and 'dge' (five mornings, 10 minutes each)

**Assumptions:** Mixed-ability class of 6 and 7 year olds, a whiteboard and mini whiteboards available, no screens needed. Word bank for the week: **kn/gn:** knock, know, knee, knit, gnaw, gnat, gnome; **dge:** badge, edge, bridge, fridge, hedge; **ge:** age, huge, change, page, stage.

**Why this order and approach:** Copying doesn't stick. What helps memory is retrieving the word, saying it, using it in a sentence, and coming back to it over several days (spaced practice, with a mix of old and new). Each morning has a quick retrieval start, a short teaching or game, and a quick check at the end. Keep the same simple routine so little time is lost explaining.

**Spelling rules to teach (kept simple):**
- 'dge' comes straight after a short vowel sound (badge, edge, bridge). Listen: "short vowel, then /j/ = dge".
- 'ge' comes after a long vowel sound or after a consonant (age, huge, change).
- In 'kn' and 'gn', the first letter is silent and the word starts with the /n/ sound.

## Monday: Listen and sort

**Aim:** Hear the sound pattern and notice the spelling.

1. **Say it (2 min):** Say each of the words slowly together, tapping them out. Ask: "What can you hear at the end of badge? Where is the /j/ sound?"
2. **Sort (4 min):** On the board, draw three columns: **kn**, **gn**, **dge/ge**. Show word cards one at a time. Children hold up a mini whiteboard with the column name (not just the word) before you place it. Talk through each one: "Why does that belong there?"
3. **Mini whiteboard quick write (3 min):** Say a word, children write it, hold it up. Show the correct spelling, children fix with a different colour pen (underline the tricky part).
4. **Close (1 min):** Say the secret rule to a partner: "Silent k, silent g. Short vowel then dge."

## Tuesday: Retrieve and rebuild

**Aim:** Pull the words back from memory.

1. **Brain dump (2 min):** "Write as many words from yesterday as you can on your whiteboard." Don't show them the list. Compare with a partner.
2. **Look, cover, write, check, but with a twist (4 min):** Show one word for 3 seconds, cover it, children say it in their head, write it, and check. Then do it again with a new word. Only 5 words. Focus on the tricky bit: underline the silent letter or the 'd' in dge.
3. **Word building (3 min):** Use letter cards or magnetic letters. Give each pair a pile and call a word. They build it quickly, then swap to check another pair's word.
4. **Close (1 min):** Ask a few children to explain the tricky part of a word to the class.

## Wednesday: Use it in a sentence

**Aim:** Connect spelling to meaning.

1. **Quick retrieval (2 min):** Rapid-fire: you say a word, children write it.
2. **Spot the mistake (3 min):** Show five sentences with one wrong spelling in each ("The gnat bit my nee." "I stood on the egde of the bridge."). Children find, correct and say why.
3. **Say it, write it (4 min):** Say a short sentence, class repeats it, then writes it from memory. For example: "I know the huge gnome has a badge." Support: sentence starters. Challenge: add a word of their own from the list.
4. **Close (1 min):** Check with a partner.

## Thursday: Game day

**Aim:** Make practice feel like play while still retrieving.

1. **Quick start (1 min):** Say three words, write them.
2. **Game (7 min):** Choose one or two:
   - **Spelling battleships:** a grid with words hidden; partner guesses by spelling a word correctly to "hit".
   - **Pass the bag:** pass a bag; when you say stop, the child with it picks a word card, reads it, spells it aloud, and uses it in a sentence.
   - **Silent letter hunt:** hide word cards around the room. Children find one, copy it, and circle the silent letter or the 'dge'.
3. **Close (2 min):** Choose three words that are still tricky, and write them on the board for tomorrow.

## Friday: Check and celebrate

**Aim:** A friendly low-stakes check, then focus next steps.

1. **Mini test (4 min):** Ten words read aloud, including a mix of kn, gn, ge and dge. Include two words from earlier weeks. Include one sentence for them to write from dictation. No pressure; call it a "challenge".
2. **Self-check (2 min):** Children check their answers against the board and put a star next to words they got right, a dot next to those to practise.
3. **Reflect (2 min):** Ask each child (or a partner) to choose one word to remember and say how they will remember it ("k is silent, like a ninja").
4. **Close (2 min):** Praise the effort and note the common problems. Plan next week's practice around those, including a couple of review words from this week at the start of the next.

## Notes for teaching

- Keep a "tricky words" list from Thursday and Friday. Put those on a short list for home practice, with a quick 5-minute game rather than copying lists.
- Use actions and quick mnemonics (for example "the k in knock is silent, so it's knocking quietly").
- Include a few words from the earlier week every day, to keep the spacing going.
- Differentiate by the number of words rather than the task. Some children focus on three or four words, others on the full set.
- Praise noticing and fixing mistakes, not just getting words right.
