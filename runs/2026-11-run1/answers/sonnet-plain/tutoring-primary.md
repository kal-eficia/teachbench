# Year 5 one-to-one: 45-minute session on 6, 7 and 8 times tables

**Assumptions:** online, shared screen or whiteboard, no equipment beyond paper, pen and a screen. She knows her 2, 5 and 10 times tables well enough. I have assumed you do not yet know exactly which facts are shaky, so the session starts with a quick, low-pressure check to find out.

## Principles for this child
- **No timers, no countdowns, no "beat your score".** Anxiety blocks recall, so keep it calm and untimed.
- **Fewer facts, deeper.** Many of the 6, 7 and 8 facts are already known: 6x2, 6x5, 6x10 and so on. The real "hard" set is small.
- **Derive, don't just memorise.** Give her a way to work out a fact she's forgotten, so she is never stuck.
- **Make mistakes safe.** Say "tricky one, let's work it out" rather than "wrong".

## The small set of hard facts
Using commutativity (6x7 = 7x6), the unfamiliar facts shrink to about **6x6, 6x7, 6x8, 7x7, 7x8, 8x8** and perhaps 6x9, 7x9, 8x9. Show her that this is only about six facts, which is far less scary than "three whole tables".

## Session plan

### 1. Warm-up and mood check (4 min)
Chat briefly. Say: "Today there are no timers and no scores, and I'll never rush you." Play a quick game of 2s, 5s and 10s counting so she starts with success.

### 2. Find the facts (6 min): the "traffic light" grid
Show a multiplication grid up to 10x10. Ask her to colour each fact **green** (I know it), **amber** (I can work it out) or **red** (I get stuck). No testing, so this is self-report. Her red and amber squares become the focus. Shade out the facts she already knows to show how much is done.

### 3. Teach one strategy per fact family (12 min)
Pick **one or two** strategies today, not all.
- **Doubling:** 8x = double, double, double. 8x6: 6, 12, 24, 48. 6x = double 3x. 4x = double double.
- **Build from a known fact:** 7x7 = 7x5 + 7x2 = 35 + 14 = 49. 6x7 = 5x7 + 7 = 35 + 7 = 42.
- **Patterns:** 6x even number ends in the same digit (6x4 = 24, 6x6 = 36). 8x facts: units digits go 8, 6, 4, 2, 0.
- **Arrays:** draw a 6 by 7 array and split it into 5 by 7 and 1 by 7.

Model it, then **do one together**, then **she does one while I watch** and she explains out loud. The aim is "I can work it out" confidence.

### 4. Practise through play (12 min), untimed
Choose two:
- **Fact family cards:** she picks a card (6x8), says the answer, then says the related facts (8x6, 48 divided by 6, 48 divided by 8).
- **"Always, sometimes, never" talk:** "An 8 times table answer is always even: true or false? Why?"
- **Missing-number puzzles:** 6 x __ = 42, __ x 8 = 56.
- **Online game with no clock**, such as a fact-family card matching game or a "make the product" board game on a shared screen, played turn by turn where I answer wrong sometimes and she spots it.
- **Keep it playful:** let her catch me out with a deliberate mistake. This gives her control and builds understanding.

### 5. Apply it (5 min)
One real use so facts feel purposeful: a short word problem ("There are 7 boxes with 8 pens in each. How many pens?") or area of a rectangle. Ask her to say which strategy she used.

### 6. Reflection and close (4 min)
- Ask: "Which strategy felt best today?" and "Which fact is still annoying?"
- Re-colour one or two squares on the traffic light grid so she can see progress.
- Celebrate the effort specifically ("you worked out 7x8 on your own with doubling").

### 7. Home practice (2 min)
Explain to a parent or carer: **5 minutes a day, untimed**, using the same two or three facts. Suggest saying the facts aloud on the way to school, a quick game with cards, or the fact "of the day". Stress no testing or timing at home, and no pressure.

## Next session ideas
- Keep revisiting the amber facts with spaced repeats (some from last session, some new, some from last week).
- Add one new strategy.
- When she is confident, try very gentle fluency: "Say the answer when you're ready", still without a timer.

## Watch-outs
- If she freezes, don't wait in silence. Offer the first step ("Start with 5 sevens") or switch to a related fact.
- Avoid calling it "baby work". Frame it as "the cleverest way of doing it".
- If the anxiety seems wider than times tables, let the parent know gently so it can be shared with her school.
