# GCSE Maths Tutoring Review: [S1]

**Period:** Weeks 1 to 6 | **Subject:** GCSE Maths (Year 11) | **For:** Parents

## Summary: is the tutoring working?

**Yes, in most areas, and the evidence is real. But one topic, ratio and proportion, has barely moved, and it needs attention in the next block.**

Across the five topics tested both times, [S1]'s average score went from **43% to 59%**, a gain of about **16 percentage points**. Algebra has doubled, geometry has improved a lot, and number has gone up well. Ratio and proportion has moved only slightly, and probability has stayed the same.

## Scores

| Topic | Week 1 | Week 6 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Algebra | 35% | 70% | +35 |
| Number | 60% | 75% | +15 |
| Ratio and proportion | 40% | 45% | +5 |
| Geometry | 30% | 55% | +25 |
| Probability | 50% | 50% | 0 |
| Statistics | not tested | 65% | n/a (no starting point) |
| **Average of the five comparable topics** | **43%** | **59%** | **+16** |

*Statistics was not in the first test, so I can't say whether it has improved. 65% in Week 6 is a reasonable score, and it will be a useful starting point for next time. I have left it out of the average so the comparison is fair.*

## Effort and attendance
- **Attendance:** 11 of 12 sessions (92%). This is very good and is part of why progress has been quick.
- **Homework:** completed 4 out of 6 times (67%). This is decent, and there is room to improve. The topics where homework was done most are likely to be those showing the biggest gains, so I would like to get this up to every week.

## What has gone well
- **Algebra** is the biggest success story. A score of 35% has become 70%, which moves it from a weak area to a clear strength.
- **Geometry** has gone from 30% to 55%, a big step forward in a topic that often costs marks.
- **Number** was already the strongest area and has improved again to 75%.
- [S1] is turning up and putting in the work. That consistent effort is what produces results like these.

## What needs attention
### Ratio and proportion
This is the main concern. The score has moved from 40% to 45%, which is within the range that a different set of questions could easily produce. In other words, **I do not have good evidence that anything has changed here.**

[S1] also finds ratio "confusing" and has been avoiding it. This is common, because ratio draws on fractions, multiplication and division, and algebra all at once, and it is easy to feel lost. But avoiding it is the real risk. Ratio and proportion is a large part of the GCSE (it appears across several papers, often in multi-step problems), so skipping it will cost marks.

### Probability
No change at 50%. We have spent less time here so far, so this is not a surprise, but it is a topic with some easy marks that [S1] should be able to pick up quickly.

## How confident can we be in these results?
I want to be straightforward about what these numbers can and cannot tell us.
- The Week 1 and Week 6 tests were the same *style* but not identical papers, and each topic section is short, so a score can move by 5 to 10 points on a good or bad day. The big gains (algebra, geometry) are well beyond that. The small differences (ratio, probability) are not.
- [S1] is also being taught maths at school and doing other revision, so I cannot say the tutoring alone caused all of the improvement. What I can say is that the pattern of the gains matches the topics we have worked on most, which is a good sign.
- Six weeks is a short time. These are encouraging early results, not a final verdict.

## Plan for the next six weeks
1. **Ratio and proportion, little and often.** Start with the basics (simplifying ratios, sharing in a ratio) using short, low-pressure questions, so [S1] builds confidence and stops avoiding it. Then move on to proportion problems and harder multi-step questions.
2. **Check what is behind the "confusing".** I will ask [S1] to talk me through a ratio question so I can see exactly where it goes wrong (fractions? setting up the problem? the language?) and then target that.
3. **Probability.** A short block on the basic rules and tree diagrams, since this is where quick gains are available.
4. **Homework.** Aim for 6 out of 6. I will keep it short and focused (15 to 20 minutes) so that it is manageable, and I would be grateful if you could help [S1] set aside a regular time.
5. **Keep Algebra, Geometry and Number ticking over** with mixed practice so the gains are not lost.
6. **Re-test at the end of the next block**, including statistics, so we have clean figures for all six topics.

## How you can help at home
- Encourage [S1] to do the homework even when it is on ratio. Doing it badly is better than skipping it, because it shows me where the difficulty is.
- Ask [S1] to explain one question from each session to you. Explaining it is a good test of whether it has stuck.
- If [S1] says something is "confusing", please let me know, because I can adjust the next session.

## Overall
The tutoring is helping. The biggest topics, algebra and geometry, have improved considerably and the average has risen by about 16 points. The honest caveats are that ratio has not yet improved and that six weeks is a short time. The next block is about closing that gap and keeping up the homework, and I am confident that with the same effort the progress can continue.
