# Year 10 History Retrieval Grids: Edexcel GCSE Medicine Through Time

*Assumptions: three lessons this week, each starting with a 5 to 8 minute retrieval grid done from memory, without notes. Each grid has 6 questions: 2 from this half-term's current topic (the Renaissance), 2 from medieval medicine, 1 from last year (the Norman Conquest) and 1 "link" question that asks students to connect periods. Answers are on the last page. I've kept to what the course expects, so adjust any detail to match your own lessons.*

**How to use:** students write answers in the back of their books or on a mini whiteboard. Mark in a different colour, with the answers read aloud or shown on the board. Anything missed goes on a "to revisit" list.

# Answers

## Grid 1

1. **Andreas Vesalius.** He **dissected human bodies** himself (at the University of Padua) rather than relying on Galen's books, and published detailed drawings.
2. Books and diagrams could be **copied in larger numbers, more cheaply and more accurately** than hand-copied manuscripts, so new ideas and drawings spread more widely across Europe.
3. **Blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile.**
4. Galen's ideas about a single creator (God) fitted Christian teaching, and the Church **controlled education and universities**, so it taught Galen's work as correct and discouraged challenging it.
5. **1066.** **William of Normandy** won.
6. **Any one of:** Vesalius showed Galen had based his ideas on **animal** dissection, not human; he found errors (e.g. in the structure of the jaw bone and breastbone); his work proved that Galen could be wrong.

## Grid 2

1. **The circulation of the blood:** the heart pumps blood round the body in a one-way circuit through the veins and arteries. Published in **1628**.
2. Doctors **could not use it to cure disease**: there were no new treatments that followed from it. Many doctors still believed Galen's ideas, and bloodletting continued. There was also no way to prove everything he said with the technology available.
3. **1348.** Explanations include: **God's punishment**, **miasma** (bad air), the **positions of the planets**, **an imbalance of the humours**, and blaming outsiders such as Jews.
4. **Any two:** bloodletting, purging (making someone vomit or empty their bowels), herbal remedies, prayer, a change of diet (regimen), using astrology to choose when to treat.
5. A **survey of England's land, property and wealth** ordered by William I so he could collect taxes and control the country. Made in **1086**.
6. **Likely answer:** Harvey's ideas were accepted fairly quickly in some circles, but changed treatment slowly. Vesalius challenged Galen more directly. Accept reasoned arguments that include evidence (e.g. Vesalius' book was widely printed; Harvey's work needed experiments that others could copy).

## Grid 3

1. Paré used a **mixture of egg yolk, rose oil and turpentine** on the wound, because he ran out of boiling oil. This was **less painful and soldiers recovered better** than when the wound was cauterised with boiling oil. (He also used **ligatures** rather than cauterisation for amputations.)
2. **Any two:** shutting up infected houses with a red cross, quarantine, killing cats and dogs, carrying a pomander or herbs, lighting bonfires to clean the air, praying and fasting, plague doctors, leaving London, taking herbal remedies or charms.
3. **Any two:** **miasma** (bad air), **God's punishment**, **the position of the planets**, **an imbalance of the humours**, **strangers or beggars** spreading it. (The microbe was not discovered until the 19th century.)
4. Medieval people were strongly **religious**. They believed illness could be a punishment for sin. Responses included **prayer, processions, flagellants** (people whipping themselves), and giving money to the Church.
5. **Any one:** building **castles**, giving land to Norman lords in return for loyalty (feudal system), the **Harrying of the North**, the **Domesday Book**, replacing English nobles with Normans.
6. **Similarity:** In both, people believed in **miasma** and **God's punishment**, and people used prayer and herbs. **Difference:** In 1665 there was more government action (e.g. quarantine rules from the Lord Mayor of London, written orders) than in 1348; also the Great Plague was mainly in London while the Black Death affected the whole country. Accept reasoned answers with evidence.

## Tips for next week

- **Spacing:** carry any questions students got wrong into next week's grid.
- **Rotating in:** once you've finished the Renaissance, add Great Plague / Harvey / Paré to future grids alongside the next topic (the Industrial Revolution, Pasteur and Koch).
- **Low-stakes:** don't record marks. The aim is to practise remembering, not to test.
