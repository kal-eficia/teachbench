# Year 11 revision timetable: 3 weeks to mocks

**Mocks (Week 4):** Maths Paper 1 on Monday, English Language on Tuesday, Biology on Wednesday, History on Thursday.

**Assumptions**
- Home by about 4:00 pm on school days (school ends 3:15). If travel is longer, shift everything 15-30 minutes later.
- Prayer times and family time are fitted around these slots, not squeezed out. Move blocks if a prayer falls in them.
- Football Tue/Thu 5:00-6:30. Mosque class Sat 10:00-12:00.
- Because concentration lasts about 30 minutes, every block is **25 minutes of work + 5 minutes break**. Breaks mean away from the phone: stretch, water, a snack, kick a ball.
- As this is the first time revising, the plan is deliberately modest (about 6-7 hours per week) and built from methods that work: practice questions, flashcards, self-testing, and spacing.

## How the time is divided

## Week-by-week content

### Week 1: Learn it and find the gaps
- **Maths (algebra):** expanding brackets, factorising, solving equations, rearranging formulae. Do 10 questions on each, check answers, and write the ones you got wrong on a "mistakes list".
- **Maths (ratio):** simplifying, sharing in a ratio, ratio with a total, recipes and scale problems.
- **History:** make a one-page timeline of the main events and dates for each topic. Start 10 flashcards a day (date on one side, event and why it matters on the other).
- **Biology:** pick the three biggest topics (for example cells, organisation, infection and response) and turn the notes into question cards.
- **English Language:** read one past paper question set through, with the mark scheme. Practise a Question 2 or 4 (language/structure) and one writing task.

### Week 2: Practise and test
- **Maths:** do topic questions without notes. Redo anything on the mistakes list. Do one section of a past Paper 1 in a quiet space with a timer.
- **History:** self-test with flashcards (cover and recall). Use the "blurt" method: write everything you remember about an event for 3 minutes, then check and add what was missing. Practise one source or interpretation question.
- **Biology:** past paper questions, then mark with the mark scheme. Re-draw key diagrams from memory.
- **English Language:** timed attempts at one reading question and one writing piece. Compare with example answers.

### Week 3: Exam conditions and tidy-up
- **Maths:** one full past Paper 1 timed (Wed or Sun), mark it, and spend the next sessions on the topics that lost marks.
- **History:** timeline from memory, then check. Hardest dates only on flashcards. One exam-style long answer.
- **Biology:** full timed past paper section, plus a final pass on flashcards.
- **English Language:** one full paper in timed conditions. Focus on structure of each answer.
- **Sat/Sun before the mocks:** lighter. Only review mistakes lists and flashcards. Early night.

## How to revise in each 25-minute block (the first-time reviser guide)
1. **Start with a clear task**, for example "10 ratio questions" or "recall 15 dates", not "revise history".
2. **Test yourself.** Close the book and write what you remember. Re-reading alone does not work well.
3. **Check and fix.** Mark the work and write mistakes in a notebook.
4. **Short break.** Phone away during the 25 minutes.

## Specific tips for the two weak subjects
**Maths**
- Do a little every day instead of a long block.
- Keep a "mistakes list" of question types you got wrong and redo them two days later.
- Use worked examples first, then cover the solution and try a similar one.

**History dates and events**
- Flashcards with date on the front and event plus reason on the back.
- Group dates into small stories (cause, event, result) rather than long lists.
- Use a timeline on the wall and add to it each week.
- Test with 10 cards at a time, and put the ones you got wrong back in for the next day (spaced repetition).

## Staying on track
- Tick off each block on the timetable when done.
- If a session is missed, do it in the next free slot, don't pile on extra.
- A parent, carer or friend can quiz the flashcards for 5 minutes. It helps recall.
- Sleep, food and water matter. Do not stay up late the night before an exam.
