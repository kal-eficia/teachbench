# Year 8 Science Starter: Retrieval Quiz, then a Bridge into Acids and Alkalis

**Time:** 8 to 10 minutes. **Equipment:** mini whiteboards (or scrap paper), projector.

## Assumptions
- Mini whiteboards are available. If not, use ABCD cards or a show of fingers for the multiple-choice questions and write the short answers in books.
- You want the starter to do two jobs: fix the two gaps from last week's quiz (compounds vs mixtures, and what happens to particles when a solid melts), and warm up the chemistry ideas that acids and alkalis will build on.
- Cells (a month ago) is left out on purpose. It has no link to tomorrow's topic, and the quiz showed the bigger gaps are elsewhere. Bring it back in a later starter.

## Why these questions
| Q | Topic | Reason |
|---|-------|--------|
| 1 | Melting and particles | About half the class could not explain it last week. Particle model was 3 weeks ago, so it is due a retrieval. |
| 2 | Compounds vs mixtures | Most of the class confused these. |
| 3 | Sorting substances | Applies Q2 straight away, so the idea is used and not just recited. |
| 4 | Word equation (yesterday) | A next-day recall, which is the shaky point on the forgetting curve. |
| 5 | Bridge to acids and alkalis | Uses the word equation and previews neutralisation. |

## Slide text (what students see)

**Write your answers on your whiteboard. No hands up. Boards up when I say.**

**1.** A block of ice melts into water. Describe what happens to the **particles**. Use at least two of these words: *energy, vibrate, forces, move past each other*.

**2.** Finish the sentence: A compound is different from a mixture because...

**3.** Which of these are **compounds**, which are **mixtures**, which are **elements**? Write the letters in three groups.
- A. Sea water
- B. Carbon dioxide
- C. Oxygen
- D. Air
- E. Water (H2O)
- F. Iron
- G. Salt dissolved in water

**4.** Write a word equation for this: magnesium burns in oxygen and makes magnesium oxide. Circle the **reactants**.

**5.** Look at this word equation:
> sodium hydroxide + hydrochloric acid → sodium chloride + water

 a) Name the two reactants.
 b) Is this a chemical or a physical change? Give **one** piece of evidence you would expect to see, or say why you cannot tell.

## Teacher answers

**1.** Particles gain energy (from heating), so they vibrate more. This overcomes the forces holding them in fixed positions, so they can move past each other. They stay the same particles. They do not melt, shrink, grow or change into something new.
- *Watch for:* "the particles melt", "the particles get bigger", "the particles spread out and stop touching". Correct these by name, calmly. Draw the three-state particle diagram if more than a few boards show this.

**2.** A compound is made of two or more different elements **chemically joined (bonded)** in a fixed ratio, and can only be split by a chemical reaction. A mixture is substances that are **not chemically joined**, can be in any amounts, and can be separated by physical methods such as filtering or evaporation.
- *Watch for:* "a compound is where you mix two things". Press with: "What is different about how the atoms are held?"

**3.**
- Elements: C (oxygen), F (iron)
- Compounds: B (carbon dioxide), E (water)
- Mixtures: A (sea water), D (air), G (salt dissolved in water)

Cold-call on D (air). Students often call it a compound, because it contains oxygen and nitrogen. Ask: "Are the oxygen and nitrogen joined together, or just sitting together?"

**4.** magnesium + oxygen → magnesium oxide. Reactants: magnesium and oxygen.
- *Watch for:* arrows pointing the wrong way, or "=" signs, or the product circled.

**5.**
- a) Sodium hydroxide and hydrochloric acid.
- b) Chemical change. Evidence: temperature change is the likely answer (this reaction releases heat). Accept "new substances are made" (sodium chloride and water). Also accept "I cannot tell from the equation alone, but the products are different substances from the reactants, so a new substance has formed". The reaction gives no obvious fizz or colour change, which is a good point to make.

## How to run it
1. **Minutes 0 to 5:** Questions 1 to 4, silent, boards up together after each question so you can scan the whole room. Do not take hands. Name two or three students whose boards show a common error and ask them to explain.
2. **Minutes 5 to 7:** Go through the answers. For Q1 and Q2, ask a student with the **right** answer to explain it in their own words, then ask another student to say it again in different words.
3. **Minutes 7 to 9:** Q5. Take answers to (a) quickly. For (b), pause on "can you tell?" and say: "Today we're going to meet a very common reaction like this one, and see how to spot what's happening."
4. **Close the starter** by writing the lesson title: **Acids and alkalis**.

## If time is short
Keep Q1, Q2 and Q5 and drop Q3 and Q4. Those three cover the quiz gaps and the bridge.

## Follow-up
Note which students got Q1 and Q2 wrong again. Put one of each back into a starter later in the week, and again in about two weeks, rather than reteaching the whole thing now.
