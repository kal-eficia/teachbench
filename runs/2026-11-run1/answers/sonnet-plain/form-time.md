# Year 7 form time: four-week plan for the first term (15 minutes daily)

**Assumptions:** this is a 20-day plan (Monday to Friday). Each day has a clear structure so you are not planning from scratch. Themes: organisation (Week 1), friendships (Weeks 2 and 3, one week on making and keeping friends, one on conflict and kindness), phones (Week 4). Organisation is revisited briefly every Monday. Adapt to your school's own routines (planners, apps, behaviour policy, assemblies).

## Weekly structure

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Monday | Teach the week's key skill (5 min input, 10 min activity) |
| Tuesday | Practise it |
| Wednesday | Reflect and discuss in pairs or trios |
| Thursday | Apply it to a real situation |
| Friday | Check-in, celebrate, plan for the weekend, and one thing that went well |

## Week 1: Getting organised

**Goal:** every student leaves the week with a bag, planner and routine that work.

- **Mon:** Why organisation matters. Show two bags: one tidy, one chaos. Ask what could go wrong with each. Students list three things that regularly go missing (PE kit, homework, pens).
- **Tue:** Timetable and bag. Students colour code their timetable, then pack their bag for tomorrow using a checklist. Pair check.
- **Wed:** Using the planner. Teach how to write homework: subject, task, due date. Practise with three sample entries. Discuss: "What do you do if you don't understand the homework?"
- **Thu:** Evening routine. Students draw a simple plan: school bag by the door, kit ready, charge devices outside the bedroom. Share one idea with a partner.
- **Fri:** Check-in. Ask who has remembered everything this week. Celebrate. Students pick one habit to keep up next week. Offer a lost-property reminder and a "who can I ask for help?" list (form tutor, head of year, homework club).

**Whole-week extras:** keep a spare pen box and a "forgotten kit" system with no shaming. Check planners for a signature each Friday.

## Week 2: Making and keeping friends

**Goal:** students feel less alone and gain practical tools for starting friendships.

- **Mon:** "What makes a good friend?" Students write three qualities on sticky notes, then group them on the board. Agree on a class list of qualities.
- **Tue:** Conversation starters. Teach three ways to join a group or start talking to someone ("Can I join in?", asking about something they like, offering to share). Role-play in threes with an observer.
- **Wed:** Finding people who share interests. Students complete an "interests map" and compare in pairs. Identify clubs at school that match (lunchtime clubs list).
- **Thu:** The ones who are alone. Discuss what you can do if you see someone on their own. Plan a simple, low-pressure invitation. Emphasise that nobody should be forced to be best friends.
- **Fri:** Reflect. "One kind thing I did, or saw, this week." Quick round. Set a challenge: talk to one person you haven't spoken to before.

**Teacher notes:** quietly note students who seem isolated. Use seating plans and paired tasks to mix groups without announcing why.

## Week 3: Friendship problems and kindness

**Goal:** students know the difference between a falling-out and bullying, and what to do about each.

- **Mon:** Normal friendship ups and downs. Scenario cards: a friend left you out; a joke went too far; two friends are fighting and you're in the middle. Discuss in pairs.
- **Tue:** Fixing a falling-out. Teach a simple script: "When you ... I felt ... Can we ...?" Practise in pairs with low-stakes examples.
- **Wed:** Banter, teasing, or bullying? Sort scenario cards into three groups. Discuss repeated behaviour, power imbalance and intent. Explain that bullying can be online as well.
- **Thu:** Who can help? Students create a "who I can talk to" list: friend, form tutor, trusted adult, safeguarding lead, anonymous reporting route (add your school's system). Practise saying "I need to talk to someone."
- **Fri:** Kindness challenge. Each student writes one anonymous positive note for a classmate (you read them first). Hand out at the end of the session.

**Safeguarding:** remind students that if they or someone else is being hurt, they must tell an adult. Follow your school's policy for any disclosure.

## Week 4: Phones and screens

**Goal:** students build healthy habits and know how to respond to online problems.

- **Mon:** Our phone habits. Anonymous class survey (hands up or sticky dots): hours per day, first and last thing they do on their phone, who gets messages at night. Discuss what the data shows.
- **Tue:** Group chats. Role-play: a group chat turns unkind, or someone is left out. Teach how to respond: don't join in, screenshot, tell someone, leave or mute the chat. Discuss why things said in writing feel different.
- **Wed:** Sleep and focus. Explain how notifications and late-night scrolling affect sleep, mood and concentration. Students design a "phone-free" bedtime routine (charging outside the bedroom).
- **Thu:** Keeping safe online. Privacy settings, not sharing photos or location, what to do if a stranger messages, age limits on apps. Link back to Week 3: online unkindness is still bullying.
- **Fri:** Personal pledge. Students write one realistic change (for example "phone charges downstairs at 9pm"). Revisit your Week 1 organisation checklist and add "phone away at school". Send a short note to parents with the pledge idea and a link to reputable advice (such as NSPCC or Internet Matters), after checking your school's policy.

## Resources and routines

- Printed checklist for organisation and timetable.
- Scenario cards for Weeks 3 and 4.
- Anonymous question box on your desk; read and answer a few on Fridays.
- A visible "who can I talk to?" poster.

## Tracking and follow-up

- Keep a simple log of any students who seem isolated, repeatedly forgetful, or affected by a phone-related incident; pass concerns to your head of year or pastoral lead.
- At the end of Week 4, ask students to complete a one-minute feedback slip: "What helped? What would you like more of?" Use it to shape the next four weeks (possibly study habits, stress, or respect).
