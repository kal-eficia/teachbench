# Reception phonics: introducing the digraph 'sh' (20 minutes)

## Assumptions
- Class of 26, whole-class carpet session, following a synthetic phonics programme in which 'sh' follows the single letters and 'ck'.
- Children can blend CVC words with the letters they know. They have not met 'sh'.
- Resources: a grapheme card for 'sh', a picture for the sound (a finger-to-lips "shh" cue), mini whiteboards and pens, a few word cards, and a small bag of objects (shell, ship, shop picture, fish, shed picture, brush).
- Target words use only known letters plus 'sh': **shop, shed, ship, shut, shot, shin, fish, dish, fresh, rush, mash, cash, bash, rush**. Avoid words with unknown graphemes. "Shell" (double l) is fine as a spoken word and an object, not for blending yet.
- Use the programme's pronunciation, a clean "sh" with no added "uh".

## Learning objectives
- Children recognise the grapheme 'sh' and say its sound.
- Children blend and read simple words with 'sh'.
- Children hear and spell 'sh' words with support.

## Session plan

### 1. Revisit and review (4 min)
- Quick grapheme flash with the cards for recently taught sounds, including 'ck', mixed order, at pace.
- Blend two or three CVC words, for example "sock, duck, kick".
- Praise accuracy, and re-teach any shaky sound briefly.

### 2. Teach: the new digraph (5 min)
- Show the card 'sh' and say: "Two letters, one sound. s and h together say sh." Point to each letter, then run a finger under both while saying "sh".
- Use a cue: finger on lips, "shhh, be quiet". Children copy.
- Tell a one-line story or use a picture such as a ship or shell: "sh, sh, sh, the ship goes".
- Oral sound-spotting: teacher says objects from the bag; children say "sh" and hold up a thumb if they hear it at the start or end (ship, fish, cat, shell, dog, shed).
- Show how to write it: s then h, and say "sh" as you write. Children trace in the air, on a partner's back, then on the whiteboard.

### 3. Practise: read (5 min)
- Write or show "shop". Point to 'sh' first, say the sound as one unit, then blend: "sh-o-p, shop".
- Repeat with "ship", "shed", "fish", "dish". Use "Sound-talk" (pointing under letters) then blend. Children say the word.
- Build one more word with letters: swap the end to read "shot, shut". Notice that 'sh' stays as a single sound.
- Quick check: hold up a card and the children whisper-blend it to their partner.

### 4. Apply: write (4 min)
- Say a word, children segment it with fingers: "fish: f-i-sh, three sounds". Show on the fingers that 'sh' is one.
- Children write "fish" and "shop" on whiteboards, with the 'sh' card visible as a prompt. Walk round, correct formation and sound-to-letter matching.
- Use a caption challenge for the quicker ones: "a fish in a dish" (the, a, in, are tricky words they may know).

### 5. Finish (2 min)
- Reveal a word hidden in a bag or behind a card, children read it ("shed"). Last grapheme flash including 'sh'.
- Praise effort and say what they have learned: "Today we met a new sound, sh."

## Differentiation
- **Support:** a smaller word set, pointing together, an adult at the front for a small group, sound mat with picture, and writing over a model.
- **Challenge:** read a caption or a short sentence ("Dash the fish shuts the shed"), or write a word independently.
- Keep seating so children who need extra attention are near the front.

## Assessment
- Observe who can say the sound, who blends the words, and who segments and writes. A quick tick list is enough.
- Follow up the next day with a short revisit and extra practice for those who were unsure.

## Follow-up ideas
- A sound hunt in the classroom or outside for 'sh' items.
- Games in continuous provision: play dough 'sh' letters, a "shop" role-play corner with labels, fishing for 'sh' words with magnetic rods.
- Revisit 'sh' in the next days' review slots, and move on to 'ch' only once 'sh' is secure.
