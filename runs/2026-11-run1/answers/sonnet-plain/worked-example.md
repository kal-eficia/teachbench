## The method

**Get the x terms onto one side, the numbers onto the other. Do the same to both sides each time.**

**Worked example:** Solve 5x + 3 = 2x + 15

| Step | Working | What I did |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5x + 3 = 2x + 15 | Start |
| 2 | 3x + 3 = 15 | Subtract **2x** from both sides (so the x terms are on the left only) |
| 3 | 3x = 12 | Subtract **3** from both sides |
| 4 | x = 4 | Divide both sides by **3** |
| Check | 5(4) + 3 = 23 and 2(4) + 15 = 23 | Both sides match |

**Tip:** subtract the **smaller** x term from both sides. That keeps your x term positive.

## Teaching notes
- Common errors: subtracting from one side only; sign errors when moving a negative term (Q9 to Q12, Q15, Q16); stopping at 3x = 12.
- Q13 is a good moment to discuss fractional answers; Q17 has a negative x term, so some pupils will prefer to add x to both sides.
