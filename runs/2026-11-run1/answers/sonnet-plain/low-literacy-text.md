===
Vaccination is when a doctor puts a tiny amount of a germ into your body. The germ has either been killed or made very weak, so it cannot make you ill.

Your body has white blood cells. These cells see the germ and make special proteins called antibodies. Each antibody fits one kind of germ, like a key fits one lock. The germ has marks on its outside called antigens. The antibodies are made to match those antigens.

Later, the real germ, which is still alive, might get into your body. Your body now has memory cells. These cells remember the germ. They help your body to fight back much faster, and to make many more antibodies than the first time. This stops you from getting ill, or at least stops you feeling the symptoms.

Now think about a whole town or country. If most of the people are vaccinated, the germ finds it hard to spread from one person to the next. This means that people who have not been vaccinated are protected too. This is called herd immunity.
