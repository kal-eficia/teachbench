Here is a message you can paste into WhatsApp. Fill in the square brackets before sending. It is short on purpose, names the specific facts, treats the dip as fixable, and asks for one clear reply.

===
Hi [Parent's name], it's [Your name], [child]'s maths teacher. I wanted to get in touch about something that I think we can sort out quickly if we act now.

[child]'s last two maths assessments have come back at grade 4, down from a 6 earlier this year, and the last three homeworks haven't been handed in. He's capable of getting back to a 6, and I'd rather we catch this early than let it drift.

I run a catch-up session on Tuesdays, 3:30 to 4:15, and I'd really like [child] to come for the next four weeks. We'll go over the topics he's finding tricky and get him back on track with homework.

Could you let me know by [day] whether he can make it? If Tuesdays are a problem, tell me and I'll see what else we can do.

Also, if you've noticed anything at home that might be getting in the way, I'd be glad to hear it. Thanks so much for your support.

[Your name]
===

**Notes on the wording (not part of the message):**
- It opens with a clear "this is fixable" tone, states the two facts once, then moves straight to the action. That keeps it calm without being vague.
- The request is specific (Tuesdays, 3:30 to 4:15, four weeks) and has a reply-by date, so there is something concrete to do.
- The line inviting information from home gives the parent a way to share context without feeling blamed.
- If you would rather not mention a date, "by the end of the week" works. If you want to soften slightly more, change "I'd really like [child] to come" to "I think it would really help if [child] could come".
