# What to give back: Year 10 essays on Priestley's presentation of Mr Birling

*Assumptions: GCSE English Literature, one lesson (about 50 minutes) to work with, and you'd rather not write comments on 28 scripts. Your notes already tell you the picture, so the best use of your time is **whole-class feedback plus a structured improvement task**. Everything below is ready to copy.*

## The plan in one line

Hand back the essays with **no comments, only a tick-box code on each** (see the codes below), then give the class **one feedback sheet** and **one rewrite task**. It should take you about 15 minutes of preparation.

---

## Part 1: The one-page feedback sheet (copy and paste)

### Birling essays: what went well and what to fix

**What you did well**
- Your knowledge of the plot is secure. You know who says what and when.
- Several of you linked Birling to **capitalism**: Priestley presents him as a symbol of the selfish attitudes he wanted to challenge. This is the kind of idea that earns top marks.

**Three things to improve (in priority order)**

**1. Analyse, don't retell.**
Retelling says *what happens*. Analysis says *how Priestley makes us feel about it, and why*.

- Retelling: *Birling tells Eric and Gerald that a man has to look after himself and his family.*
- Analysing: *Priestley gives Birling the line "a man has to make his own way" to present him as selfish and short-sighted: the repeated focus on "his own" self shows how he ignores the responsibility he has towards others.*

Test for each paragraph: **have I said what this shows, and why Priestley wants us to see it?**

**2. Zoom in on the words.**
When you use a quotation, comment on **a single word or phrase**. Ask: What does this word suggest? What is the tone?

- "*unsinkable, absolutely unsinkable*": the repetition and the absolute word "absolutely" show Birling's arrogant over-confidence. The audience knows the Titanic sank, so he looks foolish.
- "*community and all that nonsense*": the dismissive "nonsense" suggests he has no respect for the idea of looking after others.

**3. Add context.**
The play was **written in 1945** but is **set in 1912**. Priestley uses that gap. The audience in 1945 had lived through two world wars and the Depression. So when Birling says there will be no war and the Titanic is safe, the audience *knows* he is wrong. Priestley, a socialist, makes Birling wrong about everything to show that his capitalist views are wrong too.

**Smaller fixes**
- **Paragraphs:** one idea per paragraph. If it's over about 8 lines, ask where the point changes and start a new paragraph. Use PEEL (Point, Evidence, Explain, Link) or your own version.
- **Spelling:** **Priestley** (Priest + ley), **capitalism** (*capital* + *ism*), **responsibility** (*respons* + *ibility*, say it "response-ability").

---

## Part 2: Codes to put on each essay (about 1 minute per script)

Write the codes in the margin or on the front. No other marking is needed.

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Analyse more, I can see retelling |
| **W** | Zoom in on a word or phrase |
| **C** | Add context (1912 vs 1945) |
| **P** | Paragraph too long, split it |
| **Sp** | Spelling: Priestley / capitalism / responsibility |
| **★** | Strong point (mark the best sentence) |

Add a mark or grade if you need one for your records. Underline one great sentence in every essay so each student gets some personal feedback.

---

## Part 3: The lesson (about 45 minutes)

| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Hand back essays. Students find their codes and read the feedback sheet. |
| 8 min | **Retell vs analyse sort.** Show 4 to 6 anonymised sentences (use extracts from the strongest and weakest essays, with names removed). Students decide: retelling or analysis? Then upgrade one. |
| 10 min | **Zoom-in task.** Give them 2 quotations on the board (e.g. "unsinkable, absolutely unsinkable" and "community and all that nonsense"). In pairs, find one word each and write 2 sentences: *what it suggests* + *why Priestley chose it*. |
| 5 min | **Context sentence.** Give a sentence starter: *"Writing in 1945, Priestley presents Birling as ___ because the audience would know that ___."* Everyone writes one. |
| 15 min | **Rewrite one paragraph** from their essay (the one with a code). Aim to include: a point, a short quotation, comment on a single word, and one context link. Keep it to 6 to 8 lines. |
| 2 min | Exit ticket: *"One thing I will do differently next time is ___."* |

**Collect the rewritten paragraphs** and glance at them. A quick tick or a star is enough.

---

## Part 4: Optional quick extras

- **Shout-outs:** read out (anonymised or with permission) two or three of the best analytical sentences. Showing what excellent looks like is more effective than describing it.
- **Spelling:** put the three words on the board as a starter next lesson, and run a 2-minute test.
- **Extension for strong students:** *How does Priestley use Birling to show the dangers of capitalism, and how does the play's structure (the Inspector's arrival) challenge him?*

## Why this works

You've turned 28 individual comments into **one targeted teaching moment** and given every student a specific, doable next step. It saves your time and gives students a reason to read their feedback rather than glance at a grade.
