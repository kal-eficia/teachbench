## PART 1: THE ORGANISER

### 1. Timeline

| 5 Jan 1066 | 25 Sep 1066 | 28 Sep 1066 | 14 Oct 1066 | 25 Dec 1066 | 1086 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| King Edward dies, no children | Battle of Stamford Bridge | William lands at Pevensey | Battle of Hastings | William crowned king | Domesday Book |

### 2. Who wanted the throne?

1. **Harold Godwinson**: the most powerful English earl. Said Edward named him king on his deathbed. The Witan (the king's advisers) chose him.
2. **William, Duke of Normandy**: said Edward had promised him the throne, and that Harold had sworn an oath to support him.
3. **Harald Hardrada**: King of Norway. Claimed the English throne through an old agreement between earlier kings.
4. **Edgar Ætheling**: Edward's young great-nephew. Had royal blood but little support.

### 3. Two battles, three weeks apart

1. **Stamford Bridge (25 Sept):** Hardrada invaded in the north. Harold marched his army north and won. Hardrada was killed.
2. **Hastings (14 Oct):** William landed in the south while Harold's army was far away. Harold hurried south.
3. Harold's army fought on a hill in a **shield wall**. Some Normans pretended to run away, English soldiers chased them, and the line broke.
4. Harold was killed and William won.

### 4. How William kept control

1. **Castles:** the first ones were wooden **motte and bailey** castles, built quickly. A motte is a mound with a tower on top. A bailey is the walled yard below. Later ones were built of stone.
2. **Feudal system:** William owned all the land. He gave land to barons, barons gave land to knights, and peasants worked the land. Each level promised loyalty and service to the one above.
3. **Domesday Book (1086):** a huge survey of who owned what in England, so William knew what to tax. People could not argue with it, like a final judgement day.

### 5. Key words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **claimant** | someone who says they have the right to something |
| **heir** | the person who inherits a title or property |
| **Witan** | the group of important English nobles who advised the king |
| **earl** | a powerful English noble |
| **invasion** | when an army enters another country to take control |
| **shield wall** | soldiers standing close together with shields linked |
| **motte and bailey** | a castle with a mound (motte) and a walled yard (bailey) |
| **feudal system** | a system where land is given in return for loyalty and service |
| **baron** | a noble who held land directly from the king |
| **knight** | a soldier who held land from a baron in return for fighting |
| **peasant** | someone who worked the land for a lord |
| **survey** | a detailed count and record of people, land and property |

### 6. Common mistakes: not this, but this

- **Not** "Harold died at Stamford Bridge." **But** Hardrada died there. Harold died at Hastings.
- **Not** "Hastings was fought in the town of Hastings." **But** near the hill where the town of Battle now is.
- **Not** "The Normans came from Britain." **But** from Normandy, in northern France.
- **Not** "The Domesday Book was about doom." **But** it was a record that could not be argued with.

### 7. Links

*Builds on:* Anglo-Saxon England. *Leads to:* life in a medieval village, and how the Normans changed the Church.

## PART 4: NOTE FOR YOU (not for the organiser)

- Kept to what you listed. I left out the Harrying of the North and the Bayeux Tapestry. Say if you'd like a second page covering either.
- Hastings was fought at Senlac Hill, near what is now the town of Battle. Some teachers also like to name the English earl Tostig (Harold's exiled brother), who joined Hardrada at Stamford Bridge.
- How to use it: students look, cover, write, check, ten minutes three times a week. Use the numbers to quiz: "What's fact 3.2?"
- If you want a quick check on the timeline, ask for dates only, with no events, to see whether they can place them in the right order.
