# Year 10 Biology: end-of-topic results against baseline

## Summary for the head of department

**Assumptions:** the baseline and end-of-topic tests covered the same content at a comparable level of difficulty, and the figures are class averages for one Year 10 group. If the tests were different papers, treat the change as indicative only.

| Topic | Baseline | End of topic | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cells | 38% | 72% | +34 |
| Infection and response | 35% | 65% | +30 |
| Organisation | 40% | 58% | +18 |
| Bioenergetics | 30% | 41% | +11 |
| **Mean of the four** | **35.8%** | **59.0%** | **+23.3** |

**Headline:** the class has made clear progress in all four topics, with an average gain of about 23 percentage points. Cells and Infection and response are in a good place. Organisation has improved but is still below 60%. Bioenergetics has moved least and is the clear priority, at 41%.

**What this does and doesn't tell us**
- Gains are largest where the class started lowest in absolute terms and where the test was closest to teaching. End-of-topic tests measure recent learning, so some of this will fade without retrieval.
- Bioenergetics could be weak because it was taught most recently, because the content is harder (photosynthesis, respiration and the maths of rates), or because of specific misconceptions. I can't tell which from the averages.
- A class average hides the spread. I haven't yet checked how many students are below 40% in each topic.

## What I'll do next

1. **Question-level analysis of Bioenergetics and Organisation (this week).** Find which questions lost the most marks, and separate knowledge gaps (for example, the equations for photosynthesis and respiration) from exam-technique problems (for example, calculation and graph questions).
2. **Reteach Bioenergetics (next 2 to 3 lessons).** Target the specific misconceptions found in step 1 with worked examples and a hinge question for each, then a short low-stakes quiz. Make sure aerobic and anaerobic respiration are clearly distinguished from photosynthesis.
3. **Retrieval for Organisation.** Add 5-question starters across the next fortnight, using questions students missed.
4. **Keep Cells and Infection secure.** Use spaced retrieval questions from both topics as starters, rather than reteaching.
5. **Identify individuals.** List students below 40% in Bioenergetics and Organisation, and offer a short small-group catch-up (two or three 20-minute sessions) with a re-test.
6. **Retest in six weeks.** Use equivalent questions on all four topics to check whether gains have held, and report the result to you.

**Success measure:** Bioenergetics at 55% or above on the retest, Organisation at 65% or above, and Cells and Infection not dropping more than a few points.

**Support I may need:** a short window to share the question analysis with the department, so we can see if the Bioenergetics gap appears in other classes too.
