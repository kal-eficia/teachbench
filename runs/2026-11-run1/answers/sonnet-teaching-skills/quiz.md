# Year 7 Geography: Rivers Check-In Quiz

**Assumptions:** This is a low-stakes retrieval check, taken from memory, about 10 minutes. Rivers were finished two weeks ago, so some students may have forgotten the vocabulary, and the questions test understanding as well as terms. The questions are mixed up (not in unit order), so neighbouring questions don't give each other away. The answer key is the last line of this document.

## Teacher notes: what each question checks and what the wrong answers reveal

*(Correct answers are shown in bold. The answer key is at the end of the document.)*

| Q | Topic and where it fits | Correct | What the wrong options reveal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Source to mouth | **C** | A = confuses mouth with source. B = confuses mouth with a confluence. D = vague idea that the mouth is just "the big part". |
| **2** | Erosion: hydraulic action | **A** | B = confuses with abrasion. C = confuses with solution (corrosion). D = confuses with attrition. |
| **3** | Transportation | **D** | A = suspension is fine material carried in the water. B = solution is dissolved material. C = saltation is small stones bouncing along the bed. Students may mix up the terms. |
| **4** | Deposition | **B** | A = thinks more energy leads to more deposition (the reverse of what happens). C = confuses deposition with a landform. D = thinks deposition only happens at the start. |
| **5** | Landforms: meanders | **C** | A = swaps outside and inside bends, a very common error (inside bend = deposition, slip-off slope). B, D = no sense of how flow varies across the channel. |
| **6** | Landforms: waterfalls | **A** | B = reverses hard and soft rock. C = no sense of rock layers. D = confuses a waterfall with a river reaching a plain. |
| **7** | Flooding: causes | **B** | A, D = factors that *reduce* flooding (interception, absorbing water). C = students may think dry soil means flooding, but light rain on dry sandy soil is absorbed. |
| **8** | Source to mouth: upper course | **D** | A = lower or middle course. B = lower course. C = mouth feature. |
| **9** | Flooding: management | **A** | B = soft engineering (works with nature). C = a warning system, also soft engineering. D = soft engineering. Students may think any action to reduce flooding counts as "hard". |
| **10** | Erosion: attrition | **C** | A = solution dissolves rock, doesn't round pebbles. B = deposition drops material but doesn't change the pebble shape. D = hydraulic action breaks rock banks, not pebble shape. |
| **11** | Landforms: ox-bow lakes | **D** | A = confuses with plunge pool. B, C = no sense of how a meander changes over time. |
| **12** | Source to mouth: long profile | **B** | A = reversed. C = students may think wider means shallower. D = ignores the effect of tributaries and extra water downstream. |

**Coverage:** source to mouth (1, 8, 12), erosion (2, 10), transportation (3), deposition (4), landforms (5, 6, 11), flooding (7, 9).

**Where it was taught:** Match to your scheme (the rivers unit finished two weeks ago). Questions 1 and 8 are the oldest ideas in the unit (an early lesson), so they test what has faded most.

**After the quiz (about 5 minutes):**
1. Students check from the board in a different colour pen and correct.
2. Tally the **two most-missed questions** and put them in your next starter or "do now".
3. Look at any question where students were "certain" but wrong. These are firm misconceptions worth a quick reteach.
