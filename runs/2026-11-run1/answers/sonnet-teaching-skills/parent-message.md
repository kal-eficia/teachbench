Here is the message, 66 words, so it reads in one glance on a phone.

===
Hi [parent name], I want to help [child] get back to his best in maths. His last two assessments were a grade 4 (down from a 6) and three homeworks are outstanding. I run a catch-up session on Tuesdays, 3:30 to 4:15, where we'll tackle both together. I'd like him to come for the next four weeks. Could you reply YES to confirm? Thanks, [your name]
===

**Why it's written this way**

- It opens with the shared aim, then gives the facts plainly, then offers a plan. That keeps it calm without hiding the grade drop.
- There is one ask: reply YES. Everything else is information.
- It makes clear who does what: you run the session and sort the homework with him; the parent only confirms.

**Optional:** add "by Friday" after "reply YES" if you need a firm date. If [child] hasn't replied or arrived by the first Tuesday, a quick word with him in person works better than a second message.
