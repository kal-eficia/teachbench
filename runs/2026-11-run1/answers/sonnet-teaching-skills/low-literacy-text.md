===
A vaccine puts a tiny amount of a pathogen into your body. A pathogen is a germ that can make you ill. The germs in a vaccine are inactive, which means switched off, or they are weakened. Weakened germs are also called attenuated.

Your white blood cells find the germs. Every germ has marks on its surface called antigens. Your white blood cells make antibodies that match these antigens. Each kind of antibody only matches one kind of antigen.

Your body also makes memory cells. They remember the germ.

Later, the real, live germ might get into your body. Your memory cells know it straight away. They make a lot more antibodies, and they make them much faster than the first time. This is called the secondary immune response. It is quick and strong, so you do not get ill.

Now think about a whole town or country. If lots of people have the vaccine, the germ finds it hard to spread. People who have not had the vaccine are protected too. This is called herd immunity.
===

**Glossary (words to keep):**
- **Pathogen:** a germ that can make you ill.
- **White blood cells:** cells in your blood that fight germs.
- **Antigen:** a mark on the outside of a germ.
- **Antibody:** something your body makes to match an antigen.
- **Memory cells:** cells that remember a germ.
- **Secondary immune response:** the fast, strong reaction the second time your body meets the germ.
- **Herd immunity:** protection for everyone when most people are vaccinated.
