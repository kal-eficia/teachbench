# Year 3 Maths: Column addition with one exchange (47 + 36)

**Assumptions:** 60 minutes, 30 children, paper-based with Dienes (base-10) equipment, working in pairs for the practical part. 15 sets of equipment needed (each: 9 tens rods, 15 ones cubes). Mini-whiteboards and A, B, C, D answer cards for the check. The class is secure with adding tens and ones mentally without crossing a ten. Written for the National Curriculum Year 3 objective: add numbers with up to three digits using formal written methods. I use the word **exchange** (not "carry") and write the exchanged ten **below the answer line, in the tens column**. Change this if your school's calculation policy puts it elsewhere.

This pack has five parts: **A** teacher plan, **B** pupil pages (print as a booklet), **C** homework, **D** mark scheme, **E** one-screen solutions sheet. Question numbers match across all five.

---

# A. TEACHER PLAN

## Objective
- *I can add two 2-digit numbers using column addition, exchanging 10 ones for 1 ten when I need to.*

**Success criteria (child-friendly):**
- I line up my tens and ones.
- I add the ones first.
- If I have 10 or more ones, I exchange 10 ones for 1 ten.
- I add the tens, including the ten I exchanged.

## Resources
Dienes (15 sets of 9 tens, 15 ones) · place value mats · mini-whiteboards and pens · ABCD cards · printed pupil booklet · class solutions sheet (E) · a large place value chart on the board · 8 sort cards (from page 4) per pair

## Timeline

| Time | Part | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 8 | **Starter** | Pupil page 1. Recall that 13 = 1 ten and 3 ones. |
| 8 to 22 | **Teach** | Model 47 + 36 with Dienes, then drawn, then in columns |
| 22 to 27 | **Check** | Two hinge questions with ABCD cards |
| 27 to 52 | **Apply** | Sort, then faded examples, then independent |
| 52 to 58 | **Exit** | Pupil page 5 |
| 58 to 60 | Tidy, share | Return equipment; one pupil explains "exchange" |

## Starter (8 minutes), recall first
Pupils answer S1 to S5 on whiteboards, **show me** on a count of three. Use a traffic-light dot at the end of each answer: green (certain), amber (think so), red (guessing). Look for children with a green dot and a wrong answer.

The point of S1 to S3 is that **7 + 6 makes 13, which is 1 ten and 3 ones**. This is the idea the rest of the lesson needs. If many children are slow here, spend two more minutes with Dienes before the teach.

## Teach (14 minutes)
**Hook:** "Last lesson you added without crossing a ten. Today the ones will be too many to fit. What do you think we do?"

**1. Concrete (with Dienes, together).** Pairs build 47 on one half of their mat and 36 on the other.
- Teacher: "Put all the ones together. How many?" *(13)* "Can 13 ones stay as ones? A column can only hold nine. So we **exchange** 10 ones for 1 ten rod."
- Pupils swap 10 ones for 1 ten. "How many ones are left?" *(3)* "How many tens now?" *(4 + 3 + the new 1 = 8)* "So 47 + 36 = 83."

**2. Pictorial.** Draw it on the board: tens as lines, ones as dots. Circle 10 dots and draw an arrow to a new line. Pupils copy on whiteboards.

**3. Abstract (columns).** Write 47 + 36 in columns, step by step, saying out loud what you're doing:
```
   4 7
 + 3 6
 -----
   8 3
   1
```
- "Ones first. 7 + 6 = 13. I write the 3 in the ones column. I exchange the ten: I write a small 1 **under the tens column**."
- "Now the tens: 4 + 3 + the 1 = 8. So 83."
- Say it every time: **"10 ones exchange for 1 ten."**

**Say the teaching point, then ask a question.** After each step ask: "What did I do with the 13?" *(wrote 3, exchanged 10 ones for 1 ten)*.

## Check (5 minutes), hinge questions
Use pupil page 3. Children hold up A, B, C or D. The **wrong** options are real mistakes, so read the class:
- **H1 (answer C):** 73 means they forgot the exchanged ten. If most choose A, the problem is adding the tens. If B or D, go back to place value.
- **H2 (answer A):** 713 means they wrote 13 into the ones place.

**If 80%+ are right on both:** release to Apply. **If fewer:** do one more example together (28 + 15) with Dienes before releasing. Split the class: confident children go to practice; keep a group on the carpet for 5 minutes.

## Apply (25 minutes)
**1. Sort (5 min), pairs.** Does each sum need an exchange? Don't calculate fully; look at the ones. *(Page 4, A1)*

**2. Faded examples (10 min).**
- **A2: 28 + 15, we do.** Teacher leads, children fill in the boxes.
- **A3: 35 + 27, half done.** Children finish the missing steps in pairs.
- **A4 to A6: on their own.** Children use Dienes or drawings if they need to, and then move to columns.

**3. Independent and reasoning (10 min).**
- **A7 (reasoning):** "Ali says 24 + 35 needs an exchange. Is he right?"
- **A8 (word problem).**
- **A9 (stretch):** find all the pairs. For quicker children.

**You do not need children to mark their own work.** Circulate and use the **solutions sheet (E)**. Look at each child's work, say "show me how you exchanged", and tick. Don't hand out the answers.

## Exit (5 minutes)
Pupil page 5. E1 (calculation) and E2 (sentence). Collect on the way out. E1 correct (65) and E2 right means they can move on. E1 answered 55 (forgot the exchanged ten) means start tomorrow with Dienes.

## Misconceptions to listen for

| What you'll see | What's happening | What to say |
|---|---|---|
| 47 + 36 = **73** | Forgot the exchanged ten | "Where did your ten from the ones go? Show me with the rods." |
| 47 + 36 = **713** | Wrote 13 in the ones column | "Can a column hold 13? What do we do with 10 ones?" |
| Numbers misaligned | Ones under tens | "Ones under ones, tens under tens", use a place value mat or squared paper |
| Tens added first | Left-to-right habit | "Ones first, because that's where the exchange comes from." |
| The small 1 put in the wrong column | Notation | "The ten belongs with the tens." |

## Differentiation
- **Support:** keep working with Dienes on a place value mat longer. Give them pre-drawn column templates with the exchange box already marked. Stop at A5. Pair them with a confident talker.
- **Stretch:** A9, then "Is it always true that if the ones add to more than 9 you need to exchange? Prove it."
- **Do not** move children on to 3-digit numbers today; a secure exchange with 2-digit numbers is the objective.

## Next lesson's starter
Pick out the children who made a "forgot the ten" mistake and give them 3 questions with a "sticky" exchange (e.g. 38 + 25) at the start. Follow it with spaced practice on 3-digit numbers with no exchange.

## Page 2: Learn

**Look at 47 + 36.**

First we use our rods and cubes. 47 is 4 tens and 7 ones. 36 is 3 tens and 6 ones.

Put the ones together. 7 + 6 = 13. That's too many ones for one column!

**We exchange 10 ones for 1 ten.** Now there are 3 ones left, and one extra ten.

Count the tens: 4 + 3 + 1 = 8.

```
   4 7
 + 3 6
 -----
   8 3
   1
```

**So 47 + 36 = 83.**

**Remember: ones first. 10 ones exchange for 1 ten.**

**My summary:** *When my ones make more than 9, I ___ .*
