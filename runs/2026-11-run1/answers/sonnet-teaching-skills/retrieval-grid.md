# Retrieval grid: Edexcel GCSE History, Medicine Through Time (Year 10)

**Use:** start of lesson, 6 to 8 minutes, from memory, no notes. Students answer on mini whiteboards or in books and rate confidence (G = guessing, T = think so, C = certain). The grid mixes this week's content, earlier this half-term, a thread across periods, and last year's Norman Conquest (spaced retrieval). Questions in neighbouring squares are on different topics.

**Assumptions:** Edexcel 1HI0 Paper 1, Medicine in Britain c1250 to present. The grid fits one lesson (12 squares, ask 8 to 10) or can be split across three lessons this week (suggested split at the bottom).

---

## The grid (student version)

| **1.** Name two things Vesalius showed that Galen had got wrong. | **2.** In what year did the Black Death reach England? | **3.** Who was Harald Hardrada, and what happened to him in 1066? | **4.** What did William Harvey show about the heart and blood? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **5.** Give two ways people tried to stop the Great Plague in 1665. | **6.** What were the Four Humours? Name two. | **7.** What was Ambroise Pare's new method for treating gunshot wounds? | **8.** Give one cause and one effect of the Domesday Book (1086). |
| **9.** Give two reasons why medieval people blamed the Black Death on miasma or God. | **10.** State one change and one continuity in treatments between the medieval period and the Renaissance. | **11.** Why was the printing press important for medicine? | **12.** Explain one reason why Harvey's discovery did not change treatment straight away. |

**Confidence:** write G / T / C next to each answer.

---

## Teacher answers (with the reason for each question)

| # | Model answer | Why it is here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Any two: the lower jaw is one bone (not two); the breastbone has three parts; the human thigh bone is straight (Galen's was based on animals); the heart has no holes in the septum. Vesalius dissected human bodies (1543). | This week's content; high-risk for confusion with Galen's work. |
| 2 | 1348 (reached Britain via Dorset, in the south-west, in summer 1348). | Medieval content, due for a spaced check. |
| 3 | King of Norway; he invaded England in Sept 1066 and was defeated and killed by Harold Godwinson at the Battle of Stamford Bridge. | Last year's content: spaced retrieval from 12 months ago. |
| 4 | The heart is a pump; blood is pumped around the body in a circuit, and the same blood circulates (it isn't made and used up as Galen said); valves make it flow in one direction (published 1628). | This week; common mix-up with Galen. |
| 5 | Any two: quarantine (shutting up houses with a red cross); killing cats and dogs; cleaning streets; burning fires; carrying herbs or pomanders; pest houses; avoiding crowds; prayer. | This week; plague 1665 versus Black Death 1348: students mix these. |
| 6 | Blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile. Illness was due to an imbalance. | Medieval content (and Greek roots), foundation for Renaissance change/continuity. |
| 7 | Using ligatures (tying blood vessels) instead of burning with hot oil; he treated wounds with a soothing ointment (egg yolk, rose oil, turpentine) instead of boiling oil. | This week; Pare is often forgotten. |
| 8 | Cause: William wanted to know what the land and wealth of England were, to tax and control it. Effect: gave the king a record of who owned what / allowed taxation. | Last year; short spaced retrieval of a different topic. |
| 9 | Any two: the Church taught that disease was a punishment from God; people believed in bad air (miasma); belief in the position of the planets; no knowledge of germs; some blamed outsiders. | Medieval content with explanation, not just recall. |
| 10 | Change: new ideas about anatomy (Vesalius) and circulation (Harvey), and the Royal Society; continuity: still using bleeding and purging, and the Four Humours were still believed by many; the role of the Church lessened but still strong. | Links periods; looks ahead to change and continuity questions in the exam. |
| 11 | It spread new ideas and books (such as Vesalius's) faster and more cheaply. | Renaissance factor, leads into the "factors" thread. |
| 12 | Any one: no new treatment came from it; Galen's ideas were still respected; many doctors continued to use bleeding; people still thought illness was caused by humours; lack of new technology. | Evaluation of significance; helps with longer exam questions. |

**Check for wrong answers:** (a) mixing up Black Death 1348 and Great Plague 1665; (b) saying Harvey discovered blood flow *in* the body for the first time (Galen had a model; Harvey changed it); (c) confusing Vesalius, Harvey and Pare; (d) "William of Normandy was king in 1066" (Harold Godwinson was).

## Follow-up
- Mark confidence against correctness. Anyone who writes C and gets it wrong has a misconception: tackle it that lesson.
- Re-ask any square under 60 percent correct in a week's time.
- Add a square from this week's lessons to next week's grid.
