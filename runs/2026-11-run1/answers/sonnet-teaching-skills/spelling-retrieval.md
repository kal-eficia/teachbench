# Year 2 spelling: five 10-minute mornings (ge / dge, then kn / gn)

**Assumptions:** you have mini whiteboards and pens, a set of word cards, and a screen or board to reveal answers. The children have already met all the words, so every morning starts with trying to remember before anyone sees the answer. No copying from the board at any point: copying feels like learning but doesn't build memory.

**Core words (10):** badge, edge, bridge, age, huge, change, knock, know, gnaw, gnat.
**Transfer words (for Thursday):** judge, hedge, fudge, cage, large, stage, knit, gnome.

**The rule to teach in kid-friendly words:** If the /j/ sound comes straight after a short vowel sound, we use **dge** (badge, edge, bridge). After a long vowel sound or a consonant, we use **ge** (age, huge, change). For kn and gn, the first letter is silent: say the second letter's sound.

**Why this works:** each morning begins with recall, not re-reading. The words come back on days 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 in different forms (sorting, dictation, error spotting, quizzes), and the two spelling families are mixed together so the children must choose, not just repeat. Mistakes are fixed by writing the word again from memory, never by copying.

---

## Monday: meet the rule and try it

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | **Brain dump.** On whiteboards, write any kn or gn words you remember from before. No help. Reveal the list (knock, know, gnaw, gnat) and fix any wrong ones from memory. |
| 2-6 | **Sort.** Show six cards one at a time, in this order: badge, huge, age, edge, bridge, change. Children sort into "dge" and "ge" on their boards. Then ask: "What sound is just before the j sound? Is it short or long?" Tap out short and long vowels together. Say the rule aloud as a class. |
| 6-10 | **Cover, write, check.** Say a word in a sentence, children write it, then you reveal it. They fix mistakes in a different colour. Order: change, badge, age, bridge, huge, edge. End by telling a partner the rule. |

**Quick note for you:** write down who sorted something wrongly. They are your priority for Tuesday.

---

## Tuesday: pull it back from memory

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | **Clue recall.** Say a clue only, children write the word on their boards. Clues in this order: "very, very big" (huge), "you wear it on your jumper" (badge), "to make something different" (change), "how old you are" (age), "you cross the river on it" (bridge), "the side of a table" (edge). |
| 3-6 | **Check and fix.** Show the words. Children circle the tricky part (dge or ge) in a colour. For any they missed: write it, fold over, write it again. |
| 6-10 | **Mix the families.** Say these eight in this order: knock, badge, gnat, age, know, bridge, gnaw, huge. Then show two choices each and children pick the right one: *nock / knock*, *bridge / brige*, *huj / huge*, *nat / gnat*. Recalling first, then choosing, is the order that matters. |

---

## Wednesday: use the words in sentences

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | **Beat your score.** One minute: write as many ge / dge words as you can remember. Count them. Then one minute for kn / gn words. Everyone aims to beat their last total. |
| 3-7 | **Dictation.** Say each sentence once whole, then in chunks, then once whole again. Children write, then reread. (1) Did you know the bridge is huge? (2) A gnat sat on the edge of my badge. (3) Knock, then change your badge. (4) Dogs gnaw bones at any age. Show the sentences and children tick each target word they got right. |
| 7-10 | **Error detective.** Show these six sentences, each with one wrong spelling. Children find it and write it correctly before you reveal anything: "Can you nock on the door?" / "My badg is shiny." / "That tree is hug!" / "I no the answer." / "A nat bit my arm." / "Be careful at the edj of the road." Ask: "How do you know it is wrong?" |

---

## Thursday: teach it to someone else and try new words

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | **Card choice.** Say a word; children hold up a DGE or GE card. Words in this order: huge, edge, badge, change, age, bridge. Then quick: "Which letter is silent?" for knock, gnat, know, gnaw. |
| 3-7 | **Partner quiz.** Pairs with a pile of face-down word cards. One reads, the other spells it aloud letter by letter and says why (for example, "dge because the e is short"). Swap after two minutes. |
| 7-10 | **New words.** Say these and let children use the rule to decide how to spell them on their boards: cage, judge, hedge, large, stage, fudge. Bonus for quick finishers: knit, gnome. Reveal and talk about why. This shows the rule works beyond the class list. |

---

## Friday: quiz, then plan the next recall

| Min | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-6 | **Low-stakes quiz.** Ten words said in a sentence, in this order: gnat, bridge, know, huge, edge, knock, age, badge, gnaw, change. After each, children put a green dot (sure) or an orange dot (not sure) above the word. |
| 6-9 | **Check.** Reveal the answers. Children tick or circle the tricky part. For every miss, write it right, hide it, write it again. Notice any green-dot words that were wrong: they are the ones to watch. |
| 9-10 | **Tally.** On a class grid (or just a note to yourself) record which words most children missed. |

**Bring it back later:** put the missed words into a two-minute retrieval at the start of next Monday, and again the following week. That spacing is what turns this week's practice into long-term memory.

---

## What to expect and what to say

| Common mistake | What to say |
|---|---|
| "brige", "baj", "edj" | "Is the vowel short? Then it needs the dge, the d helps the vowel stay short." |
| "hudge" or "chandge" | "That sound comes after a long vowel (huge) or after the n (change), so no d is needed." |
| "nock", "nee", "nat" | "Say it the old-fashioned way: k-nock, g-nat. The first letter is hiding but it is still there." |
| Correct on Monday, wrong on Wednesday | That is normal and useful: it shows recall is working. Re-teach briefly, then retest the same day. |
