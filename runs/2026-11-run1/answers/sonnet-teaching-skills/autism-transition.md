# Preparing an autistic Year 7 student for a cover teacher (two days) and a museum trip

**Assumptions:** UK secondary school. I haven't seen his support plan, so check it and talk to his parents and SENCO (and to him) first; they will know what works for him. Below, "S" is the student. Everything here is a menu: pick what fits him.

**The principle:** change is hard mainly because of **unpredictability**. Your job is to turn "unknown" into "known": who, what, when, where, how long, what happens if, and what happens after. Tell him early, show it visually, and give him some control.

---

## 1. Timeline

| When | What to do |
|---|---|
| **As soon as you know (ideally 1 to 2 weeks before)** | Tell S's parents and SENCO about both changes. Ask what has helped before and what his triggers are. Agree who tells S, and when (some students need days of warning; some become more anxious with too long). |
| **5 to 7 days before** | Tell S in person, privately, calmly, in plain words. Give him a written or visual version. Start with what will **stay the same**. |
| **2 to 3 days before** | Meet the cover teacher (or see a photo and name). Preview the trip with pictures and the itinerary. Walk through his questions. |
| **Day before** | Quick recap. Give him a one-page plan for the next day. Reassure: "You know where to go and who to ask." |
| **During** | A named adult checks in. He has an exit plan. |
| **After** | Short debrief. What went well? What was hard? Record it. |

---

## 2. Cover teacher: two days

### Tell S (suggested script)
> "On Monday and Tuesday, Mr Khan will teach your lessons because I'll be away. Mr Khan is a teacher here and knows what you've been learning. The timetable is the same: same rooms, same times, same lessons. Here's a card with Mr Khan's name and photo. If you're unsure of anything, you can ask him or go to Ms Ali. I'll be back on Wednesday."

Key points: **what is staying the same**, who it is, how long, when you're back, who to go to.

### Give him
- **A visual timetable for both days** (with the cover teacher's name beside each lesson).
- **A card** with the cover teacher's name and photo (ask the cover teacher if they are comfortable with this).
- **A safe person and place**: a named adult and a quiet place if he gets overwhelmed.

### Brief the cover teacher (one page)
Include: S's name, what helps, what to avoid, who to contact.
- Give S **clear, literal instructions**. Avoid sarcasm and idioms ("pull your socks up").
- **Don't change the seating plan or routines** (or warn S first).
- Say what the lesson will be, how long, and what comes next.
- Don't call on him to answer without warning. Give a little time to respond.
- If he's upset: stay calm, lower your voice, give space, offer the quiet place, and contact [named adult].
- **Don't treat an anxious response as defiance.**
- Praise specific effort quietly, not in front of the class.

### Make the cover lessons predictable
Leave work that follows the usual structure and routines (same starter, same way of working). Put the lesson plan on the board at the start, as you normally do.

---

## 3. The museum trip

### Collect information now
Ask the museum or find online: the route, the timetable, where the toilets, quiet spaces and exits are, what's noisy or crowded, lighting, any loud exhibits or hand dryers, lunch location, and the cost. Many museums offer **quiet times, sensory maps, a visual story, or a pre-visit** for autistic visitors. Ask about this.

### Build a "social story" or trip guide (one or two pages, with photos)
Include:
1. **Where we're going** and why.
2. **How we get there** (bus or train, how long, who sits where).
3. **A timeline of the day** with approximate times (arrive, tour, lunch, return).
4. **What it will be like** (noise, crowds, lights, smells), with photos or a virtual tour.
5. **What I need to bring** (packed lunch, water, headphones).
6. **What the rules are** (stay with the group, what to do if you're lost).
7. **What I can do if I feel worried** (tell my adult; ask for a break; go to the quiet space).
8. **When it finishes and what happens next** (return to school at 3 pm, then home as normal).

Use short, literal sentences, in the first person if it helps ("At the museum, I will be with my group.").

### Plan the logistics
- **Group and adult:** agree who he is with (a friend, if he chooses) and a named adult he knows.
- **Role:** give him a job, such as photographer, map-reader, or task-checker. This gives structure.
- **Task:** if there is a worksheet, make it clear with a finish line ("find 5 objects"), not open-ended.
- **Breaks:** plan one or two scheduled breaks and a quiet place. Agree a signal or card.
- **Sensory:** allow headphones, a hat or sunglasses, and a fidget item.
- **Transport:** seat near the front, or a seat he knows in advance.
- **Food:** make sure he knows when and where lunch happens and what is available.
- **Changes on the day:** if the plan changes (a gallery is closed, the bus is late), tell him straight away, calmly and with an alternative. Have a plan B ready and share it in advance.
- **Exit plan:** agree what happens if he can't continue (an adult goes with him to a quiet space or home early; parents contacted).

### Practise before
- Look at photos, a virtual tour, or a video of the museum with him.
- Rehearse the bus trip or the arrival if possible.
- Let him ask questions and write them down. Answer honestly, including "I don't know yet; I'll find out."

---

## 4. Talk to S himself

- Ask: "What would help most?" and "What are you worried about?" Write his answers down.
- Give him **choices** (not whether it happens, but how): which friend, which job, which seat.
- Say clearly that being worried is OK and that adults will help.
- Avoid saying "don't worry"; say what is going to happen and what will help.

---

## 5. Involve parents and the SENCO

- Share the plan, the visual timetable and the trip guide with parents before S sees them (or together).
- Ask parents to practise the trip story at home, and tell you about any concerns.
- Check whether S needs a risk assessment, a 1:1 adult, or specific arrangements.
- Make sure the trip leader and staff are briefed.

---

## 6. After

- **Debrief within a day:** "What went well? What was hard? What would help next time?" Use pictures or a scale if talking is hard.
- **Record** what worked in his support plan, to use for the next change.
- **Praise** specifically ("You handled the bus on your own. That's hard.").
- **Reset:** back to normal routines straight away, so the return is predictable too.
