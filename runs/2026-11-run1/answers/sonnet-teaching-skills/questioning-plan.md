# Year 8: Finding the nth Term of a Linear Sequence, Questioning Plan

**The problem with "Does everyone understand?"** It gets the same three confident children answering, and tells you nothing about the rest. This plan replaces it with questions where every student answers at once, then you pick who to ask.

## Routines to use all lesson

- **Ask, pause, name.** Ask the question, wait 3 to 5 seconds so everyone thinks, then choose a student. No hands up for checking questions.
- **Mini-whiteboards, all show at once** ("3, 2, 1, show me"). Scan the room before you say anything.
- **Wrong answers are useful.** Say "Thank you, that's helpful." Then ask: "Who can tell me what they think about that, and why?"
- **Say it again, better.** After a partial answer: "Can you improve that using the word *term* or *difference*?"
- Replace "Does everyone understand?" with: "Show me the nth term for this sequence on your whiteboard."

## Lesson phase 1: Retrieval starter (5 minutes)

| Question | Purpose | Who to ask | Expected answer | Likely wrong answer / follow-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "What is the term-to-term rule for 4, 9, 14, 19?" | Reminds what a sequence is | A student who rarely speaks | Add 5 | "5" without "add": ask "Add or multiply, and how do you know?" |
| "What is the value of 3n + 2 when n = 5?" | Substitution, needed for nth term | Whiteboards, then a named student | 17 | 3 + 2 + 5 = 10, or 35 + 2: ask "What does 3n mean?" |
| "What does the *n* stand for?" | The key idea: position | Cold-call a mid-attaining student | The position of the term (1st, 2nd...) | "A number": "Which number, and how do you know?" |

## Lesson phase 2: After the first worked example (hinge question 1, 1 minute)

**Hinge 1:** The nth term of a sequence is 4n - 1. What is the 10th term?
- A. 40
- B. 39
- C. 14
- D. 41

**Correct: B.**
- A (40): ignored the -1.
- C (14): added 4 + 10.
- D (41): added 1 instead of subtracting.

**Decision rule:** If 80%+ choose B, move on. If many choose A, go back to substituting and show 4 × 10 = 40, then -1. If C, go back to what 4n means.

## Lesson phase 3: Finding the nth term (explicit teaching, then hinge 2)

**Probing questions during the teaching:**
- "The sequence goes up by 4 each time. What does that tell us about the nth term?" (It starts with 4n.)
- "Why does the *n* have a 4 in front of it?" (Because the sequence goes up in 4s.)
- "How do you know your answer is right?" (Check with the 1st and 2nd terms.)

**Hinge 2:** Which is the nth term of 7, 11, 15, 19?
- A. 4n
- B. n + 4
- C. 3n + 4
- D. 4n + 3

**Correct: D.**
- A (4n): stopped after finding the difference.
- B (n + 4): used the term-to-term rule as the nth term.
- C (3n + 4): swapped the difference and the number you add.

**Decision rule:** If most choose D, move on. If A is popular, reteach "difference, then adjust": compare 4n with the sequence (4, 8, 12, 16) and ask what must be added. If B, contrast term-to-term and position-to-term rules.

## Lesson phase 4: Decreasing sequences (hinge 3)

**Hinge 3:** Which is the nth term of 20, 17, 14, 11?
- A. -3n + 23
- B. 3n + 17
- C. -3n + 20
- D. -3n

**Correct: A.**
- B (3n + 17): ignored that the sequence goes down.
- C (-3n + 20): used the first term as the number to add.
- D (-3n): stopped at the difference.

**Decision rule:** If many pick C, check n = 1 on the board: -3 + 20 = 17, not 20. Show the zero term idea (what would term 0 be?).

**Probing follow-up:** "What would the 0th term of this sequence be?" (23.)

## Lesson phase 5: Independent practice (cold-call questions while circulating)

Ask three students per row of the room, choosing different students each time, not volunteers.
- "Tell me what you've done for this one."
- "Why did you write -3n and not 3n?"
- "How could you check?"

## Lesson phase 6: Stretch question (whole class thinking, then cold call)

"Is 99 in the sequence 4n + 3? How do you know?" (Solve 4n + 3 = 99: n = 24, so yes.)
Follow-up: "Is 100 in it?" (4n + 3 = 100 gives n = 24.25, so no.)
Ask students to talk to a partner for 30 seconds first, then name a student.

## Lesson phase 7: Exit ticket (all answer, 3 minutes)

1. Find the nth term of 5, 8, 11, 14. (3n + 2)
2. Find the nth term of 30, 26, 22, 18. (-4n + 34)
3. One sentence: "To find the nth term, first I ____, then I ____."

Sort the tickets into three piles: secure, nearly there, and needs reteaching. Start the next lesson with the middle pile's most common error.

## Hinge key check

Correct positions across the three hinge questions: **B, D, A**. No repeats, runs or staircases, and each position is used once or not at all.

*Assumptions: 50 to 60 minute lesson, mini-whiteboards available, students have already met substitution and term-to-term rules. In an online lesson, use the chat: everyone types, then presses enter together.*
