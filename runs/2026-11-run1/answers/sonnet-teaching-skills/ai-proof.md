# Year 9 History Homework: Causes of the First World War

**The problem with the current task:** if a student pastes "Write an essay explaining the causes of the First World War" into an AI tool, they skip all the learning. The essay is the product, but the thinking (choosing, weighing and connecting causes) is what you want them to practise. Banning AI or trying to detect it doesn't work reliably, so the better fix is to redesign the task so the thinking has to happen in their own head, and then let AI be useful at the right moment.

**Assumptions:** Year 9 have covered the long-term causes (militarism and the naval race, alliances, imperialism, nationalism) and the short-term trigger (the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand in Sarajevo in June 1914). The homework should take about 40 minutes. Adjust the content to your own scheme of work.

---

## The redesigned homework: "Which cause matters most?"

### Student-facing task

**AI rules for this task: "AI to check."** Do Part 1 from memory with no AI and no notes. Only after that may you use AI for Part 2. Hand in both.

#### Part 1: From memory (15 minutes, no notes, no devices)
Set a timer. Without looking at your notes, a book or anything online:

1. **List** four long-term causes of the First World War and the one short-term trigger. *(Five lines, in your own words.)*
2. **Choose the cause you think was most important.** Write **exactly 60 words** explaining why. You must include **one specific fact or example from our lessons** (a name, date, number or event) and the word **"because"** at least once.
3. **Write one sentence** saying how your chosen cause **linked to** another cause. For example: "[Cause A] made [Cause B] worse because..."

*Stop when the timer ends. Don't edit it later. This is your first draft.*

#### Part 2: Spot the mistakes (20 minutes)
Here is a paragraph an AI wrote when asked "Explain the causes of the First World War." It has **at least five problems**: some are **factual errors**, and some are **weaknesses in the explanation**.

> The First World War began in 1916 after Archduke Franz Ferdinand of Germany was shot in Vienna. Europe was divided into two alliances: the Triple Alliance of Britain, France and Russia, and the Triple Entente of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. Britain and France were also building enormous numbers of Dreadnought battleships to compete with each other. Countries wanted bigger empires and were proud of their nations. All of these causes were important and they all led to the war.

**Your job:**
1. **Underline** every mistake or weakness you can find.
2. **Rewrite the paragraph correctly** in your own words. Fix the facts, and make the **last sentence** into a real judgement: say which cause mattered most and why.
3. **Write two sentences:** what did the AI get wrong about *how causes link together*, and what does a good historian do differently?

*Check your own facts with your class notes or textbook. You may use AI to check your corrections, but note down anything it said that you think is wrong or unclear.*

#### Part 3: Be ready to explain (next lesson, 60 seconds)
Next lesson I'll ask some of you to explain your top cause in **60 seconds, out loud, without notes**. Practise at home. Say it to a family member, a pet or a mirror.

---

### What students hand in
1. Part 1 (handwritten or typed in a document with the timer time written on it).
2. Part 2: the marked-up paragraph, your corrected version, and your two sentences.
3. If you used AI to check: **the prompt you used** and **one thing you disagreed with or changed**.

## Teacher notes

### Why this works (and what it protects)
| Move | What it protects |
|---|---|
| **Memory first, then AI** (Part 1) | Students must retrieve and organise the causes themselves. Retrieval is what makes it stick, and the "timer + no devices" rule makes it hard to cheat in a way that's worth the effort. |
| **Critique, don't create** (Part 2) | Finding errors in a plausible paragraph needs real knowledge. Pasting the task into AI doesn't help, because the answer is a correction of *this* text. |
| **Judgement and linking** (Parts 1 and 2) | The real historical skill is weighing and connecting causes, which generic AI essays handle poorly: they usually list. |
| **Class-specific constraints** (exact 60 words, a fact from lessons, "because") | Tight constraints tied to your lessons are hard for a generic text to meet. |
| **Explain it live** (Part 3) | If they can say it aloud without notes, they learned it. A 60-second check takes about 10 minutes of lesson time for 10 students. |

### Answer key for the planted mistakes in Part 2

| # | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Date:** "1916" | The war began in **1914** (the assassination was 28 June 1914). |
| 2 | **Wrong country and place:** "Franz Ferdinand of Germany was shot in Vienna" | He was heir to the throne of **Austria-Hungary** and was shot in **Sarajevo** (in Bosnia). |
| 3 | **Alliances the wrong way round:** "Triple Alliance of Britain, France and Russia; Triple Entente of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy" | The **Triple Alliance** was Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy. The **Triple Entente** was Britain, France and Russia. |
| 4 | **Naval race wrong:** "Britain and France... to compete with each other" | The naval arms race was between **Britain and Germany**. |
| 5 | **Weak explanation:** "All of these causes were important and they all led to the war." | It lists causes without **weighing or linking** them. A good answer says which mattered most and explains how causes connected (for example, alliances turned a local crisis into a European war). |

*You could add an optional sixth weakness: "Countries wanted bigger empires and were proud of their nations" is vague and gives no example (for example, the rivalry over colonies or Serbian nationalism). Credit students who spot it.*

### Marking guide (quick, credits thinking)

**Part 1 (6 marks)**
- 2 marks: four causes plus a trigger, in own words, mostly correct
- 2 marks: a clear reason for the "most important" cause using a **specific fact from lessons** and "because"
- 1 mark: obeys the 60-word limit (within 5 words)
- 1 mark: a sensible link between two causes

**Part 2 (8 marks)**
- 1 mark each for four of the five planted problems identified and corrected (4 marks)
- 2 marks: a final sentence that makes a **supported judgement** about the most important cause
- 2 marks: the two reflection sentences show understanding of how causes link or how to weigh them

**Hand-in quality (2 marks)**
- 1 mark: if AI was used, a prompt and one honest disagreement or change are shown
- 1 mark: the timer time or draft is shown

**Part 3 (live, 4 marks, in the next lesson)**
- 2 marks: accurate, specific content with no notes
- 2 marks: gives a reason, not just a list

**What to watch for:** work that is polished but with no fact from your lessons, a timer time that doesn't match the length of the writing, or a Part 2 correction that simply repeats the AI paragraph with no changes. Ask those students to explain one sentence aloud.

### Quick follow-up in the next lesson (10 minutes)
1. Cold-call 5 to 6 students for their 60-second explanation (before you reveal marks).
2. Ask the class: "Which cause did most people choose as most important? Who chose a different one? Can we defend each choice?" This sets up the full essay if you do want one, written in class under your eyes.

### If you still want an essay
Use this homework as the **planning stage**, then have students write the essay **in class, handwritten, in 30 minutes**, using their Part 1 and Part 2 notes. The thinking is done and visible, and the writing is theirs.

### The simple rule to say out loud to the class
> "You can use AI after you've done your own thinking, not instead of it. If you use it, I want to see what you asked and what you changed."
