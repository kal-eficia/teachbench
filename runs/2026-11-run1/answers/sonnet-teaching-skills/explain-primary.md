# Why we have day and night: a script for Year 1

**Assumptions:** you have a torch, a ball (a football or a beach ball) with a small sticker on it, and a room you can make fairly dark. Children are sitting on the carpet. The whole thing takes about three minutes spoken, plus the child-led parts. I have kept to the idea that the Earth spins, and left out orbits and seasons on purpose, because mixing them up is the most common muddle at this age.

**Words to use every time:** Sun, Earth, spin, day, night. Use "the part of the Earth where we live" rather than abstract language.

---

## Step 1: Start from what they know (30 seconds)

**Say:** "Put your hand up if you have seen the Sun in the sky. Keep it up if you have seen the Moon or the stars. Was it light or dark when you saw the Sun? And when you saw the stars?"

**Check:** "When you see the Sun, is it day or night?"
**Expected:** Day. (If a child says the Sun and the Moon can both be out in the day, praise it: that is true, and you can come back to it later.)

---

## Step 2: Show it before you explain it (60 seconds)

Switch off the lights or draw the curtains.

**Say:** "This torch is our Sun. This ball is our Earth. This sticker is where we live, right here at school."

Hold the torch still. Shine it on the ball so one half is bright and the other half is dark. Put the sticker on the bright side.

**Say:** "Look. Our sticker is in the light. It is day for us."

Now turn the ball slowly (keep the torch exactly where it is) until the sticker goes into the dark half.

**Say:** "I am turning the Earth. Now our sticker is in the dark. It is night for us."

**Check (ask a child to point):** "Where is the sticker now, in the light or in the dark? Is it day or night?"
**Expected:** In the dark, night.

---

## Step 3: Now say the idea in proper words (30 seconds)

**Say:** "The Earth spins round and round, like this. One whole spin takes one whole day and night. When our part of the Earth faces the Sun, it is day. When our part turns away from the Sun, it is night."

Get the class to say it with actions: face the "Sun" (arm out) for day, turn your back for night.

**Check:** "Which one is spinning, the Sun or the Earth?"
**Expected:** The Earth.
**Most likely wrong answer:** "The Sun." Many children think the Sun goes round us, because that is what it looks like. Reply: "It looks like the Sun moves, but really we are the ones turning. It is like when you ride in a car and the trees seem to move past you."

---

## Step 4: One analogy, and where it breaks (30 seconds)

**Analogy:** A child sits on a spinny chair facing a lamp. "When you face the lamp, your face is lit up: that is day. When the chair turns and your back is to the lamp, your face is in the dark: that is night."

**Where it breaks:** Say it out loud: "The Earth is not a chair, and it spins very slowly. We cannot feel it spinning at all. And the Earth never has a 'front' and 'back': every place gets a turn."

**Check:** "On the chair, when is it night for your face?"
**Expected:** When it is turned away from the lamp.

---

## Step 5: A second example, so the idea travels (30 seconds)

Put a second sticker on the opposite side of the ball, in the dark.

**Say:** "This sticker is on the other side of the world. Let's say it is where a child in Australia lives. When it is day for us, what is it for them?"

Turn the ball slowly so children can see the two stickers swap places.

**Check:** "If it is night for us, what is it on the other side of the world?"
**Expected:** Day.

---

## Step 6: A non-example (30 seconds)

**Say:** "Sometimes it is cloudy and the sky looks grey. Is that night?"

Hold a tissue in front of the torch so the ball still glows faintly. "The light is dimmer, but it is still day. Night only happens when our part of the Earth turns away from the Sun."

**Also say, plainly:** "The Sun does not go to sleep, and the Moon does not push the Sun away. The Sun is always shining. It is us that turn."

**Check:** "Is a cloudy day a night? Thumbs up or thumbs down."
**Expected:** Thumbs down.

---

## Final check (to partners, 1 minute)

**Ask:** "Turn to your partner and tell them why it is night where the sticker is in the dark."

**Sentence to listen for:** "It is night because our part of the Earth has turned away from the Sun."

**Common wrong answers to listen for and fix:**
- "The Sun went to bed" or "the Sun went away": the Sun is still shining, it is the Earth that turned.
- "The Moon made it night": the Moon is out at night, but it does not cause it.
- "The clouds covered the Sun": that gives a grey day, not night.

---

## Something the children can read later

The Earth is a big round ball. It spins round and round, very slowly. The Sun shines on one half of the Earth at a time. The half facing the Sun has day. The half turned away has night. When it is day for us, it is night on the other side of the world.

---

**Safety note:** remind the children never to look straight at the real Sun. The torch is fine.
