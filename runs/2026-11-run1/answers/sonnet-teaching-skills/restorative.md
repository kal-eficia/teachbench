# Restorative conversation plan: two Year 9 boys, online comment and lunchtime fight

**Assumptions:** I'm calling the boys A (posted the comment) and B (the one it was about). Both have served their sanctions, both are 13 to 14, and your school's policy allows a joint restorative meeting. The comment was posted on a platform you can see or have a screenshot of. Parents have been told about the incident already.

**What's not up for negotiation:** the school's sanctions stand and this conversation doesn't change them. Fighting and posting unkind things online were both wrong. The meeting is about repairing things, not re-running the incident or deciding who was "more" to blame.

---

## 1. The facts to have in front of you

- What the comment said, where it was posted, when, and whether it's still up (have a screenshot, don't circulate it).
- What led to the fight, as far as you know: who threw the first punch, who was watching, whether others joined the comments or filmed it.
- Any history between them, and who their friends are. Is anyone else involved who needs a separate conversation?
- What each sanction was and that it's been completed.

## 2. Before the joint meeting (today, 5 minutes each, separately)

The most important part. A joint meeting only works if both boys come willingly.

- Speak to each privately. Say: "I'd like to help the two of you sort this out properly, not just leave it. Would you be willing to sit down together for 15 minutes tomorrow? You can say no."
- Ask each: "What would you want to say to him? What would you need to hear?" This tells you what to expect.
- Check B feels safe. If B is anxious or says no, don't force it. Do a one-to-one with each instead and pass messages only if B agrees.
- Tell both exactly how it will go, so there are no surprises: each speaks without interruption, no blaming, no insults, nobody is in trouble in this room.
- Check A isn't coming in just to "say sorry and get it over with". If A is still angry, give a short one-to-one first.

If either boy says anything that suggests he's being harassed, threatened or isn't safe at home or online, stop the plan and follow the school's safeguarding procedure straight away.

## 3. Set up

- Private room, no audience. Two chairs the same size, set at an angle, not face to face across a table. You sit where you can see both.
- Phones away. Water available.
- Time: 15 to 20 minutes (longer than a one-to-one because there are two voices). Not in lunch or just before a lesson they dislike.
- Have a tissue box and the agreed-actions sheet (blank lines for 3 actions) ready.

## 4. The conversation

### Opening (you)
> "Thank you both for coming. This isn't about getting anyone in trouble, because the sanctions are done. It's about getting things back to a place where you can both be in this school without it being tense. Each of you gets to speak and the other listens. We don't interrupt. If it gets too heated, I'll pause us. Is that okay?"

### The questions (in this order, same set for both boys)

Let A answer each first, then B. Keep your own comments to a minimum. Say "Thank you" and move on.

1. **"What happened, from your point of view?"** Listen. Don't argue the details. If it becomes a blame-trade, say: "I'm not here to decide who's right. I just want to hear how you saw it."
2. **"What were you thinking at the time?"** For A: when he posted the comment. For B: when he saw it, and when the fight started.
3. **"How were you feeling?"** Boys this age often say "I don't know" or "fine". Offer a choice: "Angry, embarrassed, hurt, annoyed, something else?"
4. **"Who has been affected, and how?"** Push gently beyond the two of them: friends who had to pick sides, families, other students who saw it, the person who filmed it.
5. **"What do you need to do to put it right?"** Ask A first. For B: "Is there anything you need from him?"
6. **"What could you each do differently if something like this starts again?"** Get something concrete, e.g. "I'd walk away and tell Mr/Ms X" or "I wouldn't comment online when I'm angry."
7. **"What can I do to help?"** Ask both.

### If B wants to say something directly to A
Let him. Then ask A: "What did you hear?" This stops A jumping to defence.

### If A isn't ready to apologise properly
Don't force a scripted apology. Ask: "Is there anything about what you posted that you'd take back?" A forced "sorry" will be worth nothing.

### Language for you (name behaviour, not the person)
- "The comment online was seen by other people and that made it harder for him." (not "you're a bully")
- "Hitting someone is never okay, and it's also true that being publicly put down hurts." (holds both)
- Own your part if there was one: "I should have picked up on the tension between you before it got to lunch."

## 5. Agreeing the repair (5 minutes)

Write the agreement down together, in the boys' words, and have both read it.

Likely actions, but let them suggest first:
- The comment is deleted today, in front of you or confirmed by A by a set time.
- A says sorry to B in his own words, in the room or by a short note if B prefers.
- Neither posts or comments about the other. If something online bothers either of them, they tell a named adult instead.
- A "reset" signal at school: if they're in the same space and it feels tense, either can walk away without it counting against them.
- Optional: a short check-in with each boy in one week, with you or their tutor.

Don't force a handshake. If B wants to shake hands, fine. If not, "we're okay to be in the same room" is a good result.

## 6. Closing (you)
> "Thank you both. That took maturity. Tomorrow is a new day and I'm looking forward to seeing you both in lessons without this hanging around. If either of you has any trouble, my door is open."

Let them leave at different times (one first, two minutes later) so there's no awkward walk-out together.

## 7. If it goes wrong

- **Raised voices or insults:** stop. "Let's take a breath." If it doesn't settle, end the joint meeting and speak to each one separately. That is still useful.
- **One boy refuses to speak:** don't push. "That's okay. Is there something you'd want me to pass on?"
- **A new allegation comes out** (more messages, threats, pressure from others): stop the restorative script and follow the school's behaviour or safeguarding procedure.

## 8. Follow-up

- Brief note on the school system: what was agreed, who attended, the follow-up date.
- Short message to each family: "The boys met today with me to put things right. Both took part well. They've agreed [one line]. Please encourage [him] to come to me or his tutor if anything online bothers him."
- Check-in with each boy after one week: "How's it been?" Look at body language, who he's with, any new tension.
- Share with the year team to keep an eye on social media and friendship groups. Remind the group that screenshots and re-sharing the comment make things worse.
- Consider a short tutor-time session for the whole year group on kindness and conflict online, without naming anyone.
