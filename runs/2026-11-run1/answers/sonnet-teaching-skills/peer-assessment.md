# Making Year 8 peer feedback useful: phone stand design sheets

**Assumptions:** a 50 to 60 minute lesson, class of about 28, students are halfway through their design sheets (ideas, annotations, some development). Peer feedback is for **improving the design**, not for giving marks. You keep the marking.

## Why you got "good" and "nice"

Students write "good" when they don't know what to look for, have never seen what good looks like, and have no structure that forces them to be specific. So the fix is three things: **criteria they can point to, a calibration round on two sample sheets before they touch each other's work, and a form that makes vague comments impossible.** Then the writer has to do something with the feedback.

## The success criteria (put these on the board and on the form)

A good phone stand design sheet:
1. **Works for the user.** It shows the viewing angle and how the phone stays upright and stable. I can see where the charging cable goes.
2. **Has dimensions.** Key sizes are marked (e.g. width of the slot, height, thickness) so it could be made.
3. **Says why.** Annotations explain *why* a choice was made ("30 mm slot so it fits a phone with a case"), not just *what* it is.
4. **Chooses materials and says why.** The material is named, with a reason.
5. **Shows development.** It's clear which idea was picked and what was changed from the first sketch.

## The lesson (55 minutes)

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | **Ban the words.** "Good", "nice", "fine" and "ok" are banned today. Show two vague comments and ask: *What could the writer do with this?* Nothing. |
| 5 to 10 | Go through the five criteria. Ask: *What would I point to on a sheet to prove criterion 3?* |
| 10 to 22 | **Calibrate** on two sample sheets (below) |
| 22 to 40 | **Peer review** in pairs (swap sheets) with the feedback form |
| 40 to 50 | **Writer responds:** change one thing on your own sheet |
| 50 to 55 | **Exit:** one sentence on what you changed and why |

## Step 1: Calibrate on two model sheets (12 minutes)

Project or hand out two sample design sheets. Describe them on the board if you don't have scans of real work. Here are two you can use or mock up. Students use initials only for any real work shown.

**Sample A: strong.** Three sketches. One chosen and circled. Annotations include "70° angle so the screen can be seen from a desk" and "slot is 14 mm wide to fit a phone and a case". Dimensions on the front and side view. "5 mm acrylic: strong, can be laser-cut, and easy to wipe clean." A note: "Changed from V-shape to a wider base because the first sketch tipped over." Cable gap marked.

**Sample B: weaker.** One neat, coloured-in sketch. Annotations: "phone stand", "base", "good design". No dimensions. "Wood." No reason for the choice, no sign of change, no cable gap.

**Process:**
1. Students work alone for 4 minutes: for each sheet, tick which criteria are met and **write the exact part of the sheet** that proves it.
2. Pairs compare.
3. **You reveal your judgement:** Sample A meets all five, with evidence. Sample B meets none fully: it's neat, but it only meets criterion 1 in part. Discuss disagreements. This tells you who understands the criteria, before they review each other.
4. Ask: *What one improvement would you give B, and how exactly could they make it?* (e.g. "Add the width of the slot, because without it the phone might not fit.")

## Step 2: The peer feedback form

Students review a partner's sheet using this form. **Initials only** on the form.

> **Reviewer:** ___ **Writer:** ___
>
> **1. Criteria check.** Tick each one that's met and write where you can see it.
> | Criterion | Met? | Where I can see it |
> |---|---|---|
> | 1 Works for the user | | |
> | 2 Has dimensions | | |
> | 3 Says why | | |
> | 4 Materials and why | | |
> | 5 Shows development | | |
>
> **2. A strength, in specific words.**
> *"You met ___ when you wrote / drew ___ ."*
>
> **3. One improvement, and how to do it.**
> *"To make it better, add / change ___ because ___ ."*
>
> **4. One question for the designer.**
> *"What would happen if ___ ?"*

### Sentence starters (on the board)

- "You met criterion __ when you wrote / drew ..."
- "I can see you thought about the user because ..."
- "To make it stronger, add ... because ..."
- "What would happen if the phone was ... ?"
- "I'm not sure why you chose ... Can you add the reason?"

### Rules for reviewers

- Comment on the **work**, not the person.
- You must point to a place on the sheet.
- Avoid the banned words; if you write one, you have to follow it with "because".
- Do a quick test where possible: hold a phone, or a cut-out card the same size, next to their drawn slot and ask: *does it fit? would it tip?*

## Step 3: The writer responds

This is what stops feedback being wasted.

> **Feedback I received:** ___
> **What I changed on my sheet:** ___
> **Why I changed it:** ___

Students must make **one** visible change in a different colour pen. If they disagree with a comment, they write why. That counts too.

## Step 4: Teacher check (while students work)

- Walk round and **sample three or four forms** for accuracy. Ask: *did the reviewer point to evidence? Is the improvement something the writer could actually do?*
- Praise precise comments out loud, e.g. "A has told B exactly which dimension is missing. That's the kind of comment I want."
- If feedback is wrong (for example, it says dimensions are present when they aren't), correct it on the spot. This is the one risk with peer review. Keep marks for yourself.

## Support and stretch

- **Support:** give a reviewer a reduced form with only criteria 1 to 3, and a sentence starter already begun for each box.
- **Stretch:** add the question *"What might go wrong when someone uses this stand on a bed, a desk, a car dashboard?"* and have reviewers suggest one design change for the extra use.

## Exit ticket

*"One thing my partner told me that I used, and what I changed."* Collect these. They show you which comments were useful, and you can read a few next lesson as examples.

## If you have limited time

Skip the second sample sheet. Calibrate on Sample B only, since it shows students what weak looks like. Keep the form and the response step: those are the parts that matter most.
