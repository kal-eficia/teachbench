# Year 5 Geography: Rivers and the Water Cycle Check

*Run this three weeks after the unit. About 10 minutes. No notes, no talking. It's about what you can remember, not the score.*

**For each question, tick how sure you are: G = guessing, T = I think so, C = certain.**

# Teacher answer key

| # | Answer | Topic | What a wrong answer tells you |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Evaporation | Water cycle | "Condensation" or "precipitation": mixing up the stages. "Boiling" is a common everyday word to look for. |
| 2 | Source | River features | "Mouth" means they've swapped the two ends. "Spring" is fine as a partial answer if they mention the source idea. |
| 3 | **C** | Water cycle | A: thinks warmer vapour makes clouds. B: thinks rain starts at the sea and goes up. D: confuses cloud formation with freezing. |
| 4 | False. The water keeps moving round: it flows to rivers and the sea, evaporates and falls again. | Water cycle | "True" shows they think of rain as a one-way journey, not a cycle. |
| 5 | Evaporation, condensation, precipitation, collection | Water cycle | Condensation and precipitation swapped, or collection put second. |
| 6 | **A** | River features | B: mixes up tributary with mouth or estuary. C: mixes it with meander. D: mixes it with source. |
| 7 | Meander | River features | "Bend" alone isn't enough; look for the word. "Mouth" or "tributary" shows vocabulary mix-up. |
| 8 | **D** | River courses | A and B: describe the middle or lower course. C: describes the mouth. Any of these means they don't link features to where they are along the river. |
| 9 | False. Rivers also get water from tributaries, springs, melting snow, groundwater and run-off from the land. Accept any one or two correct sources. | Where river water comes from | "True" means they picture the river as a bath filling from above. |
| 10 | Deposition | Erosion, transport and deposition | "Erosion" is the usual mix-up (wearing away vs dropping). "Transport" means carrying. |
| 11 | **B** | Rivers | A: wind. C: the sea "attracts" water. D: temperature, not gravity. |
| 12 | A full answer: sun heats sea, water evaporates, rises and condenses into clouds, falls as rain (precipitation) over land, runs downhill into streams and rivers (collection), and the river flows back to the sea. 3 steps in the right order is a good answer. | Water cycle and rivers together | Missing steps or "the sea flows into the river" shows they haven't joined the cycle to the river. |

**Multiple-choice key (positions):** C, A, D, B (Q3, 6, 8, 11). Every position is used once, with no runs, repeats or staircase.

## What to do with the results
- Students swap nothing: they check from the key, then correct in a different colour.
- Note the two questions most children get wrong or tick "G" for, and put them in your next starter.
- If many answers are confident but wrong (T or C with a wrong answer), that is a misconception to reteach, not just a gap to fill.

*Assumptions: the unit covered the four stages of the water cycle, main river features (source, mouth, tributary, meander), the idea of upper course, and erosion, transport and deposition. Swap any question for something you didn't teach. If you want to mix in older topics, replace Q10 and Q11 with questions from last term.*
