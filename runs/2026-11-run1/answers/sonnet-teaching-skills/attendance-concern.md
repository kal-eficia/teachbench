# Email to parents about attendance

**Assumptions:** you're writing to both parents or carers and don't know the reasons for the absences. I've kept it to 150 words or fewer, with a single ask. I've not included the school's attendance policy or anything about fines, because you want to meet them, not threaten them. Swap in the name, dates and the school's usual greeting before sending.

**Subject:** Can we find a time to talk about [child]?

Dear [parent or carer names],

I'm [child]'s form tutor and I really like having him in my group. I'd like to talk with you about how school has been going for him this term.

His attendance is currently 84%, and most of the missed days have been Mondays and Fridays. I'm not worried about blame, I'd just like to understand what's happening and see how school can help.

Would you be able to come in for 15 minutes, or have a phone call, in the next week or so? Please reply with a day and time that suits you and I'll fit around you.

Many thanks,
[Name]
Form tutor, [School]

---

## Notes for you (not part of the email)

- **Word count:** about 130 words in the email body.
- **Why this works:** it opens warm, states the fact once in plain terms, makes clear it is a conversation and not a warning, and has one clear ask (reply with a time).
- **Mondays and Fridays:** I've mentioned the pattern because it's the useful fact, but not guessed at the cause. In the meeting, ask open questions first: "How are mornings at home?", "What does [child] say about those days?", "Is there anything about Mondays or Fridays at school he finds hard (a lesson, a person, travel)?" Listen before suggesting anything.
- **If you don't hear back in three school days:** send a short follow-up or phone, and offer a call instead of a visit.
- **Check before sending:** you've not named any other student, and your head of year or attendance lead is happy with you contacting the family this way.
