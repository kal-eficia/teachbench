# Enzymes: exam-style question (AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy, Biology, Foundation Tier)

**Assumptions:** AQA Combined Science: Trilogy (8464), Biology Paper 1, Foundation Tier. Topic: enzymes and digestion (Organisation). 8 marks, about 8 minutes. All parts share one context, so the **describe** in (b) leads into the **explain** in (c) and (d), which is where your students lose marks. The question is original.

---

## QUESTION

A student investigated the enzyme **amylase**. Amylase breaks down **starch** into sugar.

She mixed amylase with starch solution at six different temperatures. She tested the mixture with iodine solution every 10 seconds. Iodine turns blue-black when starch is present. She recorded the time it took for the starch to disappear.

**Table 1**

| Temperature in °C | Time for starch to disappear in seconds |
|---|---|
| 10 | 240 |
| 20 | 120 |
| 30 | 60 |
| 40 | 30 |
| 50 | 90 |
| 60 | Starch still present after 300 seconds |

**(a)** Amylase is an enzyme. Enzymes are biological catalysts.
What does a catalyst do?
Tick **one** box.

☐ Slows down a reaction
☐ Stops a reaction from happening
☐ Speeds up a reaction and is not used up
☐ Turns a reaction into a different reaction

**[1 mark]**

**(b)** Describe how the time taken for the starch to disappear changed as the temperature increased from 10 °C to 60 °C.
Use data from Table 1 in your answer.

**[2 marks]**

**(c)** Starch was **not** broken down at 60 °C.
Explain why.

**[3 marks]**

**(d)** Amylase works best at about pH 7. Food then moves into the stomach, which has a pH of about 2.
Explain why amylase does not break down starch in the stomach.

**[2 marks]**

**Total: 8 marks**

---

## MARK SCHEME

### (a) [1]

| Marking point | Mark |
|---|---|
| Speeds up a reaction and is not used up (third box) | 1 |

Do not award the mark if more than one box is ticked.

### (b) [2]

| Marking points | Mark |
|---|---|
| The time **decreased / got shorter** as the temperature increased from 10 °C to 40 °C (for example, from 240 s to 30 s) | 1 |
| Above 40 °C the time **increased** (90 s at 50 °C), and at 60 °C the starch was **not** broken down in 300 s / 40 °C was the fastest | 1 |

**Accept:**
- Correct data quotes in place of the description of the trend, if the trend is clear (for example, "it went from 240 down to 30 and then up again").
- "Fastest / quickest at 40 °C."
- "Starch broken down faster as temperature went up until 40 °C."

**Do not accept:**
- "It went down" with no mention of the rise afterwards, for the second mark.
- Describing the rate when the question asks about time, if it contradicts the table (for example, "the time went up" for 10 to 40 °C).
- Data with no trend and no comparison, for example only "240 seconds".

### (c) [3]

| Marking points | Mark |
|---|---|
| The (high) temperature changes the **shape of the active site** | 1 |
| The enzyme is **denatured** | 1 |
| (so) the **starch / substrate no longer fits** (into the active site) | 1 |

**Accept:**
- "Enzyme/active site changed shape", "the shape of the enzyme changed."
- "Starch doesn't fit / can't bind / isn't the right shape any more / is not complementary".
- "Enzyme is denatured" for the second mark even without a mention of shape, but then the shape mark must come from the other points.
- "The enzyme stops working because it's too hot" **only** as context. It does not earn a mark by itself.

**Do not accept:**
- "Enzyme is killed / dies / is destroyed / has melted" (enzymes aren't alive).
- "Enzyme is used up."
- "The starch is denatured."
- "There's not enough energy" or "particles move too slowly" (this is wrong for 60 °C).
- Restating the question: "the starch wasn't broken down because the enzyme didn't work".

### (d) [2]

| Marking points | Mark |
|---|---|
| The low pH / acid **changes the shape of the active site** / **denatures** the enzyme | 1 |
| (so) the **starch no longer fits** the active site | 1 |

**Accept:**
- "Amylase is denatured by the acid."
- "pH 2 is not its optimum pH / is too acidic."
- "Substrate can't bind", "starch is not complementary".

**Do not accept:**
- "Enzyme killed by the acid."
- "Stomach acid breaks down the starch" (it doesn't).
- "Enzyme is used up."

---

## MODEL ANSWERS (full marks)

**(a)** Speeds up a reaction and is not used up.

**(b)** As the temperature went up from 10 °C to 40 °C, the time for the starch to disappear got shorter, from 240 seconds to 30 seconds. After 40 °C the time got longer again, 90 seconds at 50 °C, and at 60 °C the starch was not broken down at all.

**(c)** At 60 °C the temperature is **too high**, **so** the shape of the active site changes. **This means** the enzyme is denatured, **so** the starch can no longer fit into the active site, **and so** the starch is not broken down.

**(d)** The acid in the stomach (pH 2) **changes the shape of the active site** of amylase, so the enzyme is denatured. **Because** of this, starch can no longer fit into the active site, **so** it is not broken down.

**Linking words to point out to your students:** *so, because, this means, therefore.* Every mark in an "explain" answer comes from a **reason** joined to the one before it. The chain in (c) is: *temperature too high → shape of active site changes → denatured → starch doesn't fit → no breakdown.*

## NOTES FOR THE TEACHER

**Assessment objectives (approximate):** (a) AO1 recall. (b) AO3 interpreting data. (c) and (d) AO1/AO2, applying knowledge of enzymes to a situation.

**How the parts connect:** (b) asks students to *describe* the pattern, including the drop at 60 °C. (c) then asks them to *explain* the exact thing they have just described. (d) asks for the same chain of reasoning in a new situation (pH instead of temperature), so you can see who has understood and who just learned a phrase for temperature.

**Why your students might struggle with explain, and how to use this question:**
- Give them the chain as a scaffold: *because ... so ... which means ...* and have them write one answer to (c) as a chain of linked short sentences.
- After marking, ask them to underline the **linking words** in the model answer. Students who write a list of facts ("denatured, active site, shape") get 1 or 2 marks. Those who write the chain get all three.
- Cover (c) and (d) with the "explain" frame: *Cause: ___. Effect on the enzyme: ___. Effect on the reaction: ___.*

**Timing:** about 8 minutes (1 minute per mark).

**What's not asked:** the explanation of why the reaction is faster from 10 to 40 °C (more energy, more collisions) is deliberately left out. It isn't needed at this tier for the denaturing part, and it would add a different type of reasoning. Add it as an extra 2-mark part if you want to stretch higher-attaining students.
