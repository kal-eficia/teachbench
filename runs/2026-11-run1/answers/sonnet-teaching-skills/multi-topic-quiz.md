# Year 9 Physics mixed quiz: energy, waves and forces (10 questions)

**Name:** ______________  **Date:** ____________

For each question, circle one answer. Then tick how sure you were: Guessing / Think so / Certain.

**1.** A car travels along a straight road at a constant speed. The engine provides a driving force of 2000 N. Which statement is correct?
- A. The resistive forces are less than 2000 N, otherwise the car would stop.
- B. The resistive forces add up to 2000 N.
- C. There are no resistive forces acting on the car.
- D. The resultant force is 2000 N forwards.

**2.** A ball is thrown straight upwards. As it rises, what happens to its energy stores?
- A. The kinetic and gravitational potential stores both increase.
- B. The kinetic store increases and the gravitational potential store decreases.
- C. The total energy of the ball decreases to zero at the top.
- D. The kinetic store decreases and the gravitational potential store increases.

**3.** A sound wave has a frequency of 500 Hz and a wavelength of 0.66 m. What is its speed? (wave speed = frequency × wavelength)
- A. 330 m/s
- B. 758 m/s
- C. 0.0013 m/s
- D. 500 m/s

**4.** A box is pushed to the right with a force of 20 N. Friction of 8 N acts to the left. What is the resultant force on the box?
- A. 28 N to the right
- B. 20 N to the right
- C. 12 N to the right
- D. 12 N to the left

**5.** A lamp transfers 100 J of electrical energy. 20 J is transferred as light. What happens to the other 80 J?
- A. It is transferred to the surroundings as thermal energy.
- B. It is destroyed.
- C. It is stored in the filament as light.
- D. None, because no energy is wasted.

**6.** A student moves one end of a rope up and down. The wave travels along the rope, away from the student. Which describes this wave?
- A. Longitudinal, because the wave moves along the rope.
- B. Transverse, because the oscillations are at right angles to the direction the wave travels.
- C. Transverse, because the oscillations are parallel to the direction the wave travels.
- D. Longitudinal, because the oscillations are at right angles to the direction the wave travels.

**7.** A hockey puck slides across smooth ice where there is almost no friction. According to Newton's first law, what will the puck do?
- A. Slow down, because moving objects naturally come to rest.
- B. Speed up gradually.
- C. Stop once its force runs out.
- D. Keep moving in a straight line at a constant speed.

**8.** A student lifts a 2 kg mass through a height of 3 m. What is the increase in its gravitational potential energy? (gravitational field strength = 10 N/kg)
- A. 6 J
- B. 30 J
- C. 60 J
- D. 600 J

**9.** A water wave moves from deep water into shallow water, where it slows down. Which quantity stays the same?
- A. Speed
- B. Wavelength
- C. Both wavelength and speed
- D. Frequency

**10.** A parachutist is falling at a constant speed. Which statement is correct?
- A. The weight equals the air resistance and the resultant force is zero.
- B. The weight is greater than the air resistance.
- C. There are no forces acting on the parachutist.
- D. The weight is less than the air resistance.

## Teacher notes: what each wrong answer reveals

| Q | Topic | Misconception revealed by the wrong options |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Forces | A: a moving object needs a bigger driving force than resistance. C: constant speed means no resistance. D: confusing driving force with resultant. |
| 2 | Energy | A, B: energy stores rising or falling together or swapped the wrong way. C: energy lost rather than conserved. |
| 3 | Waves | B: divides instead of multiplies. C: divides the wrong way round. D: gives back the frequency. |
| 4 | Forces | A: adds forces that act in opposite directions. B: ignores friction. D: wrong direction. |
| 5 | Energy | B: energy is destroyed. C: confuses wasted energy with stored useful energy. D: believes no energy is wasted if energy is conserved. |
| 6 | Waves | A, C, D: confuses direction of travel with direction of oscillation. |
| 7 | Newton's 1st law | A and C: belief that objects need a force to keep moving. B: the puck speeds up with no force. |
| 8 | Energy | A: multiplies mass and height only. B: multiplies g and height only. D: slip in magnitude. |
| 9 | Waves | A, B, C: students think speed or wavelength is fixed. Frequency is set by the source. |
| 10 | Forces | B and D: unbalanced forces. C: believes constant speed means no forces. |
