## Attendance and habits

- Attendance: 11 of 12 sessions (92%). That's very good and is almost certainly part of why the gains are real.
- Homework: 4 of 6 (67%). Acceptable, but it is the weakest habit. Homework is where spaced practice happens, so each missed one costs more than a missed page of practice in a session.
- Attitude to ratio: says it is "confusing" and avoids it.

## Analysis for the tutor

**What moved, and what to keep doing.** Algebra, geometry and number all improved by 15 points or more. Those are the topics where the sessions have had direct teaching and practice, so keep the same pattern: short retrieval at the start, one focused teach, guided practice, then independent questions.

**What didn't move, and likely reasons.**

1. *Ratio and proportion (+5).* The avoidance is the main finding. If [S1] skips ratio in homework and steers away from it in sessions, there has been little real practice, so a flat score is what you'd expect. This is probably a practice and confidence problem as much as a knowledge problem. It matters because ratio underpins other areas too: scale and similar shapes in geometry, percentages and rates in number, and fractions in probability. A weak spot here may be holding other topics back.
2. *Probability (0).* Probability is a hypothesis to test rather than a conclusion: it depends heavily on fractions and proportional thinking, so it may be stuck for the same reason as ratio. If the next test shows ratio moving but probability still flat, it is a separate gap. Check how much probability has actually been taught and retrieved in the last six weeks. If very little, "not taught yet" is the simpler explanation.
3. *Statistics.* No baseline, so treat the 65% as a starting point only.

**A caution on the evidence.** Two tests is a small sample. A 5-point change in ratio could be noise, and a 35-point jump in algebra could be partly down to which questions came up. The direction is encouraging and consistent across four topics, but I wouldn't promise parents a particular final grade from this. Re-test the same topics in six weeks to confirm.

## Next six weeks: three priorities

1. **Ratio and proportion, in small steps, every session.** Start with a 5-minute ratio question at the start of each session (an easy win first: sharing in a ratio, then simplifying, then recipe-style scaling, then proportion problems). Use bar models so the method is visual. Aim to make it feel routine rather than a big scary topic. Ask [S1] to say what exactly feels confusing, because "confusing" usually means one specific step.
2. **Make homework a habit.** Move to short, fixed tasks (15 to 20 minutes) with a clear deadline, and include 2 ratio questions in every one, so it can't be dodged but doesn't feel like a mountain. Target: 6 of 6.
3. **Lock in and extend the gains.** Keep algebra and geometry in the starters (spaced retrieval) so they don't fade, and check probability with a short diagnostic in session 1 of the next block to decide whether it needs its own teaching.

**Target for the week 12 test:** ratio and proportion to 65% or above, probability above 60%, everything else at least holding.
