Assumptions: GCSE-level chemistry (any UK board), covering rates of reaction as set out. Cards are ordered so related cards are not back to back. Calculation cards use units in cm³ and g.

Q: What is the rate of a reaction? | A: How fast reactants are used up or products are formed.
Q: According to collision theory, what must happen for two particles to react? | A: They must collide with enough energy.
Q: What is the activation energy? | A: The minimum energy particles need to react when they collide.
Q: What are three ways to increase the rate of a reaction (not counting catalysts)? | A: Raise the temperature, raise the concentration or pressure, or increase the surface area.
Q: How does increasing temperature affect the rate of reaction? | A: It increases the rate.
Q: What does a catalyst do to the rate of a reaction? | A: It speeds the reaction up without being used up.
Q: What is the formula for mean rate of reaction? | A: Quantity of reactant used or product formed ÷ time taken.
Q: Why does a higher temperature increase the rate? Give the two reasons. | A: Particles move faster so collide more often, and more collisions have at least the activation energy.
Q: What does a catalyst do to the activation energy? | A: It lowers it by providing an alternative reaction pathway.
Q: What are the units of rate if you measure gas volume in cm³ and time in seconds? | A: cm³/s.
Q: Why does increasing concentration increase the rate? | A: More particles in the same volume, so more frequent collisions.
Q: What does "frequency of collisions" mean? | A: How many collisions happen each second.
Q: What is the effect of a catalyst on the amount of product made? | A: None. It doesn't change the amount of product.
Q: Which type of reaction does pressure affect? | A: Reactions involving gases.
Q: In the term "successful collision", what makes a collision successful? | A: The particles collide with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.
Q: What is the mean rate if 20 cm³ of gas is made in 40 s? | A: 0.5 cm³/s.
Q: Why does increasing the pressure of a gas increase the rate? | A: The particles are squashed closer together, so they collide more often.
Q: Why does powdered marble react faster than marble chips? | A: Powder has a larger surface area, so more particles are exposed to collide.
Q: How does a gas syringe let you measure rate? | A: It collects the gas and shows the volume made over time.
Q: What does increasing surface area do to the number of exposed particles? | A: It increases the number of exposed particles.
Q: Does a catalyst get used up in a reaction? | A: No. It is unchanged at the end.
Q: Name a biological catalyst. | A: An enzyme.
Q: When measuring rate by mass loss, why does the mass go down? | A: A gas is made and escapes from the container.
Q: What is the mean rate if 3.0 g of product forms in 60 s? | A: 0.05 g/s.
Q: Which factor applies only to gases: concentration, pressure or surface area? | A: Pressure.
Q: Describe the experiment that uses a disappearing cross. | A: Time how long a cross under the flask takes to be hidden by the solid formed.
Q: Which reaction is used for the disappearing cross experiment? | A: Sodium thiosulfate with hydrochloric acid, which makes sulfur.
Q: On a rate graph, where is the reaction fastest? | A: At the start, where the line is steepest.
Q: Why is a reaction fastest at the start? | A: The reactants are at their highest concentration.
Q: What does it mean when the line on a rate graph becomes flat? | A: The reaction has finished (one reactant is used up).
Q: On a rate graph, what does a steeper line show? | A: A faster rate of reaction.
Q: How do you find the rate at a particular time from a graph? | A: Draw a tangent to the curve at that point and calculate its gradient.
Q: Two experiments use the same amounts of reactants but different temperatures. How do their final volumes of gas compare? | A: They are the same. Only the rate is different.
Q: What must you keep the same in a fair test of a rate factor? | A: Every variable except the one you're changing.
Q: Why is mass loss a poor method for measuring a reaction that makes hydrogen? | A: Hydrogen is so light that the mass change is too small to measure.
Q: Does raising the temperature change the activation energy? | A: No. More particles simply have enough energy.
Q: Concentration versus surface area: which one is about particles in a solution and which about pieces of a solid? | A: Concentration is about particles in a solution; surface area is about pieces of a solid.
Q: Why can flour dust in a factory cause an explosion? | A: The tiny particles have a huge surface area, so the reaction is very fast.
Q: Which of these does NOT change activation energy: catalyst, temperature or concentration? | A: Temperature and concentration. Only a catalyst lowers it.

How to use these: say the answer out loud before you check it. Sort the cards into "know" and "not yet". Come back to the "not yet" pile tomorrow, then in three days, then in a week. With eight weeks to go, aim to go through the whole set once a week.
