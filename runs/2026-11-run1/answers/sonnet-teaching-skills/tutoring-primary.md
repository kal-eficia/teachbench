# Tutoring plan: Year 5, 45 minutes online, 6, 7 and 8 times tables

**Assumptions**
- I don't have last session's notes, so I've assumed she is secure on 2s, 5s and 10s, partly secure on 3s and 4s, and gets 6×, 7× and 8× wrong or slow. Adjust the retrieval questions to what she actually got wrong last time.
- She uses a shared whiteboard or screen with you. Everything below works with just paper and a pen if needed.
- **Nothing is timed.** No countdowns, no timers on screen, no "quick fire". She gets as long as she needs. She rates how sure she is instead.

## Focus for today

**One focus: the 6× table, built from the 5× table she already knows.** Park 7× and 8× for later sessions. The hard facts left to learn across all three tables are only a handful (6×6, 6×7, 6×8, 7×7, 7×8, 8×8), because she already knows the others from 2s, 3s, 4s and 5s by swapping the order. Tell her this at the end. It makes the task feel much smaller.

**Why this approach:** she can use what she already knows (5×) plus one more group of 6 to reach any 6× fact. That means she can work out a fact she has forgotten, which reduces the anxiety.

## Plan (45 minutes)

| Time | Stage | What happens |
|---|---|---|

| 0:03 to 0:11 | Retrieval | Facts she knows, rated for confidence |
| 0:11 to 0:25 | Teach | 6× = 5× + one more 6 |
| 0:25 to 0:33 | Guided practice | She does, you prompt |
| 0:33 to 0:41 | Independent practice | Mixed 6× questions, no time limit |
| 0:41 to 0:44 | Exit check | Three questions |

### 1. Retrieval (8 minutes, from memory, untimed)

Ask each question, wait as long as she needs, and after each one ask her to show a thumb (up, sideways or down) for how sure she was. Don't correct silently. If she's wrong, say "Let's check that one together" and work it out.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 5 × 4 | 20 |
| 10 × 6 | 60 |
| 2 × 8 | 16 |
| 4 × 6 | 24 |
| 3 × 7 | 21 |
| 5 × 8 | 40 |
| 9 × 5 | 45 |
| 4 × 8 | 32 |

If she gets one wrong, note it. These are the facts to revisit next time. Don't try to fix them all today. If she is shaky on 5×, replace the teach stage with a 5× focus, because the 6× method depends on it.

### 2. Teach (14 minutes)

**The idea: a 6× fact is a 5× fact plus one more.**

1. **Show with an array (3 min).** Draw 5 rows of 6 dots on the whiteboard, then add one more row of 6. "5 rows of 6 is 30. One more row of 6 is 6 more. So 6 rows of 6 is 36."
2. **Model, thinking aloud (4 min).** Write: **6 × 7 = (5 × 7) + 7 = 35 + 7 = 42.** Say each step out loud. Then do **6 × 4 = (5 × 4) + 4 = 20 + 4 = 24**.
3. **Check she's following (3 min).** Ask her to explain back to you how to do 6 × 3. Answer: (5 × 3) + 3 = 15 + 3 = 18. If she can't, model another one.
4. **Spot the trick (4 min).** Write the 6× column: 6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 42, 48, 54, 60. Ask: "What do you notice?" Let her find patterns: it goes up by 6 each time, the answers are all even, and the last digit goes 6, 2, 8, 4, 0, 6, 2, 8, 4, 0. Don't rush this. Letting her notice is worth more than telling her.

### 3. Guided practice (8 minutes, you prompt)

She does the working. You ask questions, not give answers: "What's 5 times that?" "What do you add?"

| Question | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| 6 × 5 | | 30 |
| 6 × 6 | (5×6) + 6 = 30 + 6 | 36 |
| 6 × 8 | (5×8) + 8 = 40 + 8 | 48 |
| 6 × 9 | (5×9) + 9 = 45 + 9 | 54 |

If she stalls, give a hint, not the answer: "What's 5 times 8? Good. Now what do you add?" Praise the method, not just the answer: "I like how you used the 5s."

### 4. Independent practice (8 minutes, labelled separately)

She works alone while you watch the method (camera on her page, or she holds it up). No timer. Tell her she can use the dots if she wants.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 6 × 2 | 12 |
| 6 × 7 | 42 |
| 6 × 10 | 60 |
| 6 × 3 | 18 |
| 6 × 8 | 48 |
| 6 × 4 | 24 |
| 6 × 9 | 54 |
| 6 × 6 | 36 |

Then one reasoning question: "Which is right, 6 × 7 = 42 or 6 × 7 = 48? Prove it using 5 × 7." Answer: 42, because 5 × 7 = 35 and 35 + 7 = 42.

Watch for: adding the wrong number (adding 6 instead of the number she is multiplying by), mixing up 5× and 6× answers, or counting on her fingers. Counting is fine for now, so don't comment. Note it.

### 5. Exit check (3 minutes, three questions, untimed)

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| 6 × 6 | 36 |
| 6 × 7 | 42 |
| 6 × 9 | 54 |

If she gets two or three right and can say how she did it, the session worked. If she gets one or none, repeat this method next session before moving on.

### 6. Homework and close (1 minute)

**Homework (5 minutes, three days this week, nothing a parent has to teach):** on a card, she writes the 6× column from memory: 6, 12, 18... up to 60, then checks against the list on the back and circles any she got wrong. If she gets stuck, she uses "5× plus one more". No timer, and she doesn't need a parent to mark it.

Say: "You did the hard part today. You worked out facts yourself instead of just remembering them."

## Notes for after the session

## A longer path (so she can see the end)

1. Today: 6× via 5× plus one more.
2. Next: 7× via 5× plus two more (7 × 8 = 40 + 16 = 56).
3. Then: 8× via doubling the 4s (8 × 7 = 2 × 28 = 56).
4. Then: mixed practice with a confidence rating, still untimed.
5. Later, if and only if she is happy, make a self-set challenge: "beat your own score", always her choice and never a countdown.
