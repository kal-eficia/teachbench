# A level Economics, Year 13: 15-minute retrieval task

**Assumptions:** the class is mid-year, studying macroeconomics, and the exam is in five months. I've assumed a typical UK A level (AQA/Edexcel/OCR style), done on paper from memory with no notes, no phones and no AI. The task mixes macro topics with microeconomics from last year, because that older material is the most likely to have faded. Teacher key at the end.

## Student sheet: Starter, 15 minutes, closed book

**Instructions:** No notes, no searching, no AI. Have a go at everything; a wrong attempt is more useful than a blank. For questions 1 to 10, add **G / T / C** next to each answer: **G** = guessing, **T** = think so, **C** = certain.

### Part A: Quick recall (6 minutes)

1. Name two causes of **cost-push** inflation.
2. State the formula for **price elasticity of demand** and say what a value of -0.4 means.
3. In the AD/AS model, what is the effect on equilibrium price level and real output of a **rise in the exchange rate**? Say why.
4. Define **structural unemployment** and give one example of a cause.
5. What is a **negative externality**? Give one example other than pollution.
6. Which of these is **not** a tool of monetary policy: interest rates, quantitative easing, a change in income tax, forward guidance? Explain in a sentence.
7. What is the difference between a **budget deficit** and **national debt**?
8. Give one reason why a **subsidy** may fail to correct market failure.
9. The central bank raises interest rates. Give **two** ways this reduces aggregate demand.
10. Explain the difference between **real GDP** and **nominal GDP**.

### Part B: Apply it (5 minutes)

11. Draw a labelled AD/AS diagram (use the Keynesian or the neoclassical LRAS, whichever you prefer) showing the effect of an **increase in government spending** when the economy has spare capacity. Write one sentence under it explaining what happens to the price level and output.

### Part C: Link it together (4 minutes)

12. Choose **one**:
    - a) A government introduces a **maximum price** (price ceiling) on rents. Explain **one** possible effect on the market for rented housing and **one** effect on the wider economy.
    - b) Explain why a rise in **unemployment** might lower inflation, and why this might not happen.

### Check (do last, 30 seconds)
Open the answers your teacher shows you. Mark in a different colour. Circle every question where you wrote **C** (certain) and got it wrong. Those are your priority.

## Teacher key and reasons

| Q | Answer | Topic | Why chosen |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Any two: rising wage costs, higher raw material or energy prices, higher import costs from a weaker exchange rate, higher indirect taxes. | Inflation (macro, this term) | Core definition. Check they link to a shift **left** of SRAS. |
| 2 | PED = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price. -0.4: inelastic. A 1% price rise leads to about a 0.4% fall in quantity demanded. | Elasticities (micro, last year) | Likely to have faded. Check they say the sign and interpret it. |
| 3 | Price level falls, real output falls (or growth slows). Exports become more expensive, imports cheaper, so net exports fall and AD shifts left. Cheaper imported inputs can shift SRAS right, which lowers prices further. | AD/AS (macro) | Chain of reasoning; they often only get the AD shift. |
| 4 | Unemployment due to a mismatch of skills or location between workers and available jobs, caused by long-term changes in the economy. Examples: decline of an industry, automation, technological change. | Unemployment (macro) | Often confused with frictional and cyclical. |
| 5 | A cost imposed on a third party not involved in the transaction, not paid for by the producer or consumer. Examples: noise, congestion, passive smoking, antibiotic resistance. | Market failure (micro) | Retrieval from last year; the "other than pollution" stops memorised definitions. |
| 6 | A change in income tax. It is fiscal policy. | Monetary vs fiscal policy (macro) | Common muddle; spot confident-wrong. |
| 7 | Deficit: the amount the government borrows in one year (spending exceeds tax revenue). National debt: the total accumulated borrowing over time. | Fiscal policy (macro) | Frequently confused in exam answers. |
| 8 | Any one: government may not know the right level; cost to taxpayer and opportunity cost; may not be passed on to consumers; it may encourage inefficiency; information failure; unintended consequences. | Government intervention (micro) | Evaluation of intervention, which links to macro evaluation too. |
| 9 | Any two: borrowing is more expensive so consumption and investment fall; saving is more attractive; mortgage payments rise so disposable income falls; exchange rate may rise, reducing net exports; fewer firms invest. | Monetary policy (macro) | Chains of reasoning. Marks lost for no link back to AD. |
| 10 | Real GDP is adjusted for inflation (price changes removed) so it shows the change in output. Nominal GDP is measured at current prices and can rise just because prices rise. | Measuring the economy (macro) | Foundation for growth and inflation questions. |
| 11 | Diagram: AD shifts right along the horizontal or upward-sloping part of the SRAS curve. Real output rises, price level rises little or not at all because of spare capacity. | AD/AS, fiscal policy | Diagram skills; wrong labels are common. |
| 12a | Rent ceiling below equilibrium: excess demand, shortage of rental housing; landlords may sell or reduce quality and maintenance; wider effects might include reduced labour mobility or black markets. | Government intervention in markets (micro) | Applies last year's content to a real situation, which is how exam questions work. |
| 12b | Higher unemployment reduces demand-pull pressure and wage growth, so inflation falls (Phillips curve). It may not if inflation is cost-push, if expectations are high, if the unemployment is structural, or if there are supply shocks. | Inflation and unemployment (macro) | Evaluation, which is where A level marks are won. |

## Why this mix
- **Questions 2, 5, 8 and 12a** are last year's micro content, which is likely to have faded most. Five months out, these are the questions that need spaced return.
- **Questions 1, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11 and 12b** cover this term's macro, with a mix of definitions, chains of reasoning, a diagram and evaluation.
- Topics alternate between micro and macro so no two neighbouring questions come from the same topic.
- No MCQs, so no answer key pattern to worry about.

## Running it
1. **Before the task:** write on the board, "No notes. Guesses welcome. Mark G/T/C."
2. **During:** circulate quietly; do not help. Note which questions most students leave blank.
3. **Marking (3 to 4 minutes, within the 15 or straight after):** show the key on the board or read it out. Students self-mark in a different colour pen. Ask for a hand count of "certain but wrong" on any question. These are your priorities.
4. **After the lesson:** record the three questions with the lowest scores or most confident-wrong answers. Put them in the next two starters (the next lesson and a week later), and re-ask them in a month. Make the later questions harder: e.g. add a "to what extent" evaluation line.

## Adjusting it
- If the class finishes early, add the challenge: "Write one sentence explaining how a micro idea (e.g. elasticity) affects a macro outcome (e.g. the effect of a tax on inflation)."
- If you have a mixed-ability group, give three options of question 12 so everyone has a choice.
- If time is short, cut questions 7 and 10.
