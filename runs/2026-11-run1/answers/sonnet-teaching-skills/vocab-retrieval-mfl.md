# Year 7 Spanish: 10-Minute Retrieval Starter for Two Weeks

**Assumptions:** You teach roughly one lesson a day for the next two weeks, so there are 10 starters (if you have fewer lessons, use the first days that fit and carry the Friday check to your last lesson). Students have been taught greetings, numbers 1-31, school subjects, opinions (*me gusta, no me gusta, porque es...*) and family. I have assumed that "*Tengo...*" (I have) and "*se llama*" (he/she is called) were taught with family. Students have mini whiteboards and pens. No notes, dictionaries or devices.

**A note on accuracy:** To avoid unpractised grammar, every "*porque es...*" prompt uses a singular subject and either an adjective that doesn't change (*fácil, difícil, interesante, útil, genial*) or a masculine subject with *divertido/aburrido* (*el inglés, el arte, el teatro*). Plural subjects such as *las matemáticas* and *las ciencias* take *me gustan / porque son* in standard Spanish. If you've taught only *me gusta*, use these words as vocabulary items and avoid opinion sentences with them.

---

## The routine (the same every day, so it runs itself)

| Time | Step | What students do |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00-0:30** | **Settle** | Whiteboard, pen, eyes up. Say: "No notes, no whispering. Have a go." |
| **0:30-5:30** | **Part 1: Eight quick-fire questions** | You read each prompt aloud and show it on the board. Students write the answer on whiteboards and hold them up on "3, 2, 1, arriba". About 35 seconds each. Scan the boards and note any question where more than a quarter are wrong. |
| **5:30-8:00** | **Part 2: Pair speaking** | Partners take turns with the daily speaking prompt (30 seconds each, then swap). Walk around and listen. |
| **8:00-10:00** | **Part 3: Check and fix** | Reveal the answers (the key below). Students tick, then correct wrong answers in a different colour. Then ask: "Which one tripped you up?" Tally the two most-missed questions on the Tally Sheet (below). |

**Why it works:** Students pull answers out of memory before they see them. Topics are mixed (interleaved) so nobody can guess the topic from the neighbouring question. Older topics (greetings, numbers) come back often because they will fade first. Family, which is the most recent, gets more repeats early on.

**Rules to say once, on day one:** "This isn't a test. Wrong answers are useful because they show us what to practise. Correct it in colour and move on."

**Speaking and listening:** Read the Spanish aloud with the class repeating when you reveal answers. This keeps pronunciation in the starter too.

## Common mistakes to listen out for

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| **la inglés / el historia** (wrong gender) | Chant the pairs: *el inglés, la historia* |
| **Tengo un hermana** | *un hermano, una hermana*, since the word follows the gender |
| **treinta y uno** written as one word, or **veintiuno** written as three words | 16 to 29 are one word (dieciséis ... veintinueve); 31 is three words: treinta y uno |
| **veintidos** without the accent | *veintidós, veintitrés, veintiséis* have accents |
| **Me gusta** with *no* misplaced | *No* goes before *me gusta* |
