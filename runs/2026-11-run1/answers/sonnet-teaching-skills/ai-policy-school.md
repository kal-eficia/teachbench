# Student AI Use Policy (ages 11 to 18)

*Assumptions: this is a draft for your leadership team to adapt. It assumes a school-approved list of AI tools exists or will be agreed, and that your safeguarding and exam-malpractice policies are the authority for anything serious. Names in square brackets are for you to fill in.*

## Our aim

AI can help you learn, but only if you do the thinking. We want you to use it to understand more, practise more and build things, never to skip the learning, pass off its work as yours, or put yourself at risk.

## 1. Learn with it, don't let it learn for you

**Good uses:** ask it to explain something a different way, quiz you on a topic, give feedback on a draft *you* wrote, check your reasoning, help plan revision, or help you build a project.

**Not allowed:** submitting AI-written work as your own, using it to complete homework or assessments where it's not permitted, or using it in an exam.

A simple test: *Could I explain this work, in my own words, to my teacher without the tool?* If not, you haven't learned it.

## 2. Teachers will tell you which level applies

Every task will be marked with one of three levels:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **No AI** | The work must be entirely your own. |
| **AI to check or support** | You do the thinking first. AI may be used for feedback, explanations or checking. You note how you used it. |
| **AI as a tool** | AI is part of the task. You must show your prompts and say what you changed. |

If a task doesn't say, **ask before you use it.** For exams and coursework that counts towards a qualification, the exam board's rules (JCQ) apply and overrule everything here. Breaking them can lead to disqualification.

## 3. Be honest

If you used AI, say so, and say how. Being open about it is never a problem. Hiding it is.

## 4. Check what it tells you

AI can sound confident and be wrong. It can invent facts, quotations and sources, and it can be biased. Check anything important against your textbook, class notes or a reliable source.

## 5. Stay safe

- Never give an AI tool your **full name, address, school, phone number, passwords, photos of yourself or others**, or private details about anyone.
- Many AI tools have **age limits** (often 13+, sometimes 18+). Students under the minimum age use AI only in lessons, with a teacher, on school-approved tools.
- **Never use AI to make or share images, voices or videos of real people**, or to write messages that embarrass, deceive or target someone. This is bullying and will be treated as such.
- AI is not a counsellor, doctor or friend. If you are worried, upset or unsafe, **talk to a trusted adult** (your form tutor, [safeguarding lead], or a parent or carer).

## 6. By age

- **Years 7 to 9:** AI is used mainly in lessons, led by a teacher, on school-approved tools.
- **Years 10 to 11:** you may use approved tools for study at home, at the levels teachers set, and you keep a note of how you used them.
- **Sixth form:** more independence, with the same honesty and exam-board rules, and expectation that you can defend and explain your own work.

## 7. If something goes wrong

If you misuse AI, we start with a conversation about what happened and how to put it right. Repeated or serious misuse (including in assessments) follows the school's behaviour and exam-malpractice procedures, and we may contact home.
