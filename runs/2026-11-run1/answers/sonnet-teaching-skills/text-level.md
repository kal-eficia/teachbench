# Tropical Rainforests

Tropical rainforests are home to a huge number of different living things. Scientists call this **biodiversity**. There are more kinds of plants and animals in a rainforest than in almost any other place.

Why is this? Two things help. It is hot all year round. It also rains a lot. More than 2,000 millimetres of rain fall each year. That is more than two metres of water.

## The four layers

A rainforest has four layers. They are a bit like the floors of a tall building. Each layer has its own plants and animals. These living things have **adapted**. This means they have changed over a very long time to suit where they live. They are suited to how much light and how much **humidity** their layer has. Humidity means how much water is in the air.

1. **The emergent layer** is at the very top. A few tall trees poke out above everything else.
2. **The canopy** is the thick roof of leaves and branches below.
3. **The understorey** is under the canopy.
4. **The forest floor** is the ground at the bottom.

## A big danger: deforestation

**Deforestation** means cutting down lots of trees so that a forest disappears. People do this mainly for three reasons:

- **Commercial agriculture.** This is farming to grow crops or raise animals to sell.
- **Logging.** This is cutting down trees for wood.
- **Infrastructure.** This means building things like roads and towns.

Deforestation causes big problems:

- **Habitat fragmentation.** The forest is broken up into small patches. Animals have less room to live and find food.
- **Loss of endemic species.** An **endemic** species lives in one place and nowhere else in the world. If its forest goes, the species can die out for ever.
- **Release of carbon.** Trees take in carbon and **store** it. When the trees are cut down, this stored carbon goes back into the air as carbon dioxide gas.

More carbon in the air makes **climate change** worse. This kind of climate change is caused by people.

## Glossary

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| biodiversity | the number of different kinds of living things in a place |
| layer | one level of the rainforest, from the tall trees to the ground |
| emergent layer | the top layer, where a few very tall trees stick out |
| canopy | the thick roof of leaves and branches |
| understorey | the layer under the canopy |
| forest floor | the ground layer |
| adapted | changed over time to suit where it lives |
| humidity | how much water is in the air |
| deforestation | cutting down large areas of forest |
| commercial agriculture | farming to sell what is grown |
| logging | cutting down trees for wood |
| infrastructure | things like roads and buildings |
| habitat fragmentation | a habitat being broken into small pieces |
| endemic species | a living thing found in one place only |
| carbon | what trees store; released into the air as carbon dioxide |
| climate change | changes to the world's weather patterns over time |

## Comprehension questions (same core questions for any version)

1. Name the four layers of a tropical rainforest, from top to bottom.
2. Which two things about the weather help rainforests have so many different living things?
3. What does "adapted" mean? Give the two things the plants and animals in each layer are suited to.
4. Give the three main reasons that people cut down rainforest.
5. Explain what happens to the carbon stored in trees when they are cut down.

**Extension question:** Habitat fragmentation and loss of endemic species are both caused by deforestation. How are they different?
