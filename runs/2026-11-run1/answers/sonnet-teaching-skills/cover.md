# Cover Work: Year 8 Science, Period 3 (50 minutes, 30 students)

**Topic:** The digestive system (revision and application of organs and their jobs)

**Assumptions:** Students have already covered the main organs and what each does (mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, plus the idea of food passing through as a tube). The textbook has a digestive system chapter that students can use to check their facts, but the work does not depend on page numbers, so you can fill in the page on the cover sheet if you want to. There are no computers. **Enzymes are deliberately left out**, because you are teaching that next lesson yourself. The work is revision plus a writing task, so a non-scientist can run it with the answers below.

This pack has three parts:
1. **Cover teacher sheet** (print one page for the cover teacher)
2. **Student worksheet** (print 30 copies, or write the questions on the board)
3. **Answers and what to do next**

Collect all 30 worksheets at the end and leave them in the teacher's pigeonhole or on the desk with the class register. The sheets show the work was done, so please **write the names of anyone absent or anyone who did not work** on the back of the top sheet.

### If students ask something you can't answer
Don't worry about answering science questions. Say: **"Good question. Write it at the bottom of your sheet and your teacher will answer it when they're back."** This also works for **any question about how food is broken down chemically or what "enzymes" are**: say, "That's what you'll learn next lesson. Write it down."

### If a student finishes early
Part C (Challenge) is for them. If they finish that, they can make a **labelled diagram** of the digestive system from the textbook on the back of the sheet, or test a partner using the quiz questions (books shut).

### If there's a behaviour problem
Use the school's normal system. Please note names on the back of the top sheet.

### If the room or equipment is a problem
The whole lesson works with just the worksheet and a pen. If there aren't enough textbooks, students can share in pairs for Part B. If there is no printer, write the questions from Part A on the board (the answers are on the answers page) and students use paper.

### Safeguarding or medical
Follow the school's normal procedures. If a student is unwell or distressed, follow the usual process for the class.

## Part A: Starter quiz (10 minutes) 
**Books closed. Work alone, in silence. Answer from memory. If you don't know, have a guess and move on. This is for you, not for marks.**

1. Which organ do you chew food in?
2. What is the name of the tube that carries food from your mouth down to your stomach?
3. In which organ is food churned around and mixed in a stretchy, muscular bag?
4. Which organ is the longest part of the digestive system, where digested food gets absorbed into the blood?
5. In which organ is water absorbed from the food that is left over?
6. What is the name of the opening where the waste leaves the body?
7. **True or false?** Food goes inside the liver as it travels through the digestive system.
8. Put these in the correct order, from first to last: **stomach, mouth, small intestine, oesophagus, large intestine.**
9. What do we call the jobs of the digestive system? Complete the sentence: *The digestive system breaks down large pieces of food into ______ pieces so they can pass into the blood.*
10. Why do we need to digest food at all? Give one reason.

**Rate your confidence for Part A:** Circle one. *I guessed most of them* / *I knew about half* / *I knew most of them*

## Check your answers (15-20 minutes)
Your cover teacher will read out the answers. Mark in a different colour pen. For any you got wrong, write the right answer next to it.

Score: ____ / 10

## Part B: The journey of a cheese sandwich (20 minutes)
**Now you can use your textbook.**

Imagine you have just eaten a cheese sandwich. Write about its journey through your body, from your mouth to the end of the digestive system. Use the frame below. Write at least **one full sentence** for each stage. Use words from the word bank.

**Word bank:** *mouth, teeth, chew, swallow, oesophagus, muscles, stomach, churn, small intestine, absorbed, blood, large intestine, water, waste*

| Stage | Where is the sandwich now? | What happens here? (Use the frame) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The mouth | In my mouth, my teeth ... so that ... |
| 2 | The oesophagus | The muscles in the oesophagus ... |
| 3 | The stomach | In the stomach, the food is ... |
| 4 | The small intestine | In the small intestine, the digested food ... and goes into the ... |
| 5 | The large intestine | In the large intestine, ... is absorbed. The food that is left is ... |
| 6 | Leaving the body | The waste leaves the body through ... |

**Sentence starters you can use if you need them:** *First, ... / Next, ... / This is important because ... / After that, ... / Finally, ...*

## Part C: Challenge (if you finish early)
1. **Explain** why the small intestine is so important for getting energy from your food. Use the word *because*.
2. **Think:** A person is told they have to have part of their stomach removed. Which of the jobs of the stomach (from your notes or the textbook) might be harder for their body? Give **two** ideas.
3. **Make a mnemonic** (a funny sentence) to help someone remember the correct order of the organs in Question 8.

## Part D: Exit question (last 5 minutes)
In **two or three sentences**, answer this question: *What is the most important thing about the digestive system that you remembered or learned today?*

# PART 3: ANSWERS AND NEXT STEPS

## Part A answers: read these out at 15 minutes

| Q | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1 | The mouth |
| 2 | The oesophagus |
| 3 | The stomach |
| 4 | The small intestine |
| 5 | The large intestine |
| 6 | The anus |
| 7 | **False.** Food does not go inside the liver. (The liver is an organ that helps digestion, but the food doesn't pass through it.) |
| 8 | Mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine |
| 9 | **Smaller** (accept "small" or "tiny") |
| 10 | Any one of: so that our body can absorb the food into the blood; because the pieces of food are too big to get into the blood; to get energy and the building materials the body needs (to grow and repair). |

**Marking tip for the cover teacher:** Accept any sensible alternative wording. For Q4, you can accept "small intestine" only. For Q3, "stomach" only. If a student gets Q7 wrong, say: "No, the food passes through a long tube, and the liver sits next to it."

## Part B: what a good answer looks like (for the cover teacher to check against)

You are looking for six sentences in the right order that make sense. **Here's a model:**

1. **Mouth:** In my mouth, my teeth chew the sandwich into smaller pieces so that it's easier to swallow.
2. **Oesophagus:** The muscles in the oesophagus squeeze and push the food down to my stomach.
3. **Stomach:** In the stomach, the food is churned around and mixed up until it becomes a thick liquid.
4. **Small intestine:** In the small intestine, the digested food is absorbed and goes into the blood.
5. **Large intestine:** In the large intestine, water is absorbed. The food that is left is waste.
6. **Leaving the body:** The waste leaves the body through the anus.

**What counts as a good response (a quick checklist for you):**
- Are all six stages in the right order? 
- Is there at least one sentence for each stage? 
- Have they used some of the words from the word bank? 

Don't worry about the detail. If they have the organs in the right order and say roughly what each does, that's a good piece of work.

**A note for you if students ask "what happens to the cheese or bread?":** the answer is that it gets broken down into smaller and smaller pieces as it goes through. They don't need to know how at this stage.

## Part C: answers (for reference)

1. **Because** the small intestine is where digested food is absorbed into the blood, and the blood carries it to all parts of the body, so the body gets energy and the materials it needs to grow and repair. 
2. Any two sensible ideas, for example: food would not be churned and mixed as well; food might move on too fast or too slowly; the person might need to eat smaller, more frequent meals; the stomach holds less food. 
3. Any sensible mnemonic, as long as the order is mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine (for example, "My Old Spaniel Sleeps Lazily").

## Part D
No right or wrong answer. Check that each student has written something and that the sheet has a name on it.
