# Year 9 Maths: Solving equations with the unknown on both sides

**Assumptions:** one lesson's worth of practice (about 40 minutes), no calculators needed, students have not met brackets in equations yet, and negative solutions are fair game. Student sheet first, then the teacher key.

## STUDENT SHEET

**Name: ______________________   Date: ____________**

**Remember:** an equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, you do to the other. Your goal is to get all the x's on one side and all the plain numbers on the other.

**Example:** 5x + 3 = 2x + 15
Take away 2x from both sides: 3x + 3 = 15
Take away 3 from both sides: 3x = 12
Divide both sides by 3: x = 4
Check: 5 × 4 + 3 = 23 and 2 × 4 + 15 = 23. ✓

### Part B: Reasoning (Questions 13 to 16)

**13. Spot the mistake.** Sam solves 5x + 3 = 2x + 15 like this:

5x − 2x = 15 + 3
3x = 18
x = 6

(a) Find Sam's mistake.
(b) Show how you can tell the answer is wrong without solving the equation again.
(c) Write the correct solution.

**14. Always, sometimes or never true?** "When you solve an equation with x on both sides, the answer is positive." Give examples to back up your answer.

**15. Odd one out.** Which of these equations is the odd one out? Explain why.
A: 3x + 4 = x + 10
B: 5x − 2 = 2x + 7
C: 4x + 1 = 2x + 9
D: 6x = 3x + 9

**16. Make your own.** Write an equation with x on both sides that has the solution x = 5. Then write another with the solution x = −2. Swap with a partner and check each other's.

### Part C: Problem solving (Questions 17 to 19)

**17. Gym membership.** Gym A charges £3 to join, then £5 for each visit. Gym B charges £15 to join, then £2 for each visit.
(a) Write an expression for the cost of x visits at each gym.
(b) After how many visits do both gyms cost the same?
(c) What is the cost at that point?
(d) Which gym is cheaper if you visit 10 times? Show why.

**18. Triangle.** An isosceles triangle has two equal sides measuring (5x − 4) cm and (3x + 6) cm. The third side is 8 cm.
(a) Form an equation and solve it to find x.
(b) Work out the perimeter of the triangle.

**19. Parallel lines.** Two alternate angles on parallel lines are (6x − 20)° and (2x + 40)°.
(a) Find x.
(b) Work out the size of each angle.
(c) What is the size of the co-interior angle next to one of them? Give a reason.

### Challenge (Question 20)

The equation ax + 3 = 2x + 15 has the solution x = 4.
(a) Find the value of a.
(b) Find all the whole-number values of a (other than a = 2) so that the equation has a **positive whole-number** solution. What do you notice about a = 2?

## TEACHER KEY

### Part A: Fluency with variation

| Q | Equation | Solution | What it varies / the misconception it targets |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5x + 3 = 2x + 15 | x = 4 | Worked-example anchor. |
| 2 | 5x + 3 = 2x + 18 | x = 5 | Constant on the right goes up by 3, answer goes up by 1. Students see that the x-difference (3x) controls the effect. |
| 3 | 5x + 3 = 2x + 21 | x = 6 | Same pattern: every +3 on the right adds 1 to x. |
| 4 | 5x + 3 = 3x + 21 | x = 9 | Change a coefficient: now the difference is 2x, so the same gap gives a bigger answer (18 ÷ 2). |
| 5 | 2x + 15 = 5x + 3 | x = 4 | Mirror image of Q1. Targets "x must be on the left", and the idea that subtracting the larger x-term leaves a negative coefficient, which is fine but harder. Same answer as Q1. |
| 6 | 2x + 9 = 5x + 3 | x = 2 | Unknown larger on the right. Encourages subtracting the smaller x-term (2x). |
| 7 | 4x + 1 = 6x − 7 | x = 4 | Subtraction on the right; 8 = 2x. Targets sign errors when moving −7. |
| 8 | 8x − 5 = 3x + 20 | x = 5 | Negative constant on the left; 5x = 25. |
| 9 | 3x + 10 = 7x − 2 | x = 3 | Larger x-term on the right and negative constant: 12 = 4x. |
| 10 | 6x − 3 = 2x − 15 | x = −3 | Negative solution. Targets "the answer must be positive". |
| 11 | 10 − x = 3x + 2 | x = 2 | Negative x-term on the left: add x to both sides, 10 = 4x + 2, then 8 = 4x. |
| 12 | 5 − 2x = x − 7 | x = 4 | As Q11, with −2x. Add 2x: 5 = 3x − 7, 12 = 3x. |

**"What do you notice?" points to draw out**
- Q1 to 3: each +3 on the right-hand side adds 1 to the solution, because you're dividing by 3. Ask students to predict Q3 before they solve it.
- Q4: halving the x-difference (3x to 2x) means a bigger solution for the same gap.
- Q5 vs Q1: swapping the sides doesn't change the solution. Both approaches work, so students can choose to keep the x-coefficient positive by moving the smaller x-term.
- Q10: the solution can be negative. Subtract the smaller x-term and check by substitution.
- Common errors across Part A: subtracting the x-term from one side only; moving a constant and forgetting to change its sign (5x + 3 = 2x + 15 turned into 3x = 18); mixing the sides when the larger x-term is on the right; dividing only one term.

### Part B: Reasoning

**13.** (a) Sam added 3 to the right instead of subtracting it from both sides: 15 + 3 should be 15 − 3. (b) Substitute x = 6: left = 33, right = 27. They aren't equal. (c) 3x = 12, x = 4. Target: sign errors when moving constants.

**14.** Sometimes true. Q10 has x = −3, Q1 has x = 4. Accept any valid pair of examples. A strong answer says "it depends on the numbers". Target: assuming solutions are always positive.

**15.** C is the odd one out: its solution is x = 4; the others all give x = 3 (A: 2x = 6; B: 3x = 9; D: 3x = 9). Accept D, since it has no constant on the left, if a good reason is given. Target: checking by solving, and noticing structure.

**16.** Many answers. Check by substituting x = 5 and x = −2 on both sides. Example: 3x + 4 = x + 14 gives x = 5; 4x + 5 = 6x + 9 gives x = −2. Target: understanding equations as balance.

### Part C: Problem solving

**17.** (a) Gym A: 3 + 5x; Gym B: 15 + 2x. (b) 3 + 5x = 15 + 2x, so 3x = 12, x = 4 visits. (c) £23 at both. (d) At 10 visits: A = £53, B = £35, so B is cheaper. (Same structure as Q1: students should notice.) Targets: forming an equation from context and interpreting the solution.

**18.** (a) 5x − 4 = 3x + 6, so 2x = 10, x = 5. (b) Equal sides are 21 cm each, so perimeter = 21 + 21 + 8 = 50 cm. Targets: using an equation in geometry, substituting back to find lengths.

**19.** (a) 6x − 20 = 2x + 40, so 4x = 60, x = 15. (b) Both angles are 70°. (c) The co-interior angle is 180° − 70° = 110°; co-interior angles on parallel lines add up to 180°. Interleaves angle facts.

### Challenge

**20.** (a) 4a + 3 = 8 + 15, so 4a + 3 = 23, a = 5. (b) (a − 2)x = 12, so x = 12 ÷ (a − 2). For a positive whole-number solution, a − 2 must be a positive factor of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 12. So a = 3, 4, 5, 6, 8 or 14. For a = 2, the x terms cancel: 3 = 15 is never true, so there is no solution. Students who go further may note that a < 2 gives negative solutions.

### Suggested use
- Part A in pairs, with mini-whiteboards for the predictions before each group.
- Stop after Q4 and Q7 for a short discussion before moving on.
- Use Q13 as a hinge: if more than a third of the class can't spot Sam's error, reteach moving terms across the equals sign before continuing.
- Parts B and C can be homework or a second lesson. The challenge is for those who finish early.
