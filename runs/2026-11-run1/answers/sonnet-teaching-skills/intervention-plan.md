# Intervention plan: English Language Paper 1, Question 4 (evaluation)

## Assumptions

- Exam board is **AQA GCSE English Language (8700)**. Paper 1 Question 4 is **20 marks** (AO4: evaluate texts critically and support with appropriate textual references). Pupils respond to a statement about the source ("to what extent do you agree?") and should spend about 20 minutes on it.
- The six students were chosen because their mock or practice marks for **Q4 specifically** are below target. I haven't seen their scripts, so the gap below is the most common one at this level. **Before Session 1, read their last Q4 answers and confirm the gap.** If one or two are losing marks for a different reason (for example, running out of time), they get a different focus in Session 5 (see below).
- Students are referred to as S1 to S6 so no names appear in the plan.
- 6 sessions, 30 minutes, once a week after school. Pupils have the source extract and a pen. No computers needed.

## 1. The group and the gap

**Who:** Six Year 11 students below target on Q4. Group them by the gap, not the grade.

**The gap (one skill):** Pupils retell or describe what happens in the extract instead of **evaluating** it. They typically
- agree or disagree in a vague way ("I agree because the writer uses good words");
- choose quotations that are too long or from one part of the text only;
- name a technique without explaining its effect on the reader;
- don't commit to a judgement ("it's a bit of both") with reasons.

**What they need:** A clear **judgement** on the statement, backed by **short, well-chosen quotations** from across the section, with **analysis of the writer's methods and their effect**, and an overall **evaluation** (how successfully? to what extent?).

**Target level:** The top two levels of the mark scheme: Level 3 (clear, relevant evaluation, 11 to 15 marks) moving to Level 4 (perceptive, detailed evaluation, 16 to 20 marks).

## 2. The measure (set first)

| Check | When | What |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-check** | Session 1 | A full Q4 on one unseen extract and statement, 20 minutes, no help |
| **Post-check** | Session 6 | A full Q4 on a different but comparable extract and statement, 20 minutes, no help |
| **Marking** | Both | Same mark scheme, marked by you with a second marker (a colleague) on at least two scripts to check consistency |

Compare marks and level. Also compare the three things above: **judgement** (clear? committed?), **evidence** (short? spread across the extract?), **evaluation of effect** (explains the writer's choice and the effect on the reader?). Tick each on a one-page tracker for S1 to S6.

## 3. Session plan (30 minutes each)

Every session after Session 1 follows the same shape, so pupils know what to expect:

| Time | Part |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 | **Retrieval:** three questions from earlier sessions |
| 5 to 13 | **Explicit teaching:** you model, thinking aloud |
| 13 to 20 | **Worked example, then fade:** pupils finish more of each example |
| 20 to 27 | **Practice:** a short piece of writing (one paragraph) |
| 27 to 30 | **Exit check:** one question, marked quickly, and a one-line target for next week |

### Session 1: Baseline and what Q4 rewards (30 min)
- 0 to 22: **Pre-check** under timed conditions (20 minutes plus 2 minutes to read).
- 22 to 30: Show a Level 4 and a Level 2 paragraph side by side, anonymised. Pupils spot the difference: "What does the Level 4 writer do that the Level 2 writer doesn't?" Create a class list on the board (a judgement, short quotes, effects, a verdict).
- Homework (10 minutes): read the extract again and underline three short quotations you could use.

### Session 2: Take a stance on the statement
- **Retrieval:** What does Q4 ask you to do? What does AO4 reward?
- **Teach:** Unpick the statement. "To what extent" means you must say how far you agree. Model turning a statement into a **judgement** ("The writer is largely successful in showing X, although Y is less convincing").
- **Worked example:** Three sample judgements: weak ("I agree"), better ("I mostly agree"), best (committed with a reason). Pupils rank them.
- **Practice:** Write a two-sentence opening judgement on a new statement.
- **Exit check:** Underline the judgement in a sample answer.

### Session 3: Choose evidence that works
- **Retrieval:** Three words that start an evaluative judgement ("effectively", "convincingly", "less successfully").
- **Teach:** Short, precise quotations (three to six words) from across the section (beginning, middle, end), not all from one place. Model choosing a quotation and cutting it down.
- **Worked example:** Pupils are given a long quotation and trim it. Then choose three from a set of 8 for a given judgement.
- **Practice:** Choose three quotations from a new extract that support the judgement they wrote in Session 2, from different parts of the text.
- **Exit check:** Which of these two quotations is better, and why?

### Session 4: Evaluate the writer's methods and their effect
- **Retrieval:** Quotations from Session 3; terms for methods (contrast, shift in focus, simile, verb choice).
- **Teach:** The chain: **quote, method, effect on the reader, link back to the statement**. Model a full paragraph, thinking aloud: "I'm choosing this word because..."
- **Worked example (fading):** Paragraph 1 fully modelled. Paragraph 2, pupils complete the effect. Paragraph 3, pupils complete the effect and the link.
- **Practice:** Write one full paragraph with the whole chain, using their own quotation.
- **Exit check:** Add the missing "effect" and "link" to a sentence.

### Session 5: Build the whole answer under time
- **Retrieval:** The four parts of a paragraph and the opening judgement.
- **Teach:** The 3-minute plan: judgement, three quotations spread across the extract, one effect each; a closing verdict. Model the plan, then write the answer in 15 minutes.
- **Worked example:** A planned answer shown with a timing guide (3 plan, 14 write, 3 check).
- **Practice:** Plan (3 min), then write an opening and one paragraph (about 10 minutes).
- **Exit check:** Check your own plan against the four parts.
- **Variation for individuals:** Any pupil whose pre-check showed a **timing** problem (didn't finish) works on writing three paragraphs in 14 minutes, using shorter quotations and a tighter chain.

### Session 6: Post-check and reflection
- 0 to 22: **Post-check**, timed, on a new extract.
- 22 to 30: Pupils compare their pre-check and post-check, using the tracker: "What have you improved? What is your next target?" Give each their marks, their level and one target.

## 4. Materials outline

| Session | Materials |
|---|---|
| 1 | Pre-check extract and statement, mark scheme, anonymised Level 2 and Level 4 samples |
| 2 | Three sample judgements, new statement |
| 3 | Extract with annotated quotations, quote-trimming sheet |
| 4 | Model paragraph (fully done), partly done, nearly blank; method and effect word bank |
| 5 | Planning template, timing guide, new extract |
| 6 | Post-check extract and statement (different from the pre-check), tracker |
| Throughout | One-page tracker for S1 to S6 (judgement, evidence, evaluation of effect); sentence stems |

**Sentence stems on a card:** "The writer successfully shows... when..." "This word suggests... which makes the reader feel..." "However, this is less convincing because..." "Overall, I agree to a large extent because..."

## 5. Timing

- After school, once a week, same day and room, which avoids pupils missing the same lesson each week.
- Check against other after-school commitments (clubs, prayer, other interventions, part-time jobs), and confirm with each pupil and their parent. Agree a catch-up slot for absences, so that nobody misses a session.
- Run Sessions 1 to 6 in consecutive weeks. If a week is lost, shift the post-check; don't squeeze sessions.

## 6. Exit criteria

A pupil **exits** when **both** are true:
1. The post-check reaches their **target level** (or is within 2 marks, with a clear next step), and
2. They have a **committed judgement, short quotations from across the extract and an effect** in at least two of the last three practices (Sessions 4, 5, 6).

Pupils who don't reach this **continue** for a further 3 sessions with a narrower focus (for example, effect only), or move to a different group if the post-check shows a different gap (for example, AO2 on Q3).

## 7. Communication

**Message to students (read out or on paper):**
"You've been picked for a short after-school group on Question 4, because it's the question where you can still gain the most marks. It's six weeks, 30 minutes, once a week. By the end you'll know how to take a clear position, choose the best evidence, and explain the writer's effect. We'll measure it at the start and the end so you can see your progress."

**Message to parents (short):**
"Dear parent/carer, [Student] has been invited to a six-week after-school group on Question 4 of English Language Paper 1, which is where we can help most. Sessions are 30 minutes, once a week on [day], starting [date]. You don't need to do anything except help them to attend. We'll share their progress at the end."

## 8. Summary for a line manager

Six Year 11 students who are below target on Paper 1 Question 4 will attend six 30-minute after-school sessions. The group is defined by one gap, moving from retelling to evaluating, taught through judgement, short quotations spread across the extract, and analysis of methods and effect. Progress is measured by a timed Q4 under the same conditions at the start and end, marked against the same mark scheme, and exit depends on reaching target level and showing the three skills across practice. The only cost is your time and photocopying, and the post-check results will tell us whether to continue, widen the group or change the approach.
