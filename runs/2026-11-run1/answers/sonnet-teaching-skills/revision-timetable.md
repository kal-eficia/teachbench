# Year 11 mock revision timetable: 3 weeks to go

**Assumptions:** today is the start of Week 1 (Monday). Mocks run Monday to Thursday of Week 4: Maths Paper 1 (Mon), English Language (Tue), Biology (Wed), History (Thu). The student gets home around 3:45 and needs a snack and a break first. Prayer times and the evening meal are flexible, so block times can be moved by 30 to 60 minutes to fit around them. Friday is a lighter day. Topic names for Biology and History are placeholders for the mock content the teacher gives: swap in the real names.

**Plan in one line:** short 25-minute blocks, 5-minute break, about two blocks on a school day, and a lot of self-testing rather than reading.

**Weighting:** Maths gets the most blocks, then History, then Biology and English Language roughly equal. Maths and History get the spaced repeats because they are the weakest.

**Fixed commitments in:** football Tuesday and Thursday 5 to 6:30, mosque class Saturday 10 to 12. Nothing is booked on top of them.

## How to do one 25-minute block (read this first)

If you've never revised before, this is the whole secret. Don't just read your notes. That feels like revising but doesn't work.

1. **Phone in another room.** Set a timer for 25 minutes.
2. **First 3 minutes:** look at the topic once (notes, textbook or a short video).
3. **Next 15 minutes:** close everything and **test yourself**. Write down everything you remember about the topic (a "blurt"), or do questions, or use flashcards.
4. **Last 7 minutes:** open your notes, mark what you missed in a different colour, and add 3 new flashcards for the missed bits.
5. **5-minute break** away from screens: water, a walk, stretch.

**Method names used in the timetable**
- **Blurt:** write everything you remember on blank paper, then check.
- **Cards:** make flashcards (question on the front, answer on the back), then test yourself and sort into "know" and "not yet".
- **Questions:** do exam-style questions. Mark them straight away and write down what you got wrong.
- **Wrong-answer log:** a page in a notebook where you write every mistake and the correct way.
- **Teach it:** explain the topic out loud to a wall, a sibling or a parent.

**For history dates:** don't try to learn all the dates in one go. Make 10 date cards per topic (date on one side, event on the other, and a second set with the event on the front). Draw a timeline and say the story out loud: "First this happened, which caused this, which led to this." Test the cards every day for 5 minutes. Little and often beats one big session.

## Why the plan looks like this

- **Blocks are 25 minutes with a break** because that matches how long you can concentrate. Do not stretch them. If you can do one more, do a second block later, not a longer one.
- **Topics come back after 2 to 3 days, then a week.** That repeat is what makes things stick. It is why you see Maths algebra on Monday, Thursday and Saturday.
- **Maths and History appear most** because they are the weakest. History dates are practised in small, regular chunks.
- **Football evenings are easy** (flashcards, not new topics) because you'll be tired.
- **Buffer blocks and rest are deliberate.** About one block in seven is left spare, and Friday evening is free. If you miss a day, don't try to catch it all up: use a spare block and carry on.
- **Prayer times and meals are not in the table.** Move blocks around them.

## Weekly check-in (5 minutes, every Sunday)

1. How many blocks did I finish out of 14?
2. Rate confidence 1 to 5 for: Maths algebra, Maths ratio, English reading, English writing, Biology, History dates.
3. Which topic keeps going wrong? Put it in an early block next week.
4. What one thing will I change?

If you finish fewer than 8 blocks in a week, cut the plan down rather than quitting: do the Maths and History blocks first.
