## Ranking by marks you can realistically win back

Ranking by lowest average alone would put Q17 first. Marks lost times how teachable the topic is gives a slightly different order:

1. **Ratio sharing (Q9, 58 lost).** Core foundation content with a repeatable method. Almost any student can move from zero to 2 or 3 marks. Highest return per lesson.
2. **Pythagoras (Q21, 60 lost).** A fixed procedure (square, add or subtract, root) that responds well to teaching. Needs the "which side is the hypotenuse?" decision practised.
3. **Probability tree (Q17, 70 lost).** The largest loss, but also the most multi-step. Teach it as a procedure: draw the branches, write probabilities on each, multiply along. Many of these marks are method marks, so even partial answers earn something.
4. **Compound interest (Q19, 66 lost).** Teach percentage multipliers first (the same skill underpins many other questions), then repeat multiplication. High marks lost but the most to teach, so expect smaller gains per lesson.
5. **Area of compound shape (Q7, 41 lost).** Cuts lost across many students: split, calculate, add.
6. **Solving equations (Q12, 30 lost).** Keep alive in starters. It is a prerequisite for other questions on the other papers, which makes it worth more than this one paper shows.
7. **Pie chart (Q14) and fractions of amounts (Q3).** Low losses. Cover these in starters only.

## Suggested 10 lessons

| Lessons | Focus | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Ratio sharing (including "given one share" and total) | Highest return, builds division and fraction fluency |
| 3-4 | Pythagoras (finding the hypotenuse, then a shorter side, then in context) | Procedure responds fast to teaching |
| 5 | Percentage multipliers | Prerequisite for compound interest |
| 6 | Compound interest | Builds on lesson 5 |
| 7-8 | Probability trees | Needs fraction and decimal multiplication, so teach after the percentage work |
| 9 | Compound shapes plus equations (mixed practice) | Consolidates two mid-loss topics |
| 10 | Exam-style timed section, then feedback on method marks | Rehearses the final-paper routine |

Use a 10-minute starter in every lesson for retrieval of earlier topics (fractions of amounts, pie charts, equations), so those marks don't slip while you teach the rest.

## Check before you commit
- **Blanks versus wrong answers.** If a large share of the marks lost on Q17, Q19 and Q21 are zeros because students ran out of time or skipped them, add short routines (attempt every part; write down the method even when stuck) and timed practice. Teaching more content would be the wrong fix.
- **Method marks.** On these 3-mark questions, check scripts for students scoring 1 of 3. Those students know the start of the method and need the last step, which is a cheaper win than starting from zero.
- **Other papers.** This is one paper. If Q12 (equations) or fractions also appear weak on the other papers, give them more than starter time.
- **Groups.** Sort scripts into three profiles: (a) lost marks on the late, hard questions only (target Q17, Q19), (b) lost marks across ratio, Pythagoras and areas (a core knowledge group, which needs the first 4 lessons most), (c) lost marks through blanks (a technique group). Run a short extra session for group (b) if you can.

## Expected return
If the class wins back about a third of the marks lost on the top four questions, that is roughly 85 marks across 28 students, around 3 marks per student on this paper alone. Good gain for 10 lessons, and it is higher if the blanks check above shows many students simply didn't attempt the later questions.
