# Reception phonics: introducing the digraph 'sh' (20 minutes, 26 children)

**Assumptions:** whole-class carpet session, a standard synthetic phonics approach (revisit, teach, practise, apply), mid-autumn term. Children can blend simple CVC words from the letters they know. You have a flipchart or screen, a set of grapheme cards, mini whiteboards and pens (or sand trays) for the writing part. Tricky words are not part of this session.

**Learning aim:** children say /sh/ when they see the letters s and h together, read simple words containing sh, and write sh.

**Why this works with what they know:** they already read 'ck' (two letters, one sound), so "digraph" is a familiar idea. You can say "just like ck, these two letters work together to make one sound".

## Timeline

| Min | Part | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 0-4 | **Revisit** | Quick flash of grapheme cards, fast pace: s a t p i n m d g o c k e u r h b f l, then ck. Children say the sound together, then you pick a few for individuals. Keep it snappy. Then quick blend of 2 words with ck (e.g. "sock", "duck") to warm up the digraph idea. |
| 4-9 | **Teach 'sh'** | Hold up **s** then **h**. "Two letters, but they make ONE sound, a quiet sound." Model the sound: finger to lips, /shshsh/ (the "quiet please" sound). Children copy 3 times. Show the **sh** grapheme card. Place a line (sound button) under it to show it's one sound. Then say "I spy" with objects or pictures: ship, shell, shop, fish, shed. Children listen for /sh/ at the start and the end. Make sure /sh/ is said as a continuous sound, not "sss-huh". |
| 9-14 | **Practise: read** | Write words on the board and read them together with sound-talk: **sh-o-p, sh-e-d, sh-i-n, sh-i-p, sh-u-t, f-i-sh, d-i-sh, r-u-sh**. For each: children say the sounds, then blend ("shop"). Model the first two, then children do the rest. Mix the position of sh so they don't assume it only comes first. Ask a few children to point to the digraph. |
| 14-18 | **Practise: write** | Show how to form **s** then **h** (say a rhyme or verbal path for each letter). Children air-write on their palms, then write **sh** on mini whiteboards. Then dictate **shed** and **fish**. Circulate and look for backwards letters and sh written with the letters far apart. |
| 18-20 | **Apply** | Read the caption together: **a ship** (point to a picture), then **a fish in a dish**. Children read aloud to a partner. Finish with a quick review: "What sound do s and h make together?" |

## Words and sentences (all decodable from taught letters plus sh)
shop, shed, shin, ship, shut, shot, shock (sh-o-ck), fish, dish, rush, mash, cash, rash
Captions: *a ship*, *a fish in a dish*, *the shed* (avoid "the" in this session if you haven't taught it as a tricky word)

## Assessment: what to look for
- Say /sh/ on seeing the grapheme without prompting.
- Blend a sh word without reading s and h separately.
- Write s then h with correct formation.
Make a quick note of children who confuse sh with s or h on its own, and of children who can already read all the words. Plan a short follow-up for the first group, a pair of extension words for the second.

## Misconceptions and fixes
- **Reading sh as "s-h" (two sounds).** Return to the sound button and say "two letters, one sound". Say it once as a whole.
- **Making a long "shuh" sound.** Keep the sound pure: lips pushed out, quiet air.
- **Confusing sh and ch next time.** Don't introduce ch yet. Next time contrast the two clearly.

## Support and stretch
- **Support:** small group with grapheme cards on the carpet; pair with a confident partner for the blending; spend the extra time on the sound and the matching.
- **Stretch:** children who are ready write a word with sh in it (shop, fish) on their own, or read a word with two digraphs such as "shock".

## Follow-up (next day)
Start with a flash of **sh** within the revisit, then spot it in a short book or on the classroom labels. Keep sh in the daily review for a week.
